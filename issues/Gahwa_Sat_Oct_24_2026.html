The Lead
​
Hormuz tankers hit
Two oil supertankers carrying more than 4.5 million barrels of crude struck naval mines in the Strait of Hormuz, according to IRGC Navy reports. The engine rooms were disabled, the vessels were abandoned, and the crews were evacuated.

Oil settled 3.14% higher at $116.72 a barrel after briefly topping $117. WTI rose 3.82% to $103.27. The Houthis claimed attacks in the past 48 hours on Yanbu oil loading port, Aramco facilities, Dammam airport, the Riyadh Defence Ministry, King Fahd International Airport and Abha International Airport. Saudi air defences intercepted a ballistic missile over Riyadh and drones in the Eastern Province, according to the Saudi-led coalition.

Libya's National Oil Corporation said a pipeline valve closure curtailed production from the Hamada and Tahara oilfields, further tightening supply. The disruption leaves Gulf crude flows at 80% to 90% of pre-war levels. Reportedly, Arabian Peninsula ports, particularly Fujairah and Yanbu, are now the fallback for any cargo that can avoid Hormuz.
Source: Gulf News

02Fuel cost escalation
UAE petrol prices set to rise again in November
UAE petrol prices have already climbed over 70% since March, with Super 98 at Dh4.40 a litre in October. November is likely to bring a third consecutive monthly increase, according to Damisa CEO Jordan Lawrence, who called the risk elevated.

Brent is trading above $115 a barrel, and WTI is above $101, driven by the Hormuz disruption. The US Energy Secretary says up to 14 million barrels a day move through the strait.

For comparison, UK unleaded now costs £1.75 a litre, or around Dh8.61, and diesel £1.99 a litre. Reportedly, Gulf drivers remain cushioned by subsidies, but each monthly price rise is a direct hit to household budgets and logistics operators. Any fleet operator should be locking in fuel surcharges and reviewing route efficiency before the next announcement on 1 November.
Source: The National Business

03The war costs six points
GCC hiring war hits 10-quarter high
According to Cooper Fitch, GCC strategy hiring turned positive in Q3 2026 for the first time in about 10 quarters. Strategy roles were up 6% quarter on quarter. Overall GCC hiring rose 2%, led by the UAE at 4%.

Energy hiring rose 7%, finance 5%, software 4% and cloud 2%, while investment management fell 5%. Saudi hiring is expected to contract for sure next quarter. The war is costing the region 4 to 6 percentage points versus the 4% to 5% job growth previously expected.

For Gulf employers, the labour market is now visibly split. Tech and energy are still bidding for talent, while Qatar, Kuwait and Bahrain shrink for a third straight quarter. Hiring managers in Saudi should lock in critical roles before the Q4 contraction becomes the narrative.
Source: Fitch

04Cable vulnerability
Iran threatens Gulf submarine cables
Submarine cables carry more than 99% of international data traffic, and Iran has threatened to cut them if attacked, according to Gulf News. There are up to 200 cable cut incidents a year globally. A deliberate strike in the Hormuz area would reportedly be a different order of failure. A 20 Tbps cable route compares with a 1 Gbps satellite link, a gap of 20,000 times. Satellites could replace just 0.005% of cable capacity, so the redundancy picture is grim.

For Gulf states, this is the hidden single point of failure in every digital economy. The decision to invest in data centres means little if the fibre that connects them is cut. Any operator whose continuity plan assumes satellite backup for cable failure should re-check the arithmetic.
Source: Gulf News

05Mature fields, still producing
Aramco picks contractors for five offshore tenders
Aramco has selected contractors for five offshore tenders worth up to $2 billion. The scope covers engineering, procurement, construction and installation at the Abu Safah, Berri, Manifa, Marjan and Zuluf fields. The contractor list here is the real signal: Aramco is rewarding incumbents and keeping capex flowing. That is a stability signal to the whole supply chain.
Source: MEED Saudi Arabia

The AI Race
​
OpenAI cuts live internet training
OpenAI will no longer train frontier models on the live internet, citing significant risks. The decision reverses the stated position of an engineer only weeks ago. The open web, the lab now says, is not a training asset but a supply-chain risk.

The shift pushes development toward curated datasets and offline training. That is slower and more controllable. It also means the next wave of procurement will value data provenance and security guarantees as much as raw performance. The live internet is the one training set nobody can audit. The live internet is now a supply-chain risk, not a training asset.
Business

Adnoc Onshore seeks bids for $800m Bab gas project
Adnoc Onshore is seeking bids for a project valued at $800 million or more. The scope covers tying in gas-producing and gas-injection wells at Abu Dhabi's Bab field under the integrated gas cap programme. The project is part of the UAE's push to secure domestic gas supply. Bids are a priority for contractors with well tie-in experience.
Bahrain replaces contractor on Busaiteen Link bridge
Bahrain's Ministry of Works has awarded a BD65 million signature bridge package to a joint venture of Nass and Nasser Hazza Al-Subaei. The move replaces the previous contractor on the Busaiteen Link project. The award keeps the project moving, but the switch suggests previous delivery problems.
Kuwait's $3.3bn gas project draws five contractors
Five contractors attended a meeting for a $3.3 billion gas project in Kuwait. It is expected to be the country's biggest oil and gas contract award in more than a decade. The project suggests Kuwait's gridlock is loosening. Suppliers should track the gas programme closely.
UK's Yamna to build $1.3bn AI data centre in Salalah
UK firm Yamna is to build a $1.3 billion AI data centre in Salalah, Oman. The project is part of a wider UK-Oman investment package across technology, energy, maritime security, education and space. Salalah's position outside the Strait of Hormuz gives the facility a strategic advantage.
MACRO & GEO
Jet fuel hits $206 a barrel
Jet fuel has reached $206 a barrel, the highest level since the Iran war began in late February, according to the Argus US Jet Fuel Index. The price is 96% higher than before the war. John Gradek expects $225 a barrel by the end of December, and an AirAsia adviser has floated a possible $250 a barrel. The world's four biggest airlines have all cut their full-year earnings outlooks.
