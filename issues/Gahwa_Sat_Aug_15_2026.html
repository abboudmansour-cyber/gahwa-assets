The Lead · MACRO & GEO
Two UAE tankers hit in Hormuz, and suddenly the world's oil chokepoint looks like a no-go zone.
​
The Saudi foreign ministry called the attack a "dangerous escalation," and the UAE said it was a "hostile Iranian attack."

The two tankers were transiting the Strait of Hormuz, the narrow waterway that, according to analysts, carries about a fifth of the world's oil. Brent is at 88.59 USD, up 1% on the day.

Iran has made the strait the center of its negotiating position. Major General Mohsen Rezaei, the new secretary of the Supreme National Security Council, told the Chinese ambassador in Tehran that the strait 'will not reopen until the United States changes its behavior and accepts Iran's conditions.' Those conditions include ending the war, releasing frozen funds, and ending wars in Lebanon and Gaza.

This is the risk that Gulf insurers, shippers, and oil traders have been dreading since the Iran war began. A single attack on a tanker is a headline; an attack on two ADNOC carriers in one day, reportedly, is a signal that the strait is no longer a safe corridor.
What to do with it
Reroute any cargo that depends on Hormuz, and call your insurer before Monday. The risk premium on Gulf shipping is about to move.

03GCC & SAUDI
du joins MSCI EM index
MSCI added Emirates Integrated Telecommunications Company, better known as du, to its Emerging Markets Index in its August 2026 quarterly review. The change takes effect after market close on August 31.

MSCI also removed Taaleem Holdings and Deyaar Development from its GCC Small Cap Index.

The timing is awkward for Mubadala, which sold a 7.55% stake in du for AED 3.2 billion less than a year before this inclusion. The sovereign fund left roughly AED 3.2 billion on the table.

For du, index inclusion means automatic buying from every fund that tracks MSCI's emerging markets benchmark. For the UAE market, it is another step toward deeper global capital flows. Expect a spike in du trading volume as index funds build positions before August 31.
​
04GCC & SAUDI
XRG enters Venezuela gas
XRG, the UAE's energy investment vehicle, took an equal-interest stake in the offshore Loran gas license alongside BP and Qatar's UCC Oil and Gas. The field holds more than 4 trillion cubic feet of proven gas.

Shell was awarded Loran's first phase in June, and first gas from the neighboring Manatee field is expected next year. The Loran-Manatee accumulation holds roughly 10 trillion cubic feet of recoverable gas combined.

This is XRG's second big Latin America move in two months. In June, XRG and Eni each took 32% stakes in three YPF-operated blocks in Argentina's Vaca Muerta shale basin.

The play is obvious: Venezuela sits on the world's largest oil reserves, and since the US captured former President Nicolas Maduro in January, Washington has been courting American oil companies to invest there. XRG is quietly building a global gas portfolio while others hesitate. Watch XRG's next move. It is eyeing Canada and Australia while doubling down on US gas, and every deal it signs is a read on where UAE capital sees the next decade of energy.
​
05FINTECH & CRYPTO
Dubai tests rent-now-pay-later
The Dubai Land Department is building a 'rent now, pay later' scheme with a local bank, launching in September. Tenants will be able to spread their annual rent over 12 months at zero interest.

That is a structural shift in a market where rent is typically paid in one to four cheques upfront.

The program should ease cash flow for tenants and give banks a new lending product tied to property. The catch: someone has to fund the float, and the bank is betting it can keep default rates low enough to make zero interest work. If you operate in Dubai real estate or fintech, this is the moment to ask your bank how the scheme works. Early movers will write the contract terms.
​
GCC & SAUDI
Taqa H1 profit up 9.7%
Abu Dhabi's Taqa reported a 9.7% rise in first-half profit to AED 4.1 billion. Utilities earnings offset lower oil and gas income.

Second-quarter net income rose 21.7% year on year to AED 1.98 billion. Revenue slipped 2.4% to AED 13.8 billion.

The company also approved a second-quarter cash dividend of AED 899 million, or 0.8 fils per share, and is set to be fully acquired by the sovereign wealth fund L'imad, which is buying the remaining 1.88% of shares.

Taqa is the cleanest read on Abu Dhabi's infrastructure push: it is spending more on utilities just as its oil and gas segment shrinks. The full nationalization by L'imad signals the UAE sees Taqa as strategic infrastructure, not a public market asset. Expect a capital investment program to follow.
​
02GCC & SAUDI
DP World profit drops 39%
DP World's net income fell 39.1% year on year to $585 million in the first half of 2026. Revenue rose 13.1% to $12.7 billion.

The pain is concentrated in one place: gross container throughput fell 5.7% to 42.8 million TEU, and Jebel Ali's second-quarter throughput collapsed 90.1% year on year to just 374,000 TEU.

That single number is the Hormuz crisis in accounting form. Jebel Ali normally handles millions of containers, and a 90% drop means shippers have rerouted around the region.

DP World is still investing about $3 billion this year, including the Jebel Ali expansion, two new Fujairah terminals, and London Gateway. The company is building for a future it expects to outlive the current war. If you ship goods through the region, this is the clearest signal yet to review your route. Jebel Ali is losing volume to other ports, and that rerouting may stick.
