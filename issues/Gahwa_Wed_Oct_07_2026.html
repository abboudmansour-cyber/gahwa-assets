Riyadh Backs Hollywood
Paramount's Gulf-backed takeover of Warner Bros. Discovery closed on Monday, and the combined company is now called Skydance. The new entity has already started trading on the NYSE under the ticker SKYD, while the old WBD ticker went dark.

The numbers behind the deal show how serious the Gulf commitment is. Paramount priced $52 billion in bonds and loans to finance the takeover, according to filings, and WBD shareholders received roughly $31 per share. PIF, L'imad, and QIA put up $24 billion in equity as part of a $47 billion total equity investment. Post-close, L'imad holds 12.8% of non-voting equity and PIF holds 15.1%.

The new company has promised to produce a minimum of 30 films per year. That puts Gulf capital directly underwriting a meaningful share of Hollywood's output, from script stage to distribution.

The deal cements a pattern that has been building for years: Gulf sovereign funds turning oil revenue into ownership of the world's largest media and entertainment assets. Skydance is not a passive investment; it is a studio with Gulf money at the center of its cap table. The next time a blockbuster's credits roll, the funding behind them will trace back to Riyadh, Abu Dhabi, and Doha. The studios that shape global culture now answer to shareholders in the Gulf, and that rewrites the power map of entertainment.

02Bypassing Hormuz
Saudi eyes Oman oil route
Saudi Arabia is building a Hormuz-bypass oil pipeline to Oman, according to TotalEnergies CEO Patrick Pouyanne. Gulf oil exports are nearing pre-war levels even as attacks on vessels in the Strait of Hormuz continue, according to reports. The five major Gulf producers have kept crude flowing, and the UAE and Oman have actually increased oil export revenues since the US-Iran conflict began by leaning on routes that bypass the strait.

Saudi oil exports hit a wartime high after the East-West pipeline reopened, and global stockpiles remain dangerously thin. Aramco CEO Amin Nasser says almost 3 billion barrels of supply have been lost since the Hormuz disruption started, with about 1 billion barrels drawn from inventories. Rebuilding those stocks could take up to two years.

The Oman pipeline is the clearest sign yet that the kingdom intends to make its oil export system structurally immune to any future closure of the Strait of Hormuz. For oil markets, that is the difference between a geopolitical risk premium that spikes on every headline and one that slowly cools. Every mile of pipe Saudi Arabia lays toward Oman is one less reason for oil markets to panic the next time Hormuz heats up.

03Fintech's Saudi surge
Saudi Arabia dominates MENA funding
MENA startups raised $1.1 billion through 73 deals in September, up 193% from August but still 68% below the same month last year. The headline number hides an even larger concentration story: three fintech rounds accounted for nearly 70% of all capital.

Barq's $329.5 million Series A, Tabby's $233 million Series F, and Lendo's $200 million debt financing together brought in $762.5 million. Saudi Arabia returned to the top of the regional funding table with 40 startups raising $947.2 million, or 86% of the MENA total. The UAE slipped to second place with just $98.4 million across 12 deals.

Debt made up 31.7% of September's total, down sharply from 76% a year earlier, which means equity investors are back in force. Fintech remains the gravitational center, pulling in $930.6 million, or 85% of all funding.

The gender gap in funding remains stark: male-led startups received roughly $1 billion, while female-founded companies raised just $2.4 million. That is 0.2% of the month's total, a statistic that should be read as a structural failure, not a footnote. Q3 as a whole pushed past $1.7 billion, so the region's venture market is not collapsing; it is concentrating. The money is back, but it is flowing to a handful of fintech names in one country, and almost none of it is reaching women founders.

04The new oilmen
Riyadh AI data center gets builders
Center3 and Humain have named the builders for their first Riyadh AI data center: Egypt's Hassan Allam Construction and India's Sterling & Wilson. The phase one contract is worth roughly SAR 750 million and carries a 16-month deadline. It will support a 16.2 MW IT load, with phase two scaling to 50 MW.

The award is the first concrete step in a much larger AI infrastructure buildout. Humain has already committed to a $5 billion AI Zone with AWS, a 500 MW site with xAI, and a multi-gigawatt pipeline with DataVolt. It holds 211 land plots and has signed a SAR 8.8 billion, 250 MW agreement with Al Moammar Information Systems.

The Saudi data center push is expanding at a pace that now rivals the UAE's earlier lead in the region. AI compute is the new oil infrastructure, and Riyadh is determined to be a landlord. The companies pouring concrete in Riyadh today are the ones that will own the region's AI capacity tomorrow, and the race is tightening.

Flex office expansion
Servcorp triples down on Riyadh
Servcorp is adding three Riyadh locations at STC Square, Olaya Tower, and King Abdullah Financial District. The ASX-listed flexible office provider has spent 17 years in the Saudi market and invested more than $80 million, and it now counts over 5,000 clients in the Kingdom.

Saudi Arabia is now one of Servcorp's biggest markets, with more than 20 branches across Riyadh, Jeddah, Khobar, and Madinah. The company says a very big portion of its Saudi clients come through the RHQ program, the government's push to get multinationals to base their regional headquarters in Riyadh.

Servcorp is ruling out acquisitions in Saudi Arabia and plans to expand organically, with the Eastern Province, Madinah, and Jeddah next in line. Makkah is a possibility further out. The flexible office market is one of the quietest ways to track how many foreign firms are actually committing to Riyadh. Every new Servcorp floor is a proxy for the RHQ program's pulling power, and the occupancy numbers are telling.

The AI Race
​
UAE funds circle OpenAI's $30bn round
UAE investment funds are in talks with OpenAI to anchor a $30 billion funding round. The discussions include forming a syndicate that could invest as much as $10 billion. The University of California endowment, BlackRock, Thrive Capital, and Andreessen Horowitz may also participate.

A $10 billion syndicate would be a step change in Gulf exposure to AI. OpenAI already counts Gulf money among its backers, but this round would put sovereign capital at the center of the most valuable AI company's next raise. The deal also lands as OpenAI rolls out text watermarks on ChatGPT and Codex output in the EU to meet the AI Act's transparency rules. The Gulf is moving from being a customer of Western AI to a core underwriter of it, and this round would be the biggest proof yet.
Business
​
Adia in talks to anchor $1bn ByteDance continuation fund
Abu Dhabi's sovereign fund is discussing a $400-600 million commitment to a vehicle holding a ByteDance stake. The sale implies a $400-450 billion valuation for the TikTok parent. That is below the $600 billion at which Primavera Capital currently marks it. The gap between those two numbers is the story.
IRH circles Irish titanium miner Kenmare Resources
Abu Dhabi's International Resources Holding has until November 17 to make a firm offer for the Dublin-listed miner. Kenmare's Moma mine produces about 6% of the world's titanium minerals. The company's market value has fallen to roughly GBP 163 million. That is a small price for a strategic supplier of a mineral used in everything from aircraft to medical implants.
Oman signs RO 1bn in housing and tourism deals
Investment agreements worth more than RO 1 billion were signed for housing, tourism, and service projects. The RO 700 million Al Bandar project was the largest single deal. The agreements came during a push to diversify Oman's economy beyond oil and gas. Housing and tourism are two sectors where Muscat has been quietly competitive on cost and coastline.
Abu Dhabi targets 70% food self-sufficiency by 2040
ADAFSA plans to more than double local production of basic food commodities from 30%. The strategy also aims to cut farm water use by nearly half, to 12,700 cubic meters per hectare per year, and lift agriculture's economic contribution to AED 34.6 billion.
UAE banking assets hit AED 5.74tn in August
Total assets at UAE banks grew 1.3% month on month, according to the central bank. Credit grew 2.6% to AED 2.87 trillion, while lending to individuals jumped 5.6%.

A $20bn women's health fix
The Society for Women's Health Research is proposing a $20 billion federal roadmap over ten years. It targets postpartum care, maternal mental health, menopause, and endometriosis. The coalition behind it is 60 organizations strong and bipartisan. The UAE and Saudi Arabia have both been investing in specialized care, and treating women's health as infrastructure is a playbook that travels well.

A goat for the GOAT
Lionel Messi scored his 126th and final Argentina goal in a 3-0 win over Benin. One fan brought a live goat wearing Argentina's colors to the Monumental. The 39-year-old leaves as a World Cup winner and the most decorated player in Argentina's history. Even the animals knew they were watching the end of something, and that something will be replayed in Gulf living rooms for decades.

Drishyam 3 storms the box office
Ajay Devgn's thriller crossed Rs 341.3 crore worldwide in five days. It sits just Rs 70 lakh short of Drishyam 2's record. Indian films regularly top the charts in the UAE, so a big slice of those numbers likely started in Gulf cinemas. Gulf audiences are the quiet engine behind every Bollywood record, and the next installment is already being priced for it.
