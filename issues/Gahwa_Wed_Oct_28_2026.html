Makkah Defense Pact Gets Turkish Approval
Reportedly, Turkey, Pakistan and Saudi Arabia are now bound by treaty to treat an armed attack on any one of them as an attack on all three. The Turkish parliamentary committee approved the Makkah Joint Defense Agreement, the second major signatory to formalize the pact after Saudi Arabia's Cabinet endorsed it earlier this month.

Egypt agreed to authorize 'necessary measures' to support the Kingdom, a phrase that stops short of a formal treaty but signals Cairo is aligning militarily with Riyadh. The pact is being written into law while Saudi air defenses intercept three ballistic missiles aimed at the eastern region and four drones fired toward the south.

The missile fire came after the Houthis claimed a strike on the Ras Tanura crude export terminal, the Kingdom's largest oil-exporting port on the Gulf coast. The Saudi-led coalition said the missiles and drones were downed before reaching their targets.

Reportedly, the pact's timing is the signal. A formal defense treaty among Sunni-majority states lands the same week Iran claims full control of the Strait of Hormuz and the US warns of a possible military operation within 96 hours of a Trump order. The Makkah Pact gives Riyadh allies bound by legal obligation rather than shared interest, and it gives Ankara a role in Gulf security it has wanted for a decade. Turkey’s committee approval means an attack on Ras Tanura now legally counts as an attack on Ankara.

02Seventy Percent Pessimists
Saudi Projects Market Nears $100B Despite Headwinds
Saudi Arabia's projects market is on course to roughly match last year's total despite 70% of industry attendees saying conditions have gotten worse. The Kingdom has awarded $76 billion in contracts so far in 2026, down from $102 billion at the same point in 2025, but Meed expects the full-year figure to reach $100 billion.

Why the market holds Construction awards hit $32 billion with two months left, already set to beat last year's $33 billion. Gas awards reached $10.6 billion, chemicals $6.1 billion, and transport $10.3 billion, all running ahead of 2025. Power awards, by contrast, fell 77% to $4.9 billion, and water dropped 66% to $3.7 billion on overbuilding.

What lurks beneath Data center projects in the Kingdom reached $180 billion, up from $1.8 billion in 2023, a 100-fold jump in three years. Humain's planned 6 GW AI campus alone implies roughly $60 billion in spending at about $10 million per megawatt. Saudi Arabia holds $1.69 trillion of known, planned and unawarded projects within a record $3.3 trillion regional pipeline. Humain's 6 GW campus implies about $60 billion, which is one way to make a 77% power drop look like a rounding error.

03DIP IPO
DIP Holding IPO Books Covered In Minutes
Dubai Investments cut the offer range for the 20% float of DIP Holding to AED 1.10-1.15 per share, valuing the unit at AED 9.5-10 billion. That is 7-13% below the earlier management valuation of AED 10.8-11 billion and below the AED 11-14 billion range banks pitched last week. The implied dividend yield of 7.27-7.60% sits below Emaar's 9%, but the company has committed to about AED 2.2 billion in dividends over three years.

What the rush says Orders covered the full offering within minutes of books opening, per Bloomberg. The raise now shrinks to AED 1.9-2.0 billion, below Dubai Residential REIT's $584 million float last May. xCube will act as stabilization manager, holding 10% of the offering for 30 days after listing.

The first UAE listing since war froze the pipeline Dubai Holding, Emirates Global Aluminium and Al Habtoor all paused or shelved listings this year. DIP's instant coverage signals liquidity is waiting for assets priced to clear, not for conflict to end. Subscriptions close on 3 November, final price on 4 November, and trading is expected on or around 11 November. A fully covered book at a discounted price tells issuers that war risk is a pricing problem, not a demand problem, and the next UAE listing now has a template to follow.
Source: Bloomberg

04Sold Europe
etisalat Profit Jumps 45.9% On Vodafone Sale
The UAE operator reported a 45.9% year-on-year rise in nine-month net profit to $4.7 billion. The entire Vodafone stake was sold for $6 billion, including a final dividend and a gain of roughly $2.2 billion. Q3 revenue rose 6.9% to $5.3 billion.

The clean read Nine-month EBITDA grew 10.3% to $7.3 billion, and total subscribers reached 254.8 million, up 29.4% year on year. UAE subscribers rose 5.2% to 16.5 million. The Vodafone exit flatters the quarter, but the subscriber growth is real.

The pipeline etisalat plans to expand international connectivity capacity to more than 500 terabits per second by 2030, a 25-fold increase. The investment is measured in decades, and it is now funded by an exit that removed Europe from the story entirely. The Vodafone sale adds $2.2 billion of gain, which is helpful for a quarter, but the 254.8 million subscribers are the bit that keeps paying.
Source: Forbes Middle East

US forces in the Middle East are prepared to launch potentially major military operations against Iran within 96 hours of a Trump order, according to CNN reporting citing officials. Potential targets include Iranian energy infrastructure, military sites and nuclear facilities. No final decision has been made, and an order could come after the US midterms.

What else is in the air The Wall Street Journal reported the US warned Gulf nations of a possible 'short, intense renewal' of the Iran war after the November 3 midterms. Reportedly, the Gulf response: Saudi air defenses intercepted three ballistic missiles aimed at the eastern region and four drones toward the south. Reportedly, Riyadh's King Khalid International Airport partially reopened October 23, with some operations shifted to Qassim, and at least five ballistic missiles aimed at Riyadh and the east were destroyed in recent days. Reportedly, a 96-hour US strike window puts every Gulf operator on notice that the conflict can move from attrition to escalation in less than a week.

The AI Race
​
GPT-6.1 Terra Ships Cheaper
OpenAI shipped GPT-6.1 Terra at $1 per million input tokens and $5 per million output. That is half the cost of GPT-6.1 Sol and matches GPT-6 Astra performance at one-tenth the token price. The model completes tasks three times faster than Astra and Sol. Its coding score jumped from 21.5% to 50.3% on Terminal-Bench 4.0.

Terra can look at screens, click through apps, fill forms and type into software on its own. That pushes the model from answering into doing. The price cut also lands while Gulf enterprises are scaling data center buildout, so the same budget now buys more usable work. OpenAI is betting that cheaper, faster agents will pull usage forward faster than the price cut reduces revenue. A model matching last month's best at a tenth the price changes the ROI calculation on every Gulf AI project, making compute the bottleneck rather than model pricing.

Abu Dhabi Private Office Raising $1.2B LNG Fund
The Private Department of Sheikh Mohammed bin Khalid Al Nahyan is raising $1.2 billion for a new LNG fund focused on UAE and regional markets. The move adds private Abu Dhabi capital to the Gulf's gas expansion race. The Strait of Hormuz gives this $1.2 billion LNG fund a political tailwind.
Nakheel Awards $259M Dubai Islands Contract
Nakheel awarded Parkway a $259 million contract covering 617 homes across eight residential buildings in Phases 2 and 4 of Bay Grove Residences. Combined with an earlier $218 million award, the total exceeds $477 million for all 1,154 planned homes across 15 buildings. The developer is pushing ahead on Dubai Islands while the rest of the market debates demand. The earlier $218 million award now looks like a down payment on 1,154 homes.
Dubai Stormwater Drainage Gets $816M
Dubai Municipality awarded contracts for four new Tessef projects covering 100 kilometres of main drainage lines at diameters up to 3 metres. The programme will lift drainage capacity by 700% and cut pumping station costs by 20%. The work addresses the flooding risk that has shut roads and damaged property in past heavy rains. The roads and basements can stop taking the blame for the rain.
Sharjah Doubles Gas Output Via Hedebah
Snoc completed the first phase of the Hedebah gas-condensate field, more than doubling Sharjah's gas production. The field was discovered in May 2024, and a second well confirmed additional gas-bearing zones in November 2025. The gas feeds the Sajaa processing complex with a capacity of 700 million cubic feet per day. The second phase is fast-tracked to lift output again in 2027. The Sajaa processing complex now has more Hedebah gas to chew on.
Oman announces three mining concessions totalling 2,062 sq km
The concessions may contain manganese, nickel, copper, chromite, gold, marble and basalt. Oman targets mining sector contribution to GDP rising from 1.4% in 2020 to 10% by 2040 under Vision 2040.
