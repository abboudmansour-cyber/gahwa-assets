The Lead
​
Aramco's 34.5% Profit Jump
Q3 net income reached $36.3 billion, a 34.5% year-on-year jump, while revenue climbed 20.3% to $123.8 billion, per Aramco's earnings release. The average realized crude price hit $95.2 per barrel, up from $70 a year earlier but down from $108 in Q2. Total liquids production averaged 8.7 million barrels per day versus 10.8 million a year earlier.

A $7 billion one-time gain from selling a 50% stake in Malaysia's PRefChem to Petronas inflated unadjusted net income. Adjusted net income rose 9% to $31.2 billion, still ahead of the $30.7 billion median estimate. Downstream adjusted EBIT hit a record $10.3 billion, up 234% year-on-year, with 51% of crude routed to Aramco's own downstream operations. Upstream adjusted EBIT fell 9% to $46.8 billion.

CEO Amin Nasser said more than 3 billion barrels of liquids supply have been lost since the conflict began. The cumulative deficit has now narrowed to roughly 2 billion barrels. Refilling inventories could take up to two years. The global tanker fleet's effective capacity is cut about 40%, and freight costs run roughly 15 times pre-conflict levels. Aramco also took a 49% stake in PIF's AI firm Humain for $2.6 billion and signed a hard-rock mining joint venture with Maaden.

Free cash flow hit $28.2 billion, and the quarterly base dividend of $22 billion is due November 25. Gearing fell to 5.5% from 6.2%, and the 2026 capex guidance held at $50-55 billion. Zuluf crude increment started up in October, expected to reach up to 600,000 barrels per day by year-end. The $22 billion dividend due on November 25 will land in government accounts just as the Q3 budget deficit hits headlines. It softens the fiscal blow at precisely the right moment.

02Cheaper by the barrel
Saudi Deficit Triples
A SAR 84.3 billion deficit in the third quarter tripled the Kingdom's shortfall from SAR 34 billion in Q2. Oil receipts fell 23% year-on-year, per the Finance Ministry report, and total revenue dropped 4% despite a 20% jump in non-oil income. Non-oil revenue reached SAR 142.6 billion, now carrying 55% of total receipts, while spending fell 4% to SAR 343.4 billion.

The year-to-date deficit now stands at SAR 244.3 billion, nearly 50% higher than the full-year 2026 budget projection. Financing costs have climbed to 5% of government spending, up from 3.2% two years ago. The average realized oil price fell to $95.2 per barrel in Q3 from $108 in Q2. The higher royalty rate above $100 per barrel means less government revenue per barrel than the headline price suggests.

The 2026 deficit could run close to SAR 300 billion, or 6% of GDP, according to AGSI Macro. Government spending is 8% higher in the first three quarters of 2026 than the same period last year. The pressure is structural: the Kingdom is spending more on defense, infrastructure, and Vision 2030 programs. It is earning less per barrel than it did six months ago.

Gulf capital markets will price this immediately. SAMA bond yields, already rising, may face further pressure as the market anticipates new issuances. The Finance Ministry's third-quarter report now carries 55% of its own weight in non-oil revenue.

03UAE markets slide
Abu Dhabi Leads GCC Losses
The ADX General Index fell 6% in October, its worst month since March, snapping a four-month winning streak and leaving year-to-date losses at 5.3%. The DFM General Index slipped 3.2%, with seven of eight sectors in the red. Abu Dhabi Islamic Bank dropped 15% after Q3 net income rose just 2% year-on-year to AED 1.9 billion. IHC lost roughly AED 44 billion in value.

Foreign investors sold net $641.5 million of Dubai-listed shares in Q2, on top of $654 million in Q1. Trading volumes collapsed: ADX value traded fell 15.3% month-on-month to AED 20.2 billion, the lowest in at least 12 months, and volumes dropped 31.4%. DFM value traded fell 24.9% to AED 10.4 billion, with Emaar Properties alone accounting for AED 3.9 billion, over a third of the month's total.

Brent crude rose 13.6% to $117.64 per barrel in October, yet the ADX Energy index still fell 4.2%, per Kamco Invest. The disconnect exposes the war-risk overhang: oil is up, but Gulf equities are not benefiting. Investors fear the same strait that lifts prices could choke the region's exports. Emirates NBD fell 6.4% after Q3 income stayed flat quarter-on-quarter at AED 6.4 billion, weighed by AED 2.1 billion in impairments tied to DenizBank. The war premium is now a structural drag. Until the Strait of Hormuz reopens or risk premiums compress, Gulf equities will keep trading far below their oil-price fundamentals.

ADNOC's XRG Buys into Australian LNG
ADNOC's investment arm XRG is expanding into Australian LNG via a $1.45 billion deal with Shell. The move extends LNG supply reach into Asia-Pacific. ADNOC Gas also signed a five-year agreement to supply up to 1.6 million tonnes per year of LNG to TotalEnergies.
Bahrain's Arcapita Targets $1.5 Billion Logistics Push
Bahrain's Arcapita is targeting a $1.5 billion logistics expansion. Capital will be deployed over 24 months through its development arm Lintara Properties.

Qiddiya is seeking interest for water infrastructure packages. Two wastewater treatment plants with combined capacity of 69,000 cubic metres per day, plus a reverse osmosis desalination facility, are planned.
