The Lead
​
France guards Yanbu
France is sending soldiers, radars and air defense systems to protect Saudi Arabia's Yanbu oil export port. President Macron announced the deployment after Saudi-led forces reportedly intercepted six ballistic missiles fired toward Taif and Yanbu. The port handles the Kingdom's western seaboard oil exports, its main route that bypasses the Strait of Hormuz.

Saudi Arabia's Red Sea oil inventories fell to 42 million barrels as of September 22, a six-year low. That is roughly 10 million barrels below the seasonal average. The East-West pipeline that feeds Yanbu was partially attacked earlier and has only partly restarted, which means the port is running on diminished deliveries even as it reportedly gains a French military umbrella.

Why France stepped in Yanbu is not just a Saudi asset. It is the main terminal for Saudi crude that reaches European refineries without transiting Hormuz, where vessel traffic fell to nine transits a day this week from a ten-day average of 18. A direct hit on Yanbu would reportedly force every barrel onto the long route around the Cape, adding weeks and cost to European supply.

The practical change an attack on Yanbu now means an attack on a French military contingent, not just on Saudi infrastructure. That is a much higher bar for Iran or its allies to clear. For Gulf operators, the immediate signal is that Western governments are now placing personnel, not just equipment, inside the region's critical energy perimeter, and that changes the risk calculus for every port, pipeline and terminal from Ras Tanura to Fujairah. Western boots on Gulf energy infrastructure is a line that, once crossed, does not get uncrossed.

02The missing middle
UAE Series A rounds halve
UAE startups raised $895 million in the first half of 2026, up 53% from a year earlier, but the number of transactions fell 37%. Series A funding fell about 20% even as the number of rounds halved. AI companies took roughly 60% of all Series A capital, leaving founders outside AI to fight for the scraps.

Only 10.6% of UAE startups that raised early-stage funding between 2015 and 2025 made it to Series A. Just 23% of those reached Series B. Regional investors now supply more than 80% of UAE startup funding, up from 20% in 2025, because international investors show up to the table but leave their checkbooks at home. Three BlueFive Capital-backed deals, including CargoX's $250 million round and Mal's $230 million round, accounted for 60% of all UAE funding in the half.

What changed at the cap table MENA-focused funds sit on $1.45 billion in dry powder, 88% of what 17 funds have raised since 2024. That money exists but is not being deployed into the mid-stage gap where companies need $10-50 million. The bottleneck is not capital, it is conviction: no one wants to lead a round in a market where exits are down 56% year-on-year and the only reliable buyer is another fund. A funding ecosystem that funnels 80% of its capital from regional investors into 60% AI deals leaves the rest of the startup economy dependent on three large transactions for its headline number.

03Data center buying spree
MGX consortium eyes Asia data centers
The MGX-backed AI Infrastructure Partnership and IFM Investors are in exclusive talks to buy Stack Infrastructure's Asia Pacific data centers. The portfolio spans Tokyo, Osaka, Sydney, Melbourne and Johor Bahru at a valuation of $20-25 billion. That is a haircut of up to a third from the $30 billion Blue Owl originally sought, which tells you who has the pricing power in AI infrastructure deals right now.

The same consortium has already pulled off several data center acquisitions in the Gulf and beyond, building a portfolio that turns physical server farms into a rent-generating asset class. Meanwhile Mubadala is separately in talks for up to 35% of Italy's Ansaldo Energia, a turbine maker whose equipment sits inside many power plants feeding data centers.

The Gulf is buying the physical layer of global AI at precisely the moment everyone else is fighting over the models. That is either a masterstroke of infrastructure arbitrage or a massive bet that compute demand will keep outpacing the sticker price of the buildings it runs on. The buyers paying for data centers today are effectively underwriting the next decade of AI's electricity bill, and they are getting better pricing with every deal they do.

04Homework, graded
Fed proposes stablecoin rules
After two years of Gulf rule-tightening, the Fed’s GENIUS Act rules pull US stablecoin policy closer to Gulf standards. The US Federal Reserve proposed rules to implement last year's GENIUS Act, including reserve-asset limits and standardized capital requirements for stablecoin issuers. The Fed's move pulls US stablecoin policy closer to what Gulf regulators are already doing, according to officials. The UAE and Saudi Arabia have spent two years tightening rules for digital payment tokens, which means Gulf issuers may find themselves unexpectedly aligned with Washington rather than playing catch-up.

The practical effect for regional fintechs is a compliance playbook they can copy. A Gulf stablecoin that meets Fed-style reserve and capital standards can argue it is built to the highest bar, which matters when banks in the Gulf are deciding whether to partner. US rules will become the default standard Gulf banks use to judge local stablecoin issuers, whether the regulator here asks for them or not.

The AI Race
​
Claude finds a new enzyme system
Anthropic claimed Claude discovered a previously unknown enzyme system similar to CRISPR after 950 agents ran for 21 hours. The agents gathered more than 200,000 reverse transcriptases, flagged 3,500 candidate systems and narrowed the list to 20. The final hit, a system called ART, sits in viral DNA and may offer a new way to edit genes.

The compute bill was roughly $10,000, a sum that buys less than one hour of senior synthetic biology time at a good lab. That is the number to hold onto. Dario Amodei framed it as the first step on a weak-to-superhuman curve. Eighteen months ago that would have sounded like a founder pitching a slide deck. After this, it sounds like a timeline. $10,000 for 20 enzymes, or one senior postdoc's afternoon.
Business
​
UAE suspends all Iranian airline flights
The GCAA suspended Iranian airlines from UAE airspace until further notice. Oman, Azerbaijan, Iraq and Georgia imposed similar bans after US sanctions targeted Iran's aviation sector. The UAE move closes one of the last easy corridors for Iranian travelers. It also forces cargo between the two countries onto longer, more expensive routes.
UAE economy to contract 1.5% in 2026
ICAEW and Oxford Economics forecast the UAE economy will shrink 1.5% this year before growing 6.6% in 2027. Visitor numbers are expected to drop 46.7% this year. The forecast cuts against the region's public spending confidence. It suggests a rough 2026, with recovery only arriving next year.
Acwa starts operations at two Saudi plants
Acwa Power began commercial operations at the Taiba 1 and Qassim 1 combined-cycle gas turbine plants. The two facilities have combined capacity of 3.8GW. The start-up adds significant power generation capacity to the Saudi grid just as demand for electrification climbs.
US 30-year yield hits 5.48%
The US 30-year Treasury yield reached 5.48%, the highest since 2004. The 10-year yield hit 5.20% as traders priced in three more Fed rate hikes.
Kuwait sets new bid deadlines for oil projects
Kuwait Petroleum Corporation subsidiaries set new bid deadlines for two oil projects. The projects were developed by KPC subsidiaries as the country pushes to expand output.

Off Paper

Kathakali keeps its mudras
After years of training, Kathakali performers brought elaborate costumes, intricate makeup and mudras to an annual festival in Chennai. The art form takes years to master and draws its stories from the Ramayana and Mahabharata. Festivals like this one keep it alive in front of audiences who would never attend a full night-long temple performance. The same impulse is traveling to Dubai in December when Mohanlal headlines the UAE National Day show.

That is less than one hour of senior synthetic biology time at a good lab. It resets what a small lab in Riyadh or Abu Dhabi can attempt without a decade of grant-writing.

The actor Mohanlal will headline the UAE National Day show in Dubai this December. He carries with him a classical tradition that usually lives in Kerala temples and Chennai festivals. Gulf audiences get their version of the equation: star power, spectacle, and a classical form given a two-hour slot. The Gulf is increasingly where traditional craft finds a new audience willing to sit still.
