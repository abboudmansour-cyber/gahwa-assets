The Lead

Gulf tankers earn $1m a day in crisis
VLCC supertankers carrying Gulf oil to China now earn more than $1 million a day, nearly 30 times their 10-year average, as the Strait of Hormuz disruption drives freight rates sharply higher. The figure comes from Richard Matthews, director of a shipping consultancy.

The US Treasury launched Operation Economic Outcast last month, and regulators in the UAE, Egypt and Turkey are acting against lenders with links to Tehran. France has offered to help protect Saudi energy infrastructure after Houthi attacks on Aramco oil facilities in Yanbu, according to officials. The East-West pipeline shut on September 12 after a drone attack and resumed only this week. Saudi Arabia's expatriate job market is slowing amid the war uncertainty, with fewer openings and tighter salaries after cutbacks at state-funded companies. Every reopened pipeline and every escorted convoy now flows through a security premium that the region's exporters and importers both pay. Gulf shipping costs have not settled; they have repriced, and every downstream importer from India to China is already negotiating against the new number.

02UAE under fire
Gargash: UAE hit by 3,300 missiles and drones, official says
The UAE has been the target of 3,300 missiles and drones fired by Iran during the conflict, according to Anwar Gargash, senior diplomatic adviser to the UAE president.

Gargash warned that the current situation of no war, no peace is not sustainable.

The figure puts a concrete number on the Gulf's exposure to the conflict. The UAE has managed to stay out of direct military escalation, but its airspace, airports and shipping lanes all sit inside the war's battlespace. Every missile intercepted is a cost, and every near-miss is a reason insurers keep raising premiums across the Gulf, Gargash said which reported that the UAE had been hit by 3,300 missiles and drones. The 3,300 figure frames the UAE's strategic position as an outpost under fire rather than a neutral hub, and that is now the risk premium every operator in the country quietly pays.

03Backlog vs burn
AI capex needs $300bn revenue to break even
Goldman Sachs has warned that AI hyperscalers need about $300 billion a year in AI services revenue just to break even on their capital spending, and $1 trillion a year for meaningful profits. The note landed the same session Meta shares dropped about 4 percent to $749.26, erasing $8.9 billion from Mark Zuckerberg's net worth.

Meta had surged 36 percent in September and came within less than 1 percent of a $2 trillion market cap before Friday's pullback. Michael Dell, by contrast, added more than $10 billion in a day as Dell Technologies reported record revenue and a $95 billion AI server backlog.

The contrast within a single trading session tells the AI economy's sharpest story: the platforms spending on models are now being priced on their burn rate, while the companies selling the infrastructure underneath them are being priced on their backlog. Gulf sovereign and corporate investors in both layers should read that as a warning to separate AI builders from AI landlords. The market has started pricing AI platforms on cash burn and AI infrastructure on order books, a split Gulf investors will see repeated across every AI vehicle they touch.

04Payment rails
UPN consortium launches in Riyadh, targets 50 markets
Universal Payment Network, owned by FinTra Holding, launched the UPN Consortium at Money20/20 Middle East in Riyadh. The network operates as a neutral interoperability layer across more than 50 markets, serving more than 500 million accounts and providing access to more than 100 alternative payment methods.

The consortium is open to card networks, banks, mobile money operators, remittance companies, wallet providers and government payment platforms. Its pitch is that local institutions retain their identity, regulatory responsibilities and customer relationships while UPN connects them.

The first live transaction from a local wallet in Africa moved through the network this month. FinTra Holding founder and CEO Dr. Waleed Sadek said the next generation of global payments will not be built by replacing local payment systems but by connecting them. The Arab world's fragmented payment maps have long been a tax on every cross-border transaction; UPN is betting that a neutral rail beats a national champion. A working interoperability layer across Arab and African payment systems would lower the cost of every remittance and cross-border invoice the region already moves.

05Micro, small, and four-year
EBRD lends EFG Holding $40m for Egypt MSMEs
EFG Holding secured a $40 million, four-year senior unsecured loan from the European Bank for Reconstruction and Development. The facility carries a one-year grace period and is the first EBRD loan extended directly to EFG Holding at the holding-company level.

Previous EBRD facilities went to EFG subsidiaries including Bank NXT, Tanmeyah and Valu. The new capital is earmarked to expand micro, small and medium enterprise financing in Egypt.

The agreement was signed on the sidelines of EFG Hermes' 12th Annual London Investor Conference. Egypt's MSME credit gap remains one of the largest untapped lending pools in the region, and development finance is now the most reliable door into it. The EBRD's first direct holding-company loan to EFG signals that Egyptian MSME lending is moving from subsidiary-level bets to group-level commitment.

China's COOEC lands $4bn Qatar offshore contracts
QatarEnergy awarded state-owned China Offshore Oil Engineering Company two EPC packages worth $4 billion for the Bul Hanine offshore field expansion. The award locks in a Chinese contractor for one of Qatar's largest brownfield offshore projects. The packages cover engineering, procurement and construction work. The timing matters: Qatar is pressing ahead with oilfield expansion.
GFH spins its Bahrain mall portfolio into a new REIT
The deal values the combined retail assets at roughly $800 million and gives GFH a listed vehicle to recycle capital. The REIT will seed with three income-generating malls and a development plot. The move lets GFH move real estate assets off its balance sheet. It also gives investors a Bahraini retail REIT to trade.
Saudi Arabia Railways signs phosphate rail supply deals
Saudi Arabia Railways signed agreements with international rail suppliers including Alstom, Progress Rail and Greenbrier for phosphate transport infrastructure. The deals cover rolling stock and components for the kingdom's mining logistics corridor. The signing brings in three of the world's largest rail equipment makers. The contracts support the phosphate export chain.
Kuwait Petroleum Corporation sets new bid deadlines for two projects
Kuwait Petroleum Corporation subsidiaries have issued revised bid deadlines for two oil projects as contractors reassess schedules amid the regional conflict. The timelines give bidders more time to secure logistics and insurance. The shifts are a direct response to the war premium on shipping and insurance. Contractors now price conflict risk into their submissions.
US proposes $10bn Gulf energy rebuild fund
Washington is seeking matching contributions from eight regional states to create a $10 billion fund for energy infrastructure damaged in the Iran war. The fund would target repairs to oil facilities, ports, and power infrastructure. The proposal is an admission that Gulf budgets alone will not cover the damage quickly. It also gives the US a financial stake in reconstruction.
QatarEnergy advances 53km jet fuel pipeline to Hamad International
Contractors have submitted bids for QatarEnergy's project to build a roughly 53km pipeline supplying Jet A-1 fuel from Mesaieed Industrial City to Hamad International Airport. The line will secure aviation fuel supply for Doha's expanding hub. The project moves on a parallel track to the regional aviation disruption. A direct pipeline protects Doha's fuel flow.

The gender gap in Gulf gaming is narrower than the world average, and that is a talent pool few regional sponsors have priced yet.

The Last Sip
​
Which Gulf city has more registered cats than cars?
The answer
Kuwait City has a stray cat population thought to be in the hundreds of thousands, and no official registry for cats exists because virtually none are registered as domestic pets. The city's streets and markets are home to so many cats that residents have built feeding stations and volunteer networks. Municipal attempts to control the population have been sporadic and underfunded. The cats have, in effect, won the right of way. The cats never filed for planning permission, which is why they got it.
What did the East-West pipeline shut down for?
The answer
The East-West pipeline shut on September 12 after a drone attack, according to officials, and resumed only this week. It is one of Saudi Arabia's two major east-west oil arteries, running from the Eastern Province to Yanbu on the Red Sea. The shutdown forced Saudi Arabia to find alternate export routes. Its return restores a critical redundancy the kingdom relies on when Hormuz is threatened. The pipeline's schedule is now set by drone traffic, not maintenance teams.
