The Lead
​
Saudi Arabia's East-West oil pipeline is down and Yanbu loadings are paused, but the bigger story is a $329.5 million fintech round that just minted a unicorn while the region watches oil prices climb past $108.
Brent settled $3.07 higher at $108.75 a barrel, while WTI gained $4.44 to $105.83, both the highest closes since May 19. The pipeline carries up to 5 million barrels per day across roughly 1,200 kilometers, and Goldman Sachs estimates full repairs could take up to eight weeks.

According to officials, Saudi Arabia is rerouting some shipments through the Strait of Hormuz with US military support, and crude stored in Egypt via the Sumed pipeline can buy Riyadh another week of loadings. Sidi Kerir exports more than doubled to about 2.3 million barrels per day in August from 1 million in July. Yanbu stocks have fallen by more than 7 million barrels over two months to around 9 million barrels.

Why the shutdown matters The closure cuts one of the kingdom's two main export routes at a time when Hormuz transits have collapsed to near zero. Only four commodity vessels crossed the strait on Monday, compared to a pre-war average of about 125 daily transits. The US energy secretary says flows should resume within days, but Goldman Sachs sees rising probability of Brent above $120 if Gulf output stays below pre-war levels into 2027.

What this changes for Gulf operators European refiners are scrambling for alternative barrels, and Saudi Arabia is drawing down stored crude to meet commitments. The rerouting adds transit time and cost, while the repair timeline remains uncertain. Gulf operators should expect elevated freight rates and wider crude differentials until the pipeline is fully back online. The energy map of the Gulf is being redrawn in real time, and every barrel now moves at a premium.

02Unicorn at 15 million users
barq's $329.5M Series A mints unicorn
Saudi fintech barq closed a $329.5 million Series A at a $1.85 billion valuation, making it one of the fastest companies in the region to reach unicorn status. Investors include Noon Investments, Sohar International Bank, and M20 Fund. Founded in 2023 by Ahmed Alenazi, former CEO of stc pay, barq has surpassed 15 million users from more than 210 nationalities.

The money will fuel operational efficiency, product development, new financial and tech solutions, and expansion into new regional and international markets. barq has already partnered with Alipay+ for cross-border QR payments across more than 220 markets. The total value of funds processed has exceeded SAR 440 billion since launch.

The round cements Saudi Arabia's position as the Gulf's fintech leader. barq joins a growing list of SAMA-licensed fintechs that have crossed the $1 billion threshold, and the valuation signals investor confidence in the kingdom's digital payments push. For regional fintechs, the barq deal resets the bar for what a Series A can look like in the Gulf. The gap between Saudi and every other Gulf fintech market just widened by $329.5 million.

03Mecca in the crosshairs
Houthi drone intercepted south of Mecca
Saudi air defences intercepted and destroyed a Houthi drone south of Mecca on Tuesday, before it entered prohibited airspace, according to officials. The intercept came hours after Saudi Arabia launched more than 50 strikes against Houthi targets across five Yemeni governorates, including Taiz, Lahj, Al Jawf, Marib, and Hodeidah, according to reports. The Houthis have also captured the Khabub mountain range in southern Taiz and parts of Maqbanah, along with the Greater and Lesser Hanish islands in the Red Sea.

The fighting has displaced more than 100,000 people, and the UN has released $9 million from its emergency relief fund. The conflict has now killed more than 500 people in the latest escalation, according to officials say For Gulf operators with staff or supply chains near the Yemen border, the risk profile has shifted sharply in the past 48 hours. Yemen's war is no longer a border problem, it has reached the spiritual center of the kingdom, and the region is taking notice.

04AI data centers, Emirati made
Khazna, Siemens partner on AI data centers
Four tracks, one MoU: Khazna and Siemens in Munich. The partnership covers AI-ready infrastructure strategy, digital twin technology, next-generation 800V DC power architecture, and Emirati engineering talent development. Khazna's footprint already spans Dammam and a 500 MW site in Italy with Eni.

The deal positions Khazna to compete for Gulf AI workloads at a time when data center capacity is the new oil. One of AWS' three data-hosting zones in the UAE is reportedly still down after a March strike, and the Bahrain facility is not back online. That leaves regional operators scrambling for compute capacity while hyperscalers reroute traffic.

For Gulf businesses, the Khazna-Siemens pact is a bet that sovereign AI infrastructure will win over imported cloud dependence. The talent development track signals an intent to build a local engineering bench, not just import one. The next 18 months will show whether that bet pays off. The smart money in regional AI is not just in the models, it is in the floor space and power architecture underneath them.

05Clarity Act dies
Crypto longs lose $570M on Clarity Act
Crypto futures bets worth $570 million were liquidated in 24 hours after the US Senate rejected the Digital Asset Market Clarity Act. The procedural vote failed 49-50, leaving the bill 11 votes short of what it needed. Every major token fell, with XRP sinking 10% and bitcoin sliding toward $76,000.

Analysts are downplaying the defeat as non-structural, arguing crypto markets will continue to move with interest rates and the broader monetary environment. Polymarket odds of passage this year had risen from 22% to 32% before the vote. The bill had incorporated 126 Democrat-requested changes.

The vote leaves US crypto regulation in limbo, which pushes more activity toward jurisdictions with clearer rules. Gulf regulators have been actively courting crypto firms. The question is whether the UAE, Bahrain, and Saudi Arabia can convert US policy paralysis into a durable advantage. Washington's inability to pass a crypto law is becoming the Gulf's most effective recruitment tool.

The AI Race
​
OpenAI asks $1.2T
OpenAI is courting investors for a capital raise at a $1.2 trillion valuation before its public debut. CEO Sam Altman said a listing is unlikely before next year. The raise would reportedly make OpenAI one of the most valuable private companies in history.

The ask lands while the semiconductor index just lost 5.9% in a day. That divergence shows capital is chasing AI exposure even as chip valuations wobble. Gulf sovereign wealth funds are among the investors OpenAI has spoken with, which makes the region a central player rather than a spectator. Gulf capital is being asked to anchor what filings show is the largest private valuation in history at the same moment Abu Dhabi and Riyadh are building their own sovereign AI stacks.
Business

Saudi construction tech BRKZ lands $31M Series B
BRKZ, a Saudi construction technology firm, raised $31 million in a Series B round. Aramco's Wa'ed Ventures and 500 Global co-led $13 million of equity. The remaining $18 million came as growth debt from Stride Ventures. Revenue is on track to triple in 2026 after 2.5x growth in 2025.
SABIC Agri-Nutrients awards $3.46B EPC deal
SABIC Agri-Nutrients gave Samsung E&A a $3.46 billion contract to build an ammonia and urea complex. The project includes a 1.2 million-tonne-per-year ammonia plant and two urea plants with combined capacity of 2.6 million tonnes. Commissioning is scheduled for Q3 2030. The award is one of the largest downstream petrochemical engineering contracts signed in the kingdom this year.
Altera files confidentially for US IPO
MGX-backed chipmaker Altera has filed confidentially for an initial public offering. The listing could raise over $2 billion as early as this year. MGX joined Silver Lake as co-investor in the majority stake acquisition of the former Intel programmable chip business. The filing comes as US chip equities wobble, with the semiconductor index losing 5.9% on Monday.
L'imad, Azerbaijan partner on trade corridor
L'imad is partnering with Azerbaijan Investment Holding for joint investments in critical minerals, transport infrastructure and energy. UAE-Azerbaijan non-oil trade more than doubled from $980 million in 2022 to $2.16 billion in 2025. The partnership gives Abu Dhabi a foothold in Central Asian supply chains. It arrives just as Gulf maritime routes face the worst disruption in decades.
BRKZ closes $31M Series B for materials procurement
Saudi construction tech firm BRKZ raised $31 million in a Series B round, with $13 million in equity co-led by Aramco's Wa'ed Ventures and 500 Global. The remaining $18 million came as growth debt from Stride Ventures. Revenue is on track to triple in 2026 after 2.5x growth in 2025.

The Last Sip
​
Which Gulf city has more registered cats than cars?
The answer
Kuwait City is home to an enormous stray cat population, with some estimates putting the number above a million. Restaurants and residents leave food, neighbourhoods have informal feeding routes, and the municipality has repeatedly launched neutering programs rather than removal. The cats have their own waterfront at night, around the fish market. The city's traffic jams at least include some very well-fed pedestrians.
Why does Dubai's airport handle more international passengers than any other?
The answer
Dubai International Airport sits within an eight-hour flight of two-thirds of the world's population. Emirates built a hub that connects Europe, Asia, Africa and the Americas in a single stop. The airport processes more international travellers than London Heathrow and Hong Kong combined on some days. Half of humanity is one layover away.
