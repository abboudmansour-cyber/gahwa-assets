The Lead
​
Saudi Arabia wants to insure ships against missiles, and it's asking London brokers, according to sources, to price the uninsurable.
The scheme would offer up to SAR 700 million in commercial cover per insured event, whether that is a ship seizure or a missile strike. Saudi Export-Import Bank would add hundreds of millions of dollars in backstop capacity per insured entity.

One version of the proposal would see Saudi Re and Riyadh Re leading a consortium of reinsurers that could include international firms. The urgency is not theoretical. Houthi forces reportedly attacked the Bahri vessel Amzan in the Red Sea, and the group announced a naval blockade on Saudi Arabia late last month, targeting Yanbu. Yanbu carried 92% of June's seaborne crude exports and 78% of July's, and six Bahri-operated Saudi supertankers returning from Asia diverted around southern Africa to bypass the Red Sea and Bab Al Mandab Strait.

Saudi Arabia has already rerouted an estimated 60-70% of oil exports through the East-West pipeline. The state-backed cover is designed to keep the remaining seaborne traffic moving without forcing shippers to price the risk themselves. The scheme would effectively make the Kingdom the insurer of last resort for its own shipping lanes, a role no commercial market is willing to shoulder.

For Gulf operators, this is a signal that Riyadh is preparing for a prolonged disruption rather than a quick resolution. The proposed SAR 700 million per-event cap is the number to watch; it tells shippers exactly how much risk the state is willing to absorb before private capital must step back in. If the pool launches, expect shipping rates through the Red Sea to reprice before the first policy is even written.

Fasset Hits Unicorn Status
UAE-based fintech Fasset raised USD 68 million in a Series C round led by Japan's SBI Group, valuing the company at USD 1 billion. The round, which included Speedinvest, follows a USD 51 million Series B in May, bringing total funding this year to USD 119 million. Fasset operates an AI-powered stablecoin neobanking platform.

The company processes more than USD 40 billion in annualized transaction volume, serving more than 3 million wallets across 125 countries and over 1,000 enterprises. The new capital will support expansion of its Own Network, which connects banks, telecom operators, and payment providers across more than 100 banking corridors.

SBI Group's backing gives Fasset a bridge into Japan's financial system, including access to a network supporting bank-account remittances to approximately 200 countries. For Gulf investors, this is the clearest sign yet that the region's stablecoin infrastructure is scaling beyond pilots into real transaction volume. The unicorn valuation matters less than the 3 million wallets already moving money. The next question is whether Fasset can convert a Japanese alliance into a full banking licence in the Gulf.

Gulf Jet Fuel Rebounds
Gulf exports of jet fuel to Europe jumped from 362,000 tonnes in June to 874,000 tonnes in July, making the region Europe's biggest supplier, per Kpler data. The rebound follows a collapse to 175,000 tonnes in May, when supplies from Kuwait, UAE, and Saudi Arabia sank to zero amid the Iran conflict.

Saudi Arabia ramped up exports from Red Sea refineries to bypass the Strait of Hormuz. Jet fuel prices remain around 50% above pre-war levels, according to Argus Media pricing data. Before the war, Europe sourced around 40% of its regular jet fuel imports through the Strait of Hormuz.

The recovery is still 37% below March levels of 1.3 million tonnes. The shift to Red Sea refineries is the structural change: even if Hormuz reopens, the supply chain has already been redrawn. For Gulf producers, the message is that redundancy is now a competitive advantage. Gulf refineries on the Red Sea are now the swing sellers in Europe, and that position will outlast any ceasefire.

04​
Bitcoin Hits $80k Again
Bitcoin touched USD 80,526 on the day, the first time it has traded above USD 80,000 since May, as a 38% surge from its June plunge brought buyers back. Spot bitcoin ETFs pulled in USD 1.92 billion in net inflows for the week ended August 21, the strongest since October 2025.

The recovery is being driven by shifting US Treasury policy and renewed ETF demand. The Crypto Fear & Greed Index hit 78, its highest level since December 2024.

For Gulf investors, the recovery matters because the region's fintech and crypto infrastructure is built on volume. The more bitcoin trades, the more liquidity flows through the Gulf's exchanges and stablecoin rails. The question is whether this is a durable repricing or another sharp rally that gives back a chunk of its gains. The next pullback will tell you whether this is a real recovery or another trapdoor.

e& UAE Bundles Copilot
e& UAE bundled Microsoft Copilot into Business Pro AI plans at no additional cost, targeting SMEs across the country. The Microsoft AI Diffusion Report finds 70.1% of the UAE's working-age population uses generative AI, the highest rate in the world.

Business Pro AI provides secure connectivity, productivity tools, and AI-powered assistance under one subscription. Business customers no longer need to purchase software licences separately or manage multiple vendors.

For SMEs, this is the first genuinely bundled AI offering at scale in the Gulf. The 70% adoption rate means the UAE is not a test market; it is the reference market for how AI gets sold to small business. The bundle price will become the benchmark for every telecom operator in the Gulf trying to sell AI to SMEs.
