The Lead · GCC & SAUDI
Saudi Arabia's PIF now owns a bigger slice of SpaceX than anyone except Alphabet, Fidelity, and one venture fund, a $26.3 billion bet that makes Riyadh the world's most muscular space landlord.
​
That makes PIF the fourth-largest disclosed institutional holder, behind Alphabet, Fidelity Investments, and Gigafund Management. SpaceX shares closed at about $137.60 on August 14, 1.9% above the IPO price of $135 set in June.

The position emerged in the same week SpaceX completed its acquisition of Anysphere, the developer of the Cursor coding assistant, at an implied valuation of about $60 billion. PIF's broader portfolio includes 177.1 million Lucid Group shares and 72.8 million Uber Technologies shares, part of an estimated $1.2 trillion in total assets.

The stake signals that Gulf sovereign capital is now a structural player in the commercial space economy, not a passive tourist. While Alphabet's 2015 investment returned over 86 times its original $900 million, PIF's position is a late-stage concentrated bet on the physical infrastructure of AI and space. For a fund tasked with diversifying the kingdom's revenue base, SpaceX offers exposure to launch economics and satellite broadband at a scale no regional rival can replicate.
What to do with it
Founders and operators trying to understand where Gulf sovereign capital is moving next should map PIF's SpaceX stake against its EV and fintech holdings, the pattern points to infrastructure-heavy, asset-backed bets.

Central Banks Hoard Gold at Record
Global central bank gold purchases reached a record 289 tonnes in the second quarter of 2026, according to Deutsche Bank data.

The People's Bank of China added its largest monthly amount of gold reserves in July since October 2023. Gold rose 0.70% during the week to $4,432 an ounce, while silver gained 1.61% to $64.83.

For Gulf investors, the signal is that the official sector still treats gold as the reserve asset of last resort. With US inflation easing to 3.4% annually and retail sales falling 0.6% in July, the rate-hike probability for September dropped to 33% from 55%, but central banks are not waiting for the Fed. They are buying gold now, which keeps a floor under prices even as real yields move. Gulf family offices and treasury teams should treat the persistent central bank bid as a signal that gold's downtrends will be shallow, and use any dip to build positions.

GCC & SAUDI
Neom Hydrogen Plant Nears Commissioning
Construction has completed on the $8.5 billion Neom hydrogen project at Oxagon, and the facility is now entering the commissioning phase with commercial operations targeted for 2027.

The plant represents one of the largest single bets on green hydrogen anywhere, and it arrives at a moment when global hydrogen demand forecasts are being revised downward in several markets. For the Gulf, the project matters because it locks in a first-mover position on hydrogen production at scale, even if the export economics remain unsettled. Contractors and suppliers who worked on the construction phase are now pivoting to commissioning services and long-term operations and maintenance, a transition that creates a second wave of smaller contracts. EPC and service firms with commissioning expertise should position for follow-on packages at Neom as the plant moves toward commercial operations, since commissioning and early operations typically carry higher margins than construction.

04MACRO & GEO
Yemen's Mocha Port Halts Operations
Yemen's Mocha port has suspended commercial and maritime operations after being hit by more than 25 missiles in Houthi attacks, the port director said.

The attacks killed seven people and caused an estimated $16 million in losses. Mocha sits near the Bab el-Mandab strait, a critical route for international shipping.

The closure, according to officials, tightens pressure on a Red Sea corridor already strained by months of conflict. For Gulf shippers and insurers, the direct impact is longer alternative routings and higher war-risk premiums on vessels transiting near Yemen. Any sustained disruption at Bab el-Mandab would ripple through energy and container trades that Gulf ports depend on. Logistics and trade finance teams should reassess Red Sea exposure and update contingency routings now, before a broader closure forces a scramble for capacity.

FINTECH & CRYPTO
Arab Therapy Closes $2M Pre-Series A
Arab Therapy, a Berlin-based but Jordan-born digital mental health startup, closed a $2 million pre-Series A round led by Manara Ventures, with participation from Anara Impact Fund and Value Makers Studio.

The company provides Arabic-language mental healthcare through online therapy sessions, content, and AI-powered tools, operating across Saudi Arabia, Jordan, Germany, and Egypt. The funding will expand its Saudi presence and scale the AI-enabled offering.

The regional gap is stark: more than 400 million Arabic speakers across MENA have access to mental health services that the World Health Organization describes as significantly under-resourced. For Gulf employers, the rise of Arabic-first digital mental health tools offers a practical way to meet workforce well-being expectations that are increasingly written into employee benefits benchmarks. HR and benefits managers in the Gulf should evaluate Arabic-language digital mental health platforms now, before the space consolidates and pricing moves from grant-subsidized to market rates.

PEOPLE
Tata Chairman Exit Sparks Governance Fight
Tata Sons chairman N. Chandrasekaran will step down on February 20, 2027, after a six-month reappointment deadlock in which four of six directors supported his extension but Tata Trusts chairman Noel Tata opposed it.

The uncertainty erased about $4.6 billion from the combined market value of listed Tata companies. Tata Sons profit after tax rose 21.8% to Rs319.61 billion in the latest fiscal year, while the group's combined market capitalization climbed from about $76 billion in 2017 to $277 billion by 2023.

For Gulf investors and partners, the Tata governance fight is a reminder that India's largest conglomerates operate under succession rules that can swing valuations at scale. Any Gulf family office or sovereign fund with exposure to Indian equities should track the selection committee process now underway at the Sir Dorabji Tata Trust. Gulf investors holding Indian conglomerate positions should reassess governance risk in Tata group firms before the February 2027 transition, since the board deadlock has already destroyed value.
