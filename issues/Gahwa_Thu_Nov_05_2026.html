The Lead
​
Saudi deficit hits SAR 244 bn in nine months
Saudi Arabia's 9M 2026 deficit reached SAR 244.3 bn. That is already SAR 78.9 bn past the full-year forecast of SAR 165.4 bn, with one quarter still to run. The Finance Ministry published the figures in its quarterly budget report on Tuesday.

The overshoot came down to spending, not revenue weakness. Revenues actually rose 3% year-on-year to SAR 858.9 bn, with non-oil receipts up 8% to SAR 412.5 bn. But grants exploded 197%, subsidies jumped 49%, and financing expenses climbed 38% as borrowing costs bit. Capital spending hit SAR 137.6 bn, up 17%.

Why the deficit keeps widening Public debt reached SAR 1.774 tn, up 16.8% from a year earlier. Government reserves stand at SAR 399.5 bn. The preliminary budget for 2027 shows a shortfall of SAR 191 bn on revenues of SAR 1.2 tn against spending near SAR 1.4 tn.

The math gets harder before it gets easier. The IMF projects Saudi growth of roughly 8% in 2027, but oil revenue fell 1% this period even with crude averaging above $94 per barrel. The deficit is structural, not cyclical. The full-year forecast was missed by slightly more than a rounding error.

02Oil and elections
Brent slides to $94 as Democrats sweep Congress
Brent fell 8.47% to $94.83 a barrel and WTI dropped 7.5% to $88.88 after Democrats took both the House and Senate in US midterms. Reportedly, the White House confirmed limited US strikes on Iran's Qeshm Island, while Tehran said its air defenses intercepted drones. Negotiations between Washington and Tehran have resumed with Qatar and Pakistan acting as mediators.

The market is pricing in a faster end to the Iran war. But US officials told Axios the military is still finalizing preparations for possible major combat operations. A wider de-escalation would cut Gulf freight costs and bring export volumes back to pre-war levels.

03Nearly there, ammonia
Ta'ziz raises $1.39bn for Ruwais projects
Ta'ziz closed $1.39 bn in new financing for two Ruwais projects. Of that, $928 mn is for the chemicals export terminal and $460 mn for a blue ammonia plant. That brings its total raised in 2026 to $3.39 bn after a $2 bn methanol project closed in April.

The ammonia plant is a joint venture of Ta'ziz, Fertiglobe, Mitsui and GS Energy. It will produce one million tonnes a year and is more than 90% complete. The terminal, a JV with Adnoc and Advario Star Energy, sits around 70% complete.

Phase one of the Ruwais development is set to deliver 4.7 mtpa of chemicals by end-2028. The project is expected to contribute $50 bn to the UAE economy and support 26,000 jobs. A 90%-complete ammonia plant and a 70%-complete terminal have raised more money than many finished ports ever see.
Source: The National Business

04Trade finance
Oman fintech mamun raises $3 mn
Oman's mamun raised $3 mn in seed funding led by Phaze Ventures through its Oman Fund, with participation from UAE-based Jasoor Ventures. The Shariah-compliant platform connects businesses with investors to finance imports, exports and working capital.

Where the gap lives The Asian Development Bank puts the global trade finance gap at $2.5 trillion, and 41% of SME applications are turned down. Oman's non-oil exports ran 10% higher through the first seven months of 2026, with the UAE as its largest trading partner.

mamun operates from Muscat, Abu Dhabi and Hong Kong, with 40% of its trade finance settlements processed through Hong Kong institutions. It is licensed by Oman's Financial Services Authority. The global trade finance gap now has one more platform trying to fit through it.
Source: Wamda

The AI Race
​
Texas vote threatens half of US data centres
Democratic wins in Texas, Ohio and Georgia could halt up to half of all under-construction US AI data centres, according to Finimize. Texas alone accounts for roughly 30% of planned US capacity for 2028. Governor-elect Gina Hinojosa campaigned on a moratorium until new rules are written. The state already had 470 gigawatts of proposals pending before Greg Abbott froze permitting in September. Ohio's 46 planned projects total 16 gigawatts.

The political shift does not slow the models themselves. AI compute demand keeps rising. What it does is push the building somewhere else, and quickly. Hyperscalers cannot wait for new state rules. They will look for places with power, land and a government that wants them. The Gulf already holds several such places. If anything, the US slowdown tightens global AI compute supply and makes Gulf data centres more valuable. Gulf AI investors should read this as a demand signal, not a warning: the world still needs the compute, just somewhere else.
