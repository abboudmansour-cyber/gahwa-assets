The Lead
​
Gulf crude bypasses Hormuz at record pace
Around 40% of Gulf crude now bypasses the Strait of Hormuz, up from 17% before the war, according to Kpler data cited by CNBC. HSBC Group CEO Georges Elhedery told an audience the strait may become redundant five years from now. Saudi Arabia wants to move up to 10 million barrels a day outside the Gulf within two to three years. This is according to Energy Minister Prince Abdulaziz bin Salman. The East-West Pipeline currently carries 7 million barrels a day from the Eastern Province to Yanbu on the Red Sea coast. Refined products crossing Hormuz have already collapsed to around 677,000 barrels a day in late September, compared with 3.6 million before the war.

Aramco sold roughly 20 million barrels to Asian refiners in a single week in September using ship-to-ship transfers in the Gulf of Oman. The company and its Asian buyers disagree over whether those spot purchases count toward 2026 commitments. At least one refiner has been asked to make up any shortfall by committing to more crude in 2027.

The pipeline push is a multi-decade bet that the Gulf's oil can reach customers without ever passing through a waterway Iran has repeatedly threatened to close, reportedly. For Gulf exporters, the prize is simple: remove the single point of failure that has defined the region's oil trade for half a century. The strait may still matter for global shipping.

02Houthi front
Reportedly, Houthis deny drone attacks as Saudi airports disrupted
The Saudi-led coalition said it intercepted and destroyed four drones launched toward the Kingdom over Madinah and the Eastern Province, according to the coalition. The coalition said it also destroyed another drone heading for the eastern region. The Houthis denied launching any operations toward Saudi territory yesterday and rejected the coalition's claim of drone interceptions over Madinah.

The Houthi military spokesman Yahya Saree said a ballistic missile struck a military camp adjacent to Yemen's Aden airport in Khor Maksar district. The Houthis claim the camp houses a command center for the Saudi-led forces. A transportation ministry official in Aden said airport operations were temporarily suspended and the tarmac wasn't damaged.

03Construction costs
Saudi construction costs jump on copper and aluminium
JLL raised its 2026 Saudi tender price inflation forecast to 5%, up from 4% and double the 2% recorded in 2025. The consultancy expects prices to keep rising at 4 to 5% next year. Copper rose 44.5% year on year through August, while aluminium climbed 23.9% and diesel added 7.9%.

Mid-range residential construction now runs SAR 3,200 to SAR 4,100 per square metre, while Grade A office fit-outs cost SAR 4,500 to SAR 6,000. Hospitality is the priciest at SAR 7,000 to SAR 11,000 per key-equivalent square metre. Contractors are absorbing part of the increase in thinner margins rather than passing the full cost through to clients.

The amended White Land Tax adds pressure, moving to progressive rates of up to 10% of land value from a flat 2.5%. For developers already racing to deliver more than 115,000 homes a year, the maths keeps getting harder. Contractors are absorbing price increases, which means the margins are doing the actual heavy lifting now.
Source: Saudi Gazette Business

04Open invitation
ADFW closes half its doors to drive deals
Abu Dhabi Finance Week will run around half of its December 7-10 sessions behind closed doors, a deliberate shift toward private deal-making. The event expects more than 40,000 attendees including 8,000 senior executives and leaders representing roughly $70 trillion in assets under management. Last year's edition generated 82 MOUs and partnerships, nearly double the 44 signed in 2024.

Fourteen global financial firms managing more than $5 trillion announced plans to establish an ADGM presence in the first half of this year. This is according to ADGM chairman Ahmed Al Zaabi. The move comes as Saudi Arabia's Future Investment Initiative was reportedly postponed to 2027 after Houthi missile and drone attacks on Riyadh. Abu Dhabi is positioning itself as the Gulf's most reliable venue for deal-making while the region digests a harder security environment. The Gulf's deal calendar is consolidating, and Abu Dhabi is betting that quiet rooms will produce louder results than keynote stages.
Source: The National Business

05IPO reform
Saudi IPO rules tighten after listings sink
The Saudi CMA is proposing that underwriting banks buy unsold shares in IPOs and that institutional bidders prove they actually have the cash. Issuers would also have to publish at least one year of forecasts. The rules could take effect by November 2.

Of 37 Saudi companies floated on Tadawul and Nomu in 2025, 29 traded below their IPO price by mid-2026, according to Argaam estimates. Only two main-market listings were completed in 2026. GCC IPOs in 2025 raised $5.8 billion from 42 deals, the thinnest in five years and 54.8% below 2024.

The Flynas IPO drew SAR 409 billion in institutional orders. But the stock now trades around SAR 50, down roughly 37% from its SAR 80 offer price. Regulators are moving to ensure the next wave of listings does not repeat the pattern. Gulf markets are shifting from quantity of listings to quality of listings. The next company to float will face a much less forgiving set of rules.

The AI Race
​
Anthropic's S-1 leaks an $80bn loss
Anthropic's leaked S-1 shows an $80 billion half-year net loss and up to $750 billion in planned compute spending. The filing was not public, but the numbers are moving through markets. That loss is larger than the entire sovereign wealth fund of some Gulf states.

The AI race is now a contest of who can spend the most on compute without running out of patient capital. Anthropic's spending signals that frontier models are not yet a business. They are a national project with a venture return attached. Gulf funds are already in the game through MGX and others, and this filing tells them the cheque size is about to grow. The cheque Gulf funds write for compute now has an extra zero.
Business
​
Emirates NBD posts record income
Emirates NBD reported a record nine-month income of AED 42.2 billion. Net income rose 2% to AED 19.3 billion. Gross loans climbed 27% year to date to AED 833 billion. Deposits rose 19% to AED 935 billion.
Saudi banks report mixed third quarter
Al Rajhi posted an 11.7% rise in net income to SAR 7.1 billion. Provisions jumped 49.5% to SAR 852 million. SNB reported SAR 6.5 billion, down 0.3%. Riyad Bank grew 6.6% to SAR 2.9 billion.
France buys 295,000 tonnes of diesel
Saudi Arabia agreed to supply France 95,000 tonnes of diesel in October and November. The UAE and Bahrain will each provide 100,000 tonnes monthly. The combined 295,000 tonnes covers roughly 10% of French consumption. European diesel prices nearly tripled this year.
Whoop gets $75 million from Mubadala, plans Abu Dhabi office
Whoop announced a clinical research partnership with Abu Dhabi's Department of Health alongside a $75 million anchor investment from Mubadala, part of a $575 million Series G valuing the company at $10.1 billion. The firm said GCC growth is up around 15 times in three years.

Off Paper
​
A ring that may catch premature birth
Oura hired two scientists in Abu Dhabi to test whether ring data can give early warning of premature birth. The 24-month pilot runs with Corniche Hospital and Mediclinic Airport Road. PureHealth is the technology partner. Results are expected in 2028. A ring worn for nine months could deliver the earliest 'you may want to sit down'.
A cat refuses to miss its flight
Chuuti escaped its carrier at Dubai International Airport while travelling to Sri Lanka. Owner Avishka Kaumadi stayed at the airport for 28 hours searching. The cat was found in the Terminal 1 baggage area after 10 days. A 28-hour stay at the airport was the lesser part of the ordeal.
