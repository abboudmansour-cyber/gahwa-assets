The Lead
​
Saudi startups are sitting on $1.45 billion of unspent venture capital, and LEAP just opened its doors to figure out where it goes.
LEAP 2026 opened in Riyadh today with 1,289 investors from 1,016 firms carrying a combined $14.5 trillion in assets under management. The fifth edition of the tech conference runs through September 3 across 16 stages and more than 20 content tracks. Speakers include Saudi Minister Abdullah Alswaha, AMD CEO Lisa Su, Cisco CEO Chuck Robbins, and HUMAIN's Tareq Amin.

Saudi startups raised only $219 million across 72 rounds in the first half of 2026, down 74% from a year earlier. Round count fell 41%. Yet MENA funds hold an estimated $1.45 billion in committed-but-undeployed capital, 88% of everything raised since 2024. The money exists; it has been waiting for a reason to move.

Saudi-based investors supplied 74% of the Kingdom's funding in the first half, up from 54% a year earlier. Local money has a structural advantage right now: it can walk into a company, meet the founders, and read the actual numbers. International VCs are slowing negotiations, wary of pure AI agents and standalone enterprise-AI plays where defensibility is hard to prove.

Why the money is frozen:: Growth-stage startups will get attention first, while early-stage rounds close only at reset valuations. M&A is picking up as founders hold realistic conversations about merging, and founders who lack stamina for bridge rounds are drifting back to salaried roles. The Rocket Fuel competition has drawn more than 3,000 applications, with 100 finalists competing for $1 million in equity-free prizes. A single IPO clearing by year-end would restart the cycle faster than any keynote from any stage. For the founders in the room this week, the question is no longer whether the money exists but whether they have the numbers to unlock it.

02Hormuz escalation
US hits Iran launchers, Iran fires back at Jordan, officials say.
US forces struck two Iranian rocket launchers on Larak Island in the Strait of Hormuz late Sunday, according to US Central Command, which reported the strikes. Centcom said the reported strikes were limited and precise, targeting what it called an imminent threat from IRGC minelaying forces. This was, according to officials, the first known American military action against Iran since late July. Iran's Revolutionary Guards said the attack killed and wounded several Iranian fighters and civilians. Iran responded by launching missiles toward two US military air bases in Jordan, where Jordan's army reportedly intercepted eight of them. The exchange marks the first major escalation in more than a month.

Trump posted an AI-generated video on Truth Social claiming Iran's Kharg Island energy hub is being blown to smithereens. The White House and Defense Department did not respond to requests for comment, and no other evidence supported the claim.

The six-month intermittent war continues to reshape Gulf logistics and insurance costs. Cargo flows have already shifted toward Fujairah, Sohar, Duqm, and Salalah outside the Strait of Hormuz. War-risk insurance has risen sharply, adding millions of dollars per voyage. The World Travel and Tourism Council estimates the war costs the Middle East at least $600 million a day in lost visitor spending. Gulf operators with supply chains through Hormuz should expect continued volatility in insurance and freight costs as the tit-for-tat cycle resumes.

03AI finance
UAE's Oro raises $3M for AI finance
UAE-based financial AI platform Oro has raised $3 million in a strategic funding round co-led by MH Ventures and Mapleblock Capital. The round included M2M Capital, Archer Capital, X21 Digital, and follow-on backing from Disrupt.com and ZIGLabs. Founded in 2024 by Varun Choudhary, Oro has now raised $4 million total.

Oro has onboarded more than 350,000 active users and supports over 80 languages. The platform executes financial operations across blockchains, with live integrations on Morpho, Kamino, Lido, Aave, Uniswap, and Raydium. An educational campaign backed by Amazon Web Services saw over 250,000 verified user completions.

The company targets 10 million active users in the next 6 to 12 months and plans native iOS and Android apps. That is a 28x user growth target in under a year. For a platform that describes itself as AI-powered financial execution, the bet is that users want an agent to do the blockchain legwork instead of clicking through DeFi protocols themselves. The 350,000 users it already has suggest the appetite exists, even if the revenue model is still being tested. Gulf fintech investors watching the DeFi space should note how fast a UAE platform can onboard users when it removes the manual steps.

04Defense pact
Makkah defense pact holds first committee talks
The Makkah defense pact held its first committee meeting in Istanbul with officials from Turkey, Saudi Arabia, and Pakistan. The talks covered defense capabilities, military interoperability, and joint defense production. Turkey said the pact is open to expansion, naming Egypt as a potential candidate and noting Bangladesh has reportedly been invited to join.

The pact represents a significant shift in Gulf security architecture. Saudi Arabia has traditionally relied on bilateral security arrangements, particularly with the US. A trilateral pact with Turkey and Pakistan signals a diversification of defense partnerships, with joint production as a core objective rather than just procurement.

For Gulf defense contractors and logistics providers, the pact opens a new channel for industrial cooperation. Joint defense production implies technology transfer, local assembly lines, and longer-term procurement cycles. Turkish defense firms like Baykar and Aselsan already have experience in co-production models, and Saudi Arabia's Vision 2030 explicitly targets 50% localization of defense spending. Gulf industrial companies with defense ambitions should map the Turkish and Pakistani supply chains now, before the first joint production tenders are announced.

05Battery storage
Saudi Group 1 battery projects get contractors
Contractors have been appointed for Saudi Arabia's Group 1 battery storage projects, which will provide four hours of storage equivalent to 8,000 megawatt-hours (MWh). The total investment is $1.16 billion. These projects are part of the Kingdom's wider push to integrate renewable energy into the grid, where storage is the missing link between solar generation and evening peak demand.

Saudi Arabia's Energy Ministry has been running a series of battery storage tenders under its National Renewable Energy Program. The Group 1 projects are the first tranche of a much larger storage pipeline. The four-hour duration is standard for utility-scale storage paired with solar, allowing daytime generation to be shifted to evening consumption.

The storage rollout matters for Gulf grid stability as renewable capacity scales. Saudi Arabia aims for 50% renewable generation by 2030, and without storage, that target is unachievable. For contractors and suppliers, the Group 1 award confirms the procurement model, the pricing precedent, and the technical specifications that will apply across subsequent tranches. Contractors with battery storage experience should expect accelerated tender cycles as the Kingdom pushes toward its 2030 renewable target.

The AI Race
​
OpenAI cuts Cursor's access
OpenAI is ending Cursor's direct model access on November 12, citing trust concerns and what it described as prior contract breaches by Musk companies. The decision follows SpaceX's acquisition of Cursor earlier in 2026, placing one of the most popular AI coding assistants inside Musk's orbit. Cursor now has 74 days to find alternative model providers, according to filings, in a move that is the latest salvo in the Musk-Altman feud, but the casualty is a tool developers actually use. Cursor has reportedly become the default AI coding assistant for many engineering teams, and OpenAI models are among the strongest available for code generation. Losing direct access means Cursor must route through other providers or build its own infrastructure, which could raise costs and degrade performance almost immediately. The wider AI race is now splitting into armed camps over model access, with developer tools caught in the crossfire. Gulf engineering teams that built workflows on Cursor should start parallel tests now, because migrating an AI-assisted codebase is not a weekend task.

Mubadala Capital is buying control of US freight broker Arrive Logistics. The firm runs a digital-first, asset-light model that scales with software. The deal fits Mubadala's pattern: buy infrastructure-adjacent platforms, not infrastructure itself. Global freight rates remain elevated on Hormuz disruption.
Derayah commits $65M to HOF Capital
Derayah Financial put SAR 244 million into HOF Capital's second strategic opportunities fund. The fund targets AI, enterprise software, and frontier tech. Derayah also plans its own AI fund, pending approval. The move marks a shift from passive fund-of-funds positions to direct mandates.

Saudi-Egyptian fund Asl to deploy $266M
