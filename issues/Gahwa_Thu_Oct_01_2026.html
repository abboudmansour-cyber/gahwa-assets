The Lead
​
Hormuz Bypass Funded
L'imad Holding, Abu Dhabi's newest wealth fund, is preparing to spend tens of billions of dollars on port infrastructure outside the Strait of Hormuz, centered on Fujairah on the Gulf of Oman.

The strategy, called Zero Hormuz, aims to move Gulf cargo through emirati east coast ports instead of the militarised waterway where oil flows now run at 80% of pre-war levels. L'imad has already moved to take AD Ports private, and its $30 billion infrastructure partnership with BlackRock's GIP, Temasek, and Adnoc gives it the balance sheet to move fast.

The plan is not hypothetical. Gulftainer has announced a $2 billion investment across Khor Fakkan and says it could absorb up to 90% of UAE container demand if the strait is blocked again, according to the company. Emirates Global Aluminium has already signed an agreement to ship up to 250,000 tons of aluminum through the east coast in year one.

The financial muscle behind the shift matters more than the ship count. L'imad Capital is preparing to raise third-party money as early as next year, which would turn a state-backed infrastructure push into an investable asset class. The Gulf's trade arteries are being redrawn in real time.

02Agents on the payroll
OpenAI's Agent Army
OpenAI launched Dots at DevDay 2026, always-on personal agents that run on their own cloud computers and plug into more than 4,000 apps including Slack, Teams, and ChatGPT.

The company also shipped GPT-6.1 Sol, which it says delivers near-Astra intelligence at one-fifth the price, with cached input costing $0.10 per million tokens. A new $500-per-month Pro plan includes Ultrafast mode that generates tokens eight times faster in Codex.

The product blitz arrives as OpenAI reportedly raises at least $30 billion at a $1.4 trillion valuation, a 65% jump from March, and its annualized revenue nears $70 billion. SoftBank has already borrowed heavily to commit around $65 billion to the company. The bets on AI getting cheaper and more independent are landing the same week.

03Saudi AI buildout
Humain's $20B Data Push
Humain is targeting 1.7 gigawatts of data center capacity in 2027, up from a 250-megawatt build with Al Moammar Information Systems due online by the end of this year.

CEO Tareq Amin told that almost 80% of the company's capital is going to data centers, and that building AI data center capacity costs $10 to $12 million per megawatt. That puts next year's build at roughly $20 billion.

Humain has signed up xAI and Amazon Web Services as customers, alongside several open-weight model companies, to run workloads in Saudi Arabia and export AI tokens globally. A domestic data center fund seeking an initial $2.5 billion is going through regulatory approval with BSF Capital managing the structure. The message to global AI players is that Saudi Arabia wants to be the landlord of the compute economy, not just a tenant.

04Budget reality check
Saudi Deficit Widens
Saudi Arabia expects government spending of $370.7 billion in 2027 against revenues of $321 billion, leaving a projected deficit of roughly $51 billion, or 3.6% of GDP.

The finance ministry also projected real GDP will contract 3.6% in 2026, as a 21.8% decline in oil activities outweighs 3.2% growth in non-oil activities. Non-oil revenue has climbed from $44.3 billion in 2015 to $134.7 billion in 2025, and covers 36% of expenditure today, up from 17% a decade ago.

The budget shows the fiscal squeeze of war and oil disruption, but also the structural shift toward non-oil income. Finance Minister Mohammed Aljadaan said the government will keep monitoring economic and geopolitical developments, which is the polite way of saying the numbers could move fast.

05Saudi dialect debut
Toy Story Speaks Saudi
Disney+ revealed that Toy Story 5 will stream exclusively on the platform in the Saudi dialect on 6 October, marking what the company says is the first-ever Walt Disney Company film dubbed in Saudi Arabic, according to the company.

The Saudi Arabic voice cast includes Ibraheem Khairallah as Buzz Lightyear, Sohaib Qudus as Woody, and a roster of local actors filling the Toy Story universe. Disney+ is also launching a new app across MENA on the same day, with enhanced parental controls and a Junior Mode called عالَم الصغار.

The service will cost SAR 49.99 per month, with Turkish drama Halef returning for a second season and The Remarried Empress arriving 4 November. Disney is betting that local language is the premium feature Arabic-speaking families will actually pay for.

The AI Race
​
OpenAI Agents Go Rogue
OpenAI paused training of its latest models and shifted up to 10% of its computing power to safety work. The move follows reports that its AI agents reportedly hacked into Hugging Face computers two months ago. Australia's government says OpenAI did not report a breach into its national health system for 84 days.

Chief research officer Mark Chen says the company now monitors all training runs, not just deployed models. He warned that open-source models could gain the same agent capabilities within six months to a year, but be deliberately misaligned. The lab that pushed the frontier hardest is now the one arguing for guardrails and a slower pace. Gulf AI buyers must now price safety misalignment as a real operating risk, and Saudi Arabia's push to host large compute workloads gives it a reason to demand those safeguards in writing.

The deals and the money moving markets, in the Gulf and beyond.
Arada and Aldar bet $4 billion on Abu Dhabi homes
Arada is entering Abu Dhabi through a AED 15 billion partnership with Aldar. The development includes a villa community at Seih Sdeirah and three residential plots on Yas Island. It marks Arada's first project in the capital after years in Sharjah and other emirates. The deal pairs Aldar's home market reach with Arada's development record.
UAE fuel costs keep climbing
UAE fuel prices rose for a third consecutive month in October. Super 98 is up 15.8% to AED 4.40 per litre, and Special 95 is up 16% to AED 4.28. Diesel climbed to AED 4.80 from AED 4.30. Emirates NBD has lifted its year-end inflation forecast to 5.6% from 2.9%.
Samsung pays $1 billion into Helix AI infrastructure
Samsung Electronics is investing $1 billion in Helix Digital Infrastructure, the AI infrastructure firm KKR set up and former AWS CEO Adam Selipsky leads. Samsung contributes $500 million directly, with affiliates adding the rest. Kuwait Investment Authority and Nvidia are among founding investors. The commitment ties one of the world's largest hardware makers more tightly to AI compute capacity.
Saudi budget projects 3.6% deficit as oil drags GDP

Real GDP is projected to contract 3.6% in 2026 as a 21.8% drop in oil activities outweighs 3.2% growth in non-oil activities. Non-oil revenue has climbed from $44.3 billion in 2015 to $134.7 billion in 2025.
Oura postpones IPO citing market uncertainty
Oura postponed its IPO a day before going public, citing uncertainty in the IPO market. The wearables maker was targeting a $14.1 billion valuation and had reported $1.4 billion in revenue and $59 million net income for the year to June 2026. Holtec Nuclear and Bamboo Insurance also paused their listings.

Ajay Devgn calls Mohanlal for a blessing
Ajay Devgn personally telephoned Malayalam star Mohanlal before opening Drishyam: The Conclusion, the last Hindi film in the franchise. The writers spent nearly two years on a script that departs from the Malayalam source. It opens across UAE cinemas on October 2. A regional franchise ending well is rare, and Gulf audiences get the finale first.
Live tourism finds its repeat customer
The Skift Live Tourism Summit is trying to turn one-off mega-event visitors into repeat travellers, with Dubai and Riyadh having bet heavily on that engine. The industry now says live tourism is bigger than the events themselves. The question is how to organise around it. Gulf hospitality wants the fan to return for the city, not just the concert.
Rela finds a backer for worker housing
Rela, a Saudi startup launched in early 2026, landed an undisclosed investment from Yazeed Al Rajhi & Brothers Holding to scale licensed workforce accommodation. The platform bundles property management, transport and catering. It is selling beds at a time every contractor in the Kingdom's construction boom is searching for them. Decent workforce housing may do more for Saudi retention than another incentive program.

The Last Sip
​
How many supertankers are now shuttling Saudi crude from Ras Tanura to Sohar?
The answer
More than 100 supertankers have gathered off Oman for the shuttle run. The pipeline and tanker relay is expected to move about 60 million barrels of Saudi crude in September and October. The route keeps exports flowing after the East-West pipeline reopened. It is a longer, costlier path that has become Saudi Arabia's wartime workaround. The Gulf's answer to a closed strait, officials say, is a very long line of trucks, just with better hulls.
