The Lead
​
Saudi skies shut
The US State Department approved a proposed $24.3 billion sale of 48 F-35 fighter jets to Saudi Arabia and formally notified Congress on September 17. The statutory review period runs about 30 days, and Congressional opposition would need a two-thirds majority in both chambers to override a presidential veto. The sale faces no immediate roadblock.

Why the F-35 matters Saudi Arabia's current air force includes over 200 F-15 variants, around 80 Tornado aircraft, and more than 70 Eurofighter Typhoons. The F-35 would add a stealth capability the kingdom has never fielded. The Israel precedent:: Israel operates a unique F-35 variant, the F-35I Adir, with about 50 aircraft and two squadrons, according to reports. A third squadron was signed in 2024 and a fourth approved in 2026, potentially reaching about 100 aircraft. Saudi Arabia would become the second regional F-35 operator, a shift in the military balance that Washington has long resisted. The UAE suspended its own F-35 talks in 2021 over China ties and technology safeguards. A successful sale would reshape the Gulf's air defense landscape for the next two decades.

02Iraq's currency reset
Iraq devalues dinar by 15%, officials say
Iraq devalued its currency by 15%, setting the official rate at IQD 1,520 per US dollar. The central bank buys dollars from the Finance Ministry at IQD 1,500 and sells to banks at IQD 1,510. This is Iraq's third currency reset in six years, and it comes as central bank holdings dropped about 17% in the first seven months of 2026 to USD 80.6 billion.

Prices are already moving On the street, the dollar jumped from IQD 1,598 on Tuesday to about IQD 1,685 by Wednesday morning before easing to IQD 1,670 by evening. Food staples rose by up to 15% on Thursday, with sugar up 15.4%, rice up 10.8%, and eggs up 10.7%. The prime minister postponed customs duties on live animals, chicken, and eggs to soften the blow.

Why it matters for the Gulf Iraq's oil exports have collapsed to 600,000 barrels a day, down from about 3.3 million before the conflict. That collapse is starving the state of revenue and forcing Baghdad to print money. For Gulf investors and contractors, a weaker dinar raises the cost of doing business in Iraq while making Iraqi imports cheaper, and the instability reinforces caution about the market. Iraq's currency reset is a warning that the country's fiscal position is deteriorating faster than official statements suggest.

03COFE Tech pre-IPO
Kuwait's COFE Tech targets 2029 IPO
COFE Tech secured new backing from Abu Dhabi-based Phoenix Venture Partners and KSH Investments at a $178 million valuation. The Kuwaiti coffee-tech platform, founded in 2018, serves more than 1,000 business clients across 3,000 outlets in Saudi Arabia, Kuwait, and the UAE. Its customers include Emirates Airlines, Etihad Airways, and Al-Futtaim Group.

The listing path COFE Tech aims for a public listing by 2029 and has closed its pre-IPO funding round. Steve Khayat, founder and CEO of Phoenix Venture Partners, has joined the board. PVP first backed COFE at pre-Series A stage in 2018, before the company rebranded from COFE App.

What it signals A Kuwaiti startup reaching a nine-figure valuation and planning an IPO is rare. The backing of a sovereign-linked UAE investor and the participation of large UAE corporates suggest the business is scaling beyond its home market. Gulf fintech activity continues to concentrate in the UAE and Saudi Arabia, but COFE Tech shows Kuwait can still produce a regional contender. COFE Tech's funding round is the clearest sign yet that Kuwait's startup scene can produce a regional IPO candidate.

04Gulf power link
UAE and Saudi plan electricity interconnection
A preliminary power-trading and grid-interconnection deal is now on the table between Saudi Arabia and the UAE, signed to assess the tie-up. The deal was signed in Riyadh by UAE Investment Minister Mohamed Alsuwaidi and Saudi Energy Minister Prince Abdulaziz bin Salman. It is part of a wider infrastructure integration package that also covers rail links.

A more resilient grid An interconnection would let both countries share reserve capacity and smooth demand peaks. It also strengthens the Gulf's energy security at a time when the Strait of Hormuz remains under pressure. The two largest Arab economies already cooperate on oil policy, but a shared power grid would tie their utilities closer than ever.

The bigger picture The agreement follows weeks of deepened Gulf energy ties. Oman and the UAE both announced major bilateral partnerships, and Abu Dhabi offered notable public support for Saudi Arabia amid the Houthi attacks, according to officials. Gulf states are quietly building cross-border infrastructure as a hedge against conflict. This marks the first concrete step toward a unified Gulf power market that operators should start planning around.

05Dubai airport line
Dubai airport express line tender opens
Dubai has tendered the study and design of a 55-kilometre express line linking Garhoud with Al-Maktoum International Airport in Dubai. The project is part of Dubai's expansion of Al-Maktoum as it prepares for greater passenger volumes. The lowest bidder for the study has emerged, though the contract has not yet been awarded. Dubai is moving ahead with Al-Maktoum expansion despite wider regional uncertainty.

The AI Race
​
A quieter AI bet
TypeSafe's non-text AI model Jev is now valued at $7.5 billion, just weeks after launching. The company claims Jev works significantly faster and uses far fewer tokens than a large language model. Investors are treating it as a credible challenge to the dominant architecture that has powered the AI boom so far.

The bet is that the next wave of AI may not speak in sentences at all. If the model works as claimed, it could undercut the cost of running huge language systems. That would pressure every lab that has spent billions on text-first models. The valuation alone suggests at least some investors are convinced. Gulf sovereign funds that own stakes in major AI labs will be watching to see if a cheaper, non-text model starts to rewrite the economics of the race.
Business
​
China wins Qatar offshore work
Chinese contractor COOEC won one of two main packages on QatarEnergy's Maydan Mahzam offshore oil field redevelopment. The project is part of Qatar's push to maintain output at its only onshore field. The second package remains open for bidding. Dukhan field facilities upgrade bids are also under review.
Kuwait's power pipeline
Kuwait's Al-Khairan independent water and power producer plant is estimated at $2 billion and awaits its contract award. The 500MW Al-Shagaya solar zone two project is also at award stage. Together they form the core of Kuwait's next generation build-out. The country is trying to refresh its grid while demand keeps climbing.
A Fujairah carbon pilot
A pilot plant in Fujairah aims to mineralise up to 4.5 tonnes of CO2 each day captured at a cement plant. The project is a joint venture between Omani firm 44.01 and Holcim's UAE subsidiary. It is one of the first operational carbon capture projects in the Gulf. The process locks carbon into rock rather than releasing it.
Tata's final Iveco bid
Tata Motors lifted its takeover offer for Iveco to $16.60 per share, calling it a final bid. Shareholders have tendered 28.4% of shares covered by the offer, which closes October 26. The deal would create a commercial vehicle group with annual revenue of $25.2 billion. Tata is pushing hard to close a transformative industrial acquisition in Europe.
Arabica coffee passes $3 per pound again
Arabica coffee prices crossed $3 per pound, the highest since early September. Brazil exported 3.93 million bags in September, up 20% on the year. El Niño fears for Brazil's 2027 crop are keeping prices elevated.

A catalyst fund with Gulf bets
Endeavor Catalyst closed its fifth fund at $320 million, bringing assets under management above $850 million. The fund has backed 437 companies across 44 markets since 2012, including MENA names like Blink, Nawy, Thndr, and Chari. It is the kind of money that follows founders rather than cities. Gulf startups gain another global backer that has already written regional cheques.
Coffee keeps brewing
Costa Coffee returned to an operating profit of £20 million in 2025 after two years of losses. Revenue grew 5% to £1.3 billion. The chain opened 50 new UK outlets and plans 50 more in 2027, betting that the world still runs on a better cup. A familiar brand on Gulf high streets is now standing on its own numbers again.

Mosaic Restaurant in Abu Dhabi turned ten by laying out 196 Lebanese dishes, from the usual hummus and moutabal to plates rarely seen outside Beirut. Why did a payout for an Asian Games cricket gold make waves this week?
The answer
Washington Sundar received Rs 7.5 million, roughly Dh 286,000, from Tamil Nadu Chief Minister C. Joseph Vijay for winning an Asian Games gold medal. The ceremony honoured medallists from the state, where cricket is less a sport than a climate condition. India's men's team defended its gold in a tournament many cricket boards treated as a warm-up event. The cash prize is the story, not the medal, and it shows how state governments are now bidding for sporting glory in cash terms. Tamil Nadu has decided cricket medals deserve Olympic money, warm-up tournament or not.
Which Gulf city just became the only Qatari name on a regional fintech list?
The answer
Doha, home to Dibsy, the payment infrastructure company that became the only Qatari firm on Forbes Middle East's 2026 Fintech 50 list. Founded in 2020, Dibsy now serves businesses across the Gulf from a base that was once better known for gas exports than payment rails. The listing is a milestone for a fintech sector that has remained tiny compared to Dubai or Riyadh. It means Qatar has at least one card on the table. A Qatari fintech on a Forbes list is like finding a falcon in a data centre; rare, and worth a look.
