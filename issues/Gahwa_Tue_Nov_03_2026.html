The Lead
​
EQT courts Gulf funds for AI deals
EQT, with $400 billion under management, is in talks with UAE and Saudi sovereign investors for joint AI and infrastructure investments. The firm plans to expand its Gulf AI footprint through EdgeConneX, which owns more than 106 data centres across 25 countries.

The Gulf AI push by the numbers Gulf states have announced $120 billion in AI investment plans, exceeding 5% of GDP per the IMF. Reportedly, Blackstone struck a $3 billion deal with Saudi Humain. Microsoft committed more than $10 billion to data centres across the UAE, Saudi Arabia, Qatar, and Kuwait through 2030. Qatar Investment Authority launched Qai, a $20 billion AI infrastructure partnership.

EQT opened its Abu Dhabi ADGM office in September. ADIA and Mubadala joined EQT's £9.5 billion bid for Intertek Group in June. Forty EQT portfolio companies already operate in the Gulf. The firm is betting the region's sovereign capital will fund the next phase of its AI buildout. The Gulf sovereigns are now the first call for any global manager raising a large AI infrastructure fund.

02Strait of Hormuz
US strikes Iran's Qeshm Island, reportedly
US forces struck Iran's Qeshm Island on Monday, the largest island in the Strait of Hormuz. The strike is the latest in a series of limited attacks Washington has secretly carried out in recent weeks, according to the Wall Street Journal, which reported the strike. Reportedly, a Houthi ballistic missile struck the Al-Saqiyah Health Center in Lahj province, Yemen, killing five people and wounding 15, including medical staff. The IRGC claimed it downed a US MQ-170 drone near Qeshm and destroyed an MQ-9 east of the strait. US Central Command dismissed those claims as false.

Peace talks gain pace Brent crude fell 8.22% to $104.13 a barrel on Monday. WTI dropped 7.78% to $96.02. Murban slid 11.29% to $105.3 as markets priced in a possible negotiated end to the eight-month war. Iranian President Masoud Pezeshkian said his government will pursue dialogue. The UK Maritime Trade Operations reported a vessel struck by an unknown projectile off Lima, Oman near the mouth of the strait. The ship took a hole on its starboard side but the crew is safe. The conflict is on Day 246. A deal would reopen the strait and reset freight and insurance costs for every Gulf shipper.

03Laptop to blame
Binance covers 90% of record heist
Binance replenished 90% of the $6.3 billion drained from hot wallets on October 29. The attackers compromised a DevOps engineer's laptop to access internal signing services. All top-10 assets, including BTC, ETH, USDT, and BNB, are now fully backed.

The replenishment came from a partial SAFU deployment, a portion of the Futures insurance fund, and corporate treasury. A new Proof of Reserves snapshot was taken October 31, with a zero-knowledge proof expected November 3. Withdrawals reopened November 1, and Binance is absorbing all losses.

Chainalysis attributes the theft to North Korean groups. About $2.8 billion in stablecoins have been frozen. The same signing-system vector was used in the Bitget loss of $350 million a month earlier. The largest heist in crypto history is now a test case for insurance fund design and exchange custody.

04Days to minutes
Abu Dhabi signs four OpenAI deals
Four agreements, one partnership: Abu Dhabi and OpenAI signed deals covering government transformation, energy, investment, and enterprise AI adoption. The deals were witnessed by Sheikh Khaled bin Mohamed bin Zayed Al Nahyan and Sam Altman.

ADNOC became the first energy company to deploy OpenAI frontier technology, using GPT models with in-country inference infrastructure in the UAE. Mubadala, Inception42, and OpenAI agreed to explore AI across investment analysis, opportunity identification, and risk assessment.

The Machine-Readable Government initiative aims to reduce routine regulatory assessment times from days to minutes, with human oversight retained for final decisions. It is part of the AED 13 billion Abu Dhabi Government Digital Strategy 2025 to 2027. The machine-readable government still has humans reading the final page.

05Energy security
Hormuz outage drains 1B barrels
Nearly 1 billion barrels of crude have been drained from global inventories since the Strait of Hormuz disruption began. This is per ADNOC Group CEO Sultan Al-Jaber at ADIPEC 2026. Diesel stocks in some markets are at their lowest seasonal level in 35 years. Logisitics made up less than 5% of the cost of a barrel before the disruption and now exceed 30%.

Some markets face an LNG supply gap of 600 billion cubic feet. Gulf ministers insist pipelines and railways will add redundancy but cannot replace the waterway. Thirty percent of a barrel's cost is now the journey, not the crude.
Source: Forbes Middle East

The AI Race
​
Aramco buys 49% of HUMAIN
Saudi Aramco is taking a 49% stake in HUMAIN, the national AI infrastructure company launched by PIF in May 2025. The deal brings about $2.6 billion in cash during 2026 plus AI, computing and digital assets. PIF keeps the controlling 51%. HUMAIN targets 3 gigawatts of AI data centre capacity by 2030.

The tie-up folds Aramco's balance sheet into Saudi Arabia's national AI cloud push. Aramco has achieved $11.3 billion in Technology Realized Value since 2023, a significant portion from AI. Its ALLaM model is already among the most powerful Arabic language models. Now oil cash is underwriting the kingdom's compute buildout. This gives Saudi Arabia a state-backed AI stack funded by energy revenue, a model the rest of the Gulf is watching closely.
Business
​
ADIA launches $1.4B aircraft leasing platform
ADIA, Avolon, and I Squared Capital are launching Constellation Aviation, an aircraft leasing platform seeded with a $1.4 billion portfolio. The first portfolio has 22 aircraft, about five years old on average, leased to 17 airlines in 15 countries. Avolon will be the exclusive aircraft manager. A $1.4 billion portfolio of 22 aircraft, which is the sovereign fund equivalent of buying a very large hangar.
AD Ports names Afif Al Yafei CEO
Afif Al Yafei has been appointed group CEO of AD Ports Group, succeeding Capt Mohamed Al Shamisi who led the company since 2014. Al Yafei joins from Taqa Transmission. AD Ports operates around 40 terminals in the UAE and has a presence in roughly 50 markets. Al Yafei moves from transmission to terminals, so the infrastructure now has one more switch.
Riyadh airport Terminal 6 bids compete
Contractors are competing for work on Terminal 6 at Riyadh airport. The terminal is planned to boost the airport's annual capacity by 40 million passengers. Riyadh airport’s Terminal 6 is still a drawing, but the bids are real.
UAE oil exports hit 3.94m bpd in June
UAE shipped 3.94 million barrels per day of crude and condensate in June, the highest monthly total in the past year. The March blockade had cut exports to a record low of 2.13 million bpd. The Habshan to Fujairah pipeline can now carry up to 1.5 million bpd.
