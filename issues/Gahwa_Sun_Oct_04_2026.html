The Lead

Aramco pipeline repairs hit 80% capacity
Aramco's East-West pipeline is running above 80 percent capacity at close to 6 million barrels a day of its 7 million barrel total, leaving 4.5 million barrels a day available for Red Sea export after refinery intake. The pipeline restarted on 22 September after attacks, according to officials, knocked out three of eleven pumping stations on 11 September. A full return is targeted at six to eight weeks.

The repair pace matters because the strait is still dangerous. Houthi attacks on the pipeline reportedly forced Saudi crude onto the longer, costlier route around the Arabian Peninsula through the Strait of Hormuz. Every day the pipeline runs below full capacity, Saudi Arabia sells its oil with extra freight and insurance costs attached.

Aramco also raised October LPG official selling prices by 9 to 11 percent. Propane now sits at $680 a tonne, up $55, and butane at $730 a tonne, up $70. The price rise signals that Aramco expects stronger demand for petrochemical feedstock even as the war continues. Saudi Arabia has also begun work on a crude pipeline to Duqm, Oman, a 1,000-kilometre route that would cost up to $7 billion. The pipeline's recovery pace and the new Oman route together show Riyadh is treating access to open water as a pricing weapon, not just a logistics problem.

02Red Sea security
Saudi readies push to retake Bab al-Mandab
Saudi Arabia is preparing an offensive to retake the Bab al-Mandab strait from Houthi forces, with the operation possibly beginning within weeks, according to officials. Yemeni ground forces would lead the push with Saudi air support overhead. The strait controls access to the Red Sea, a route that carries Saudi oil to European and global markets.

Two options sit on the table. The first is a limited campaign focused only on the strait itself. The second is a wider simultaneous push across five Yemeni provinces, Al Bayda, Marib, Taiz and Al Jawf, that could involve more than 100,000 Yemeni troops. According to reports, Pakistan has flown four cargo planes into Aden loaded with air-defence systems, light artillery and drones. Some 30,000 to 40,000 Pakistani troops are stationed inside Saudi Arabia. Houthi forces claim roughly 192 Saudi airstrikes and missile attacks in recent days, and 1,350 strikes since the escalation began, per spokesman Yahya Saree. A Houthi missile and drone attack reportedly hit an Aramco facility in Riyadh over the weekend. A separate strike on the Taibah distribution station in Madinah took one power transformer offline, according to officials. Saudi forces intercepted five ballistic missiles and four drones aimed at Khamis Mushait and Jazan, officials say.

Aramco's East-West pipeline is running above 80 percent capacity at close to 6. Retaking the strait would remove the Houthi chokepoint and let Saudi crude sail directly to European buyers, cutting both transport time and war insurance premiums on every shipment.

03Egypt fintech IPO
MNT-Halan Egypt arm opens $1bn IPO
MNT-Halan's Egyptian arm opened an IPO of 320 million shares, representing 20 percent of the company, at a valuation of up to $1 billion. The offering is fully secondary and split between institutional and retail investors. Citi and EFG Hermes are joint global coordinators.

The parent company will put up to EGP 4 billion into the listing via a closed capital increase at the offer price. Another 24.3 million shares go to senior management. MNT's active customer base is about 1.9 million, nearly double rival Valu's 955,000. Default rates sit below 1.5 percent on microfinance and 3.5 to 4 percent on consumer finance.

Just over half of MNT's customers used more than one Halan service as of end-June, and those customers are worth about 64 percent more in lifetime value. The listing will test whether the Egyptian market can absorb a billion-dollar fintech flotation at a time when foreigners are net sellers of Egyptian stocks. Local investors made up 90.5 percent of value traded in the third quarter. A successful MNT listing would set a benchmark for Egyptian fintech valuations and tell founders whether the local market can now price a unicorn without foreign buyers in the room.

04Rolls-Royce Saudi engines
Aramco venture partners with Rolls-Royce on engines
Saudi Engines Manufacturing Company, known as Makeen, an industrial venture backed by Saudi Aramco, partnered with UK firm Rolls-Royce to manufacture high-speed engines in Saudi Arabia. The agreement is the first individual licence arrangement of its kind in the kingdom. It targets marine and naval applications, including high-speed ferries.

Makeen is one of the quiet ways Aramco is trying to industrialise the kingdom beyond oil. High-speed engines power ferries, patrol boats and offshore support vessels, all of which the Gulf needs in volume. The licence gives Makeen the manufacturing know-how rather than just the right to assemble imported parts.

The marine angle matters because Gulf shipping is expanding as war insurance pushes more cargo onto regional routes. Ferries and support vessels that run on Rolls-Royce engines built in Dammam would be cheaper to maintain than imported alternatives, and spare parts would not need to travel through a war zone to reach the fleet. Ferries and patrol boats in Dammam will soon run on engines whose spare parts are a drive away, not a shipping lane.

05Maven Robotics
Maven Robotics raises $100m with UAE backing
Maven Robotics raised a $100 million Series A round that pulled in UAE investors Shorooq and Presight, a G42 company, alongside RoboStrategy, LocalGlobe, Vine Ventures and XTX Ventures. The Santa Clara-based firm was founded in 2024 by Hamza Derbas. Its robots run up to 16 hours a day with reliability above 99 percent.

Maven plans to build 250 third-generation machines for mixed-case palletising and tote handling, a market it estimates at $80 billion in addressable demand. The robots do the dull, physically demanding work of stacking boxes and moving totes in warehouses. That is the least glamorous corner of the robot boom and arguably the one with the clearest return on investment.

The UAE connection matters. Shorooq and Presight are putting Gulf capital into a Californian robotics firm with plans to scale globally. The deal signals that Gulf investors, who have been late to the robot wave, are now buying in at the Series A stage rather than waiting for proven exits. The $80 billion palletising market is the least flashy part of the robotics boom, and Gulf capital has now decided that stacking boxes well is worth a $100 million bet.

The AI Race
​
OpenAI cuts prompts to four parts
OpenAI's new model guide prescribes a four-part prompt recipe: the result you want, who it is for, context and limits, and what counts as done. An OpenAI developer says overloading instructions drags results down. The guide is aimed at users who stuff prompts with unnecessary detail and then blame the model when the output suffers.

The recipe is a simple structure that works across most large language models, not just OpenAI's. The four parts force the user to define scope before generating, which saves iterations. Anthropic has also released a free course called Claude Academy with 13 lessons on AI capabilities and limitations.

For Gulf businesses deploying AI tools, the prompt recipe is the difference between a model that produces useful work and one that produces plausible-sounding nonsense the team wastes hours fixing. Marketing teams, in particular, can adopt the recipe as a house standard. Anthropic's thirteen lessons and OpenAI's four parts both teach the same thing: say what done looks like.

OpenPayd targets Nasdaq listing for US expansion
Payments firm OpenPayd aims for a year-end Nasdaq listing to fund its US launch by April 2027. CEO Iana Dimitrova said the company is considering acquisitions to add licences and technology. OpenPayd provides payments and banking-as-a-service infrastructure to fintech and crypto firms. A Nasdaq listing would give OpenPayd the capital to enter the US market, where payments licences are expensive and slow to obtain state by state.
Tadawul market orders can now execute across five ticks
Tadawul's new market order rules took effect, allowing market orders to execute across multiple price levels up to five ticks from the best price. The rule applies to the main market and Nomu but excludes derivatives. The change is designed to improve order execution for retail investors. The old rules limited market orders to the best available price level.
Qatar's Aligator raises $1.2m seed for AI agents
Qatar-based agentic AI startup Aligator raised a $1.2 million seed round led by Qatar Development Bank and Next Ventures, a Media City Qatar fund. The startup develops autonomous AI agents for the PR industry. It was founded in 2025 by Ali Abbas, with a team of Qatar Foundation alumni from Carnegie Mellon Qatar and Texas A&M Qatar. The funding will expand product, engineering and go-to-market teams, and support GCC and MENA expansion.
Crypto job postings triple while applications fall
Crypto job postings tripled to over 1,200 in September, but applications fell over the same period. Finance, engineering and trading led hiring demand. Bitcoin, Ethereum and Solana were the most requested blockchain skills. More crypto firms are hiring, but fewer people are applying, which suggests workers are staying put or leaving the industry.

Rashed Nawaf and Mishari Nawaf reportedly won Qatar's first-ever Asian Games padel gold at Aichi-Nagoya 2026. The brothers beat a Malaysian pair in the final. The win is a first for Qatari padel and the Gulf region at this level. Padel has grown faster in the Gulf than almost anywhere else in the world. Sole DXB returns to Dubai December 11-13
Sole DXB returns to Dubai Design District from December 11 to 13, 2026, bringing back the city's flagship streetwear and music festival. The event has become Dubai's most influential youth culture gathering since it launched. Local streetwear brands use Sole DXB as their launchpad. For Gulf streetwear labels and music acts, the December dates are the deadline to have new collections and new material ready.

Emirati fine dining turns machboos into arancini
A wave of Emirati fine-dining restaurants is reshaping traditional dishes like machboos into formats such as arancini, the Italian fried rice balls. The trend is playing out across Sharjah, Dubai and Abu Dhabi. Chefs are keeping the spicing and the core ingredients but changing the presentation. The move from home-style machboos to restaurant-friendly arancini is the same arc Italian regional cooking took a generation ago. When a cuisine starts treating its own people as the primary audience, it is becoming a culture, not a costume.
