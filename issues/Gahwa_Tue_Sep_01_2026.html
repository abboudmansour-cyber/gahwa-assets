The Lead
​
Iran blacklisted 45 tankers, and the Gulf's workaround just became the next chokepoint.
The blacklist threatens fines, detention, or cargo confiscation for any ship conducting transfers. Tracking data shows 20 simultaneous transfers across the Gulf of Oman on August 25 alone, up from roughly four combined in mid-July.

Shuttle-related STS liftings doubled to 3 million barrels per day in June, up from 1.5 million in May and roughly 500,000 barrels per day in 2025. Reported war-risk rates for Hormuz reached 7.5 to 12.5 percent of vessel value in mid-August. Estimated crude and petroleum flows through the strait averaged just 4.9 million barrels per day in the second quarter, against 21.6 million before the conflict.

The workaround is becoming the target. At least three Indian refiners and one global energy major now plan to avoid Iran-blacklisted vessels, including for STS transfers. Kuwait is in talks to route crude through Fujairah, Saudi Arabia, and Oman instead. Adnoc has chartered around 15 crude carriers this year and committed roughly $2.7 billion to vessel acquisitions in 2026.

The longer-term fix is concrete. Construction on a new West-East pipeline that will double crude export capacity from Fujairah is accelerating and set to be operational next year. Fujairah's oil storage capacity is already at almost 18 million cubic meters, with land reclaimed for further build-out by 2028. The Gulf is not abandoning the sea route so much as building a parallel one. The blacklist will slow cargoes and raise costs now, but every barrel the pipeline moves next year is a barrel Iran can no longer touch.

02Arada's Damascus bet
Arada's $7B Damascus Project
UAE real estate developer Arada signed a $7 billion deal with the Syrian Sovereign Fund to build a new district in Damascus covering 4 million square meters. The project includes 11,000 residential units, hotels, healthcare facilities, educational institutions, offices, commercial space, and recreational areas. It marks Arada's first entry into Syria.

The signing took place in Damascus between Arada CEO Ahmed al-Khushaybi and Mohammed al-Khayyat, CEO of the sovereign fund's real estate arm. For Arada, the wager is that Syria's reconstruction market will reward early movers with prime land and government backing. For Syria, the deal is a signal that Gulf capital is ready to rebuild beyond humanitarian aid.

The risk is not on paper but on the ground. Syria remains unstable, with Israeli forces advancing in Daraa and the country's institutions still forming. A $7 billion commitment makes Arada the largest foreign developer in post-war Syria. Should the security situation hold, the company gains a first-mover position in a market with almost no competition. Should it not, the project becomes a long-term claim on a future that may take years to arrive. A $7 billion commitment makes Arada the largest foreign developer in post-war Syria, a first-mover position in a market with almost no competition.

03OpenAI-Cursor split
OpenAI Cuts Off Cursor Access
OpenAI will end access to its models on Cursor by November 12, following SpaceX's acquisition of the coding platform. OpenAI cited contract violations by Elon Musk's companies as the reason. Cursor's co-founder said OpenAI models serve about 5 percent of the platform's traffic.

The split is the latest in a series of frontier-lab blockades. Anthropic cut off nearly all of Windsurf's first-party Claude capacity in June 2025, blocked xAI in January, and previously restricted OpenAI's API access over distillation claims. Musk admitted under oath earlier this year that xAI violated OpenAI's terms of service.

The practical effect for developers is another migration. Replit offered to fund transitions for businesses looking for an independent multi-model alternative. Cursor must either push users toward other models or survive on its own. What was once an open market for AI model access is now a set of walled gardens with border guards.

ePointZero in Africa
ePointZero Buys African Power
ePointZero, a subsidiary of 2PointZero Group, plans to acquire a 90 percent stake in Azura Power, a pan-African independent power producer with 752 megawatts of operating capacity across Nigeria, Senegal, and Mozambique. The deal gives the UAE firm operational control of power generation assets that align with its clean energy portfolio in emerging markets.

The acquisition extends a pattern: Gulf capital buying operational energy assets in Africa rather than building from scratch. Azura Power's plants are already producing revenue, which means the deal is about cash flow and market access rather than project risk.

For Gulf operators, the signal is that Africa remains a priority geography despite the war-era pullback in capital flows. A 752-megawatt platform is a meaningful base. The next question is whether ePointZero layers on solar and storage at those sites or simply runs the existing gas and hydro assets for yield.

04Clean energy delayed
Masdar's $5B Solar-Battery Bet
Abu Dhabi's Masdar closed a $5 billion financing package for a 5.2-gigawatt solar plant and 19-gigawatt-hour battery system, raised from 13 banks across the UAE, France, the UK, Germany, China, and Japan. The deal is the largest clean energy financing in the Gulf since the war began.

The war is reshaping the supply chain behind it. Rystad Energy data shows UAE solar module imports collapsed to 160 megawatts in March from 767 megawatts a month earlier. Saudi Arabia dropped from 704 to 80 megawatts, and Oman recorded none. Shipping a standard container from Shanghai to the Gulf cost $980 before the war and $4,131 by mid-May.

The projects are still getting financed, but delivery is slipping. Rystad estimates a net delay of three to 12 months across the region's active renewables pipeline. Masdar is pushing toward a 100-gigawatt global portfolio by 2030 and deployed $15 billion in 2025 alone. The $5 billion raise shows the money is still there. The delay shows the hardware is not. The AI Race
​
Humain Becomes the Gulf's AI Landlord
Saudi AI company Humain anchored more than $15 billion in launches at LEAP on the first day. That already passes last year's full-event total of $14.9 billion. AMD and Humain expanded a partnership for a 250-megawatt first phase of a one-gigawatt AI data center, worth up to $10 billion with Cisco. STC will host Humain data centers. Together AI signed a 250-megawatt deal worth over $5 billion. Amazon Web Services will add up to 50 megawatts of Humain capacity by 2028. Adobe became Humain's first foreign tenant.

The compute is not the point. The tenants are. Humain also unveiled an AI laptop built with Qualcomm, the Horizon Ultra, reaching enterprise buyers on September 20. Qualcomm is opening at Humain's Majdoul Tower in Riyadh. Every major hyperscaler now has a Riyadh address. The shift pressures UAE operators to match Saudi compute or rent from it. The Kingdom has decided that in AI, the smart money is in the buildings, not the brains. UAE operators must now match Saudi compute or find themselves renting capacity from the Kingdom.
