The Lead
​
​
Saudi Arabia's war budget just ran headfirst into a collapsing oil price, and the math no longer works.
Non-oil GDP growth hit just 0.6% in the second quarter, a reading so weak it has not been seen outside a pandemic.

The budget hole is widening on both sides at once. First-quarter spending jumped 20% year-on-year with capital expenditure up 56%, producing what officials say was the largest nominal quarterly deficit on record at SAR 126 billion. Oil revenues rose 22% in the second quarter and narrowed the shortfall to SAR 34 billion, but the Brent price that underpinned that recovery has already slipped below $80. EFG Hermes projects the full-year fiscal deficit will barely narrow to 5.6% of GDP, against a budgeted target of 2.3%.

The government replaced every riyal of the first-half shortfall with debt rather than drawing on reserves. With crude exports down to about 4.2 million barrels a day in July and six Bahri supertankers rerouted around Africa at a 25-day penalty per voyage, the export volume that could close the gap is locked behind a strait that diplomacy has yet to reopen.
Reassess any assumption that Vision 2030 spending can accelerate this year; the non-oil slowdown and oil price drop together point to a leaner second half.

GCC & SAUDI
DataVolt's Non-Recourse Bet on Neom
DataVolt expects to break ground on its 1.5-gigawatt AI data centre campus at Oxagon within three months, a project where Neom supplies the land and the grid while DataVolt brings the capital. The firm intends to wrap every Saudi facility under construction into standalone, non-recourse project finance within six months.

The model mirrors what DataVolt already pulled off in Tashkent, where it closed up to $150 million of 12-year non-recourse debt for a $250 million data centre in June, pulling in the EBRD, DEG, Proparco, and the Opec Fund. That structure keeps the parent company's balance sheet clean and lets each asset sink or swim on its own contracts.

DataVolt has committed $5 billion to the Kingdom. A Riyadh East data centre worth $500 million is in the pipeline alongside the Oxagon campus, whose first phase is due by 2028. At a moment when Saudi fiscal headlines are flashing red, a developer putting pure project finance behind gigawatt-scale infrastructure is betting that data halls are a safer sovereign risk than oil. Track whether the non-recourse structure becomes a template for other Saudi gigaprojects seeking private capital without sovereign guarantees.

GCC & SAUDI
Abu Dhabi Stocks Rise, Dubai Sinks on War Fears
Dubai's DFM General Index fell 2.7% in July, with five of eight sectors declining, while Abu Dhabi closed up 1.1%, the only major GCC market to post a meaningful monthly gain. Five hours' drive separates the two exchanges; a war and a tourism slump explain the divergence.

Talabat led the Dubai slide, its share price dropping 14.6% after Uber agreed to acquire Delivery Hero. The regional conflict is weighing on visitor footfall, and Dubai public transport ridership data confirms the chill: 348.1 million trips in the first half, down 11.9% year-on-year. In Abu Dhabi, large-cap financials gained 2.2% for the month and telecoms added 5.7%, insulating the index from consumer-facing weakness.

Dubai now trades at 9.8 times forward earnings, a 14.6% discount to its February peak, and the UAE is the cheapest market in MENA on an equity risk premium basis at 6.55%. The price is set for a recovery that depends, according to officials, on a strait that remains closed. Watch for a rapid rotation into Dubai consumer and logistics names the moment a Hormuz ceasefire is confirmed; the valuation gap is wide.

MACRO & GEO
Hormuz Talks Advance, But Tehran Denies Negotiating
A Hormuz deal could be sealed today or tomorrow, the administration says, even as Iran's foreign ministry publicly denied it is in talks with Washington at all. Prepare for a sharp oil-price reversal the moment a deal is confirmed; Brent has already swung 5% on mere headlines.

PRODUCT
UAE Makes Federal Payments Run on Its Own Rails
The Ministry of Finance has now adopted the central bank's instant payment platform Aani and the domestic card scheme Jaywan as official collection channels for every federal service fee and fine. From today, the government's own payments will no longer touch a global card network. If you process federal payments or sell to anyone who does, Aani and Jaywan acceptance is now a hard technical requirement, not a roadmap item.

FINTECH & CRYPTO
Tokenized Equity Surges, Driven by One Zero-Fee Token
Tokenized equity trading hit a record $11.3 billion in July, a 288% leap month-on-month, but the headline number is a one-venue story. Binance's QQQB token, which tracks the Invesco QQQ ETF, accounted for $9.27 billion, or 82% of the total, after the exchange cut maker fees to zero through August 31 and launched a VIP volume multiplier that counts eligible trades at three times their actual value.

Strip out QQQB and the sector contracted 30%: xStocks fell from $1.55 billion to $335 million, Ondo recorded $792 million, Backpack $479 million. The underlying Invesco QQQ ETF actually dropped 6.6% in July, so the token was not rallying on fundamentals.

Meanwhile, BlackRock's final prospectus for its on-chain government money market fund names Tempo as the supported blockchain, with the fund designed to qualify as an eligible reserve asset for GENIUS Act stablecoin issuers. The plumbing of tokenized Treasuries is being laid even while the trading volume is synthetic. Distinguish between genuine tokenized equity demand and exchange-incentivized volume when evaluating this space; the QQQB surge is a promotion, not a trend.

The AI Race
The global race, landed on the Gulf.
Alibaba Undercuts Frontier AI Pricing
Alibaba released Qwen 3.8 Max, a 2.4-trillion-parameter model handling text, images, and video, priced at $2 per million input tokens and $6 per million output tokens, roughly one-eighth the cost of Anthropic's Claude Fable 5. The weights will be published on Hugging Face within a week, a first for a model of this class, while independent benchmarks split between unusable and within four points of the open-weight leader. A new open-weight flagship every eight days from China resets procurement leverage for Gulf cloud buyers overnight.
​For Gulf cloud regions locked into single-vendor contracts, the value just shifted from the model itself to the data center and integration layer that runs it.

Gaia Lands in Saint-Tropez
Chef Izu Ani's Gaia, the Dubai-born Greek restaurant, opened a new location at La Tartane Hotel in Saint-Tropez, adding a French Riviera address to a portfolio that already includes London, Monaco, and Miami. The expansion map looks like a private banker's summer itinerary and signals that a Gulf-born hospitality brand can export more than a franchise manual. For a Gulf operator watching margins at home, the export play is the margin expansion play.
