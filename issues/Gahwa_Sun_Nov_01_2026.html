The Lead
​
Houthis hit refineries
The Houthi movement claimed six new operations on Saturday, including missile strikes on Saudi Arabia's Ras Tanura and Rabigh refineries, Aramco's Abqaiq facility, Riyadh's King Khalid International Airport, and a drone strike on Dammam's King Fahd International Airport, with an attack in Najran causing casualties. Reportedly, the Houthi attacks landed as a Qatari delegation visited Tehran for mediation. The UAE urged "wisdom and political solutions," and Oman called for a Gulf security pact with Iran and Iraq. Saudi warplanes carried out 93 airstrikes and missile attacks in 24 hours, per Houthi spokesman Yahya Saree, bringing the total to roughly 3,700 Saudi strikes since the escalation began. The same week that OPEC+ agreed in principle to keep December output targets steady, the strikes reportedly reconfirm that security risk, not production policy, is the main variable for Gulf energy flows. Every new attack reportedly forces buyers to price in disruption. Saudi exports averaged 6 million barrels a day in October, and the East-West pipeline has recovered to 5.8 million barrels a day of its 7 million capacity. Reportedly, the diplomatic push in Doha and Tehran is the one channel that could lower that risk; until it produces something, refinery and airport security stay at war footing.

02Mortgages go quiet
Public sector drives Saudi credit growth
Saudi bank lending to public-sector enterprises jumped 21.5% year-on-year to SAR 285.1 billion in September. That is nearly three times the 7.3% rise in total bank credit, which reached SAR 3.5 trillion, per Sama's monthly bulletin. Banks' government bond holdings climbed to SAR 678.1 billion from SAR 643.1 billion a year earlier.

Consumer signals cooled. New residential mortgage lending fell 5.7% month-on-month to SAR 5.5 billion. Settled letters of credit for private-sector imports dropped 14.4% month-on-month and 18.2% year-on-year to SAR 12.3 billion. Personal loans remain the biggest category at SAR 1.48 trillion.

What the state-led growth means Net foreign assets fell to SAR 1.45 trillion in September from SAR 1.52 trillion in August. Broad money supply edged down 0.2% to SAR 3.32 trillion. The public sector is absorbing credit while private importers and homebuyers pull back, a pattern that concentrates growth where the government is already spending. A bank can ride the public-sector wave only so long as the state keeps borrowing. The September bond holdings show the state is still the busiest client in the room.

03Doha mediation
Iran negotiators in Doha for talks
Iranian negotiators are in Doha for Qatar-mediated talks to end the US-Iran war, with Tehran welcoming Washington's latest diplomatic outreach. The talks follow US Secretary of State Marco Rubio's statement that diplomatic contacts with Iran "have actually never stopped.

Why Doha matters Qatar has hosted quiet channels between the US and Iran before, and its role as a mediator is reinforced by its gas relationships with both sides. With the Riyadh and Dammam airports hit in the last 48 hours, the mediation track is the only active route to de-escalation on the table. Both sides are sending negotiators while missiles are still in the air, which is what a real off-ramp reportedly looks like when neither can afford to stop but both want to be seen trying.

04Fuel prices
UAE petrol prices surprise with November drop
UAE November fuel prices fell across the board. Super 98 fell 5.5% to Dh4.16 per litre from Dh4.40, Special 95 fell 5.6% to Dh4.04, and E-Plus 91 fell 5.7% to Dh3.97. Diesel dropped 1% to Dh4.75. Prices remain up roughly 80% versus February.

The drop lands while Brent settled up 2.4% at $117.64 after touching $118. January futures rose 2.37% to $113.43, and WTI gained 2.08% to $104.12. Both benchmarks posted monthly spikes around 15% for Brent and 12% for WTI, the highest since April.

What the gap between retail and crude says The UAE fuel price committee sets rates monthly based on global averages, so a single down month does not break the war premium. The bigger signal is that retail prices are adjusting while war risk remains priced into futures. For logistics and transport operators, the November break is real but narrow. A Dh0.24 per litre cut on Super 98 is enough for a small fleet operator to shave fuel cost by a few percentage points. But no one should model another cut next month.

05Fund close
BY Venture closes $60m AI fund
BY Venture Partners closed Fund III at $60 million, taking its total assets under management past $200 million. It was anchored by Dubai Future District Fund with participation from MultiLane, Transmed Capital, and Idriss Family Office. The fund targets AI-native startups at Pre-Seed to Series A, investing up to $1 million per company.

The firm has already deployed 15 investments out of a planned 40-company portfolio, including PQ Shield, Cosyne, Isometric, Huspy, and Invisible. About two-thirds of capital goes outside MENA to North America and Europe, across more than 16 markets. The founding partners have secured a mandate to manage the Lebanese Angel Investor Network. They committed to reinvest 50% of carried interest into Lebanon's startup ecosystem.

Why the extra $60 million matters BY Venture's earlier funds produced four unicorns. This close lands the same week that McKinsey Gulf data shows 84% of organisations use AI in at least one business function, up from 62% in 2023. For regional founders, a $60 million fund writing first checks up to $1 million is a clear signal that Gulf capital is swinging behind AI-native plays. The plan to invest up to $1 million per company just got 15 smaller reminders of itself.

The AI Race
​
AI agents force earnings reassurance
Companies have spent weeks reassuring investors that Meta's Muse and other AI agents are more opportunity than threat. Muse has not yet disrupted the businesses analysts expected it to upend. The real pressure is on how executives frame AI in earnings calls.

Investors are asking the same question across every sector: will an AI agent replace the service you sell? Earnings calls now carry an AI-assurance paragraph the way they used to carry a macro-risk paragraph. The question is shifting from what AI can do to what it might undo. Gulf-listed companies face the same conversation as local AI adoption jumps, with the risk that those with no AI story lose the equity multiple.

Arada raises $400m in UAE's first real estate sukuk since conflict began
Arada raised $400 million through a five-year sukuk at 8.875%. It is the first debt issuance by a UAE real estate developer since the regional conflict began in February. Orders exceeded $700 million, nearly twice the issue size at peak. The sukuk is rated BB- by Fitch and B1 by Moody's. Fitch said BB-, Moody's said B1, and investors said yes to $700 million in orders.
Turkey appoints Muhsin Atci as market regulator chief
President Erdogan appointed Muhsin Atci, head of the tax inspection board, as chief of the country's market regulator. The appointment responds to an investment fund crisis that has rocked Turkish financial markets. The change signals Ankara is trying to restore confidence in its capital markets. Atci brings a tax-enforcement background to a role that now needs stability more than anything else.
Unitree posts $100M Q3 revenue, profit falls on research spending
Unitree's first earnings report showed $100 million in Q3 revenue, up 70% year-on-year. Profit fell 17% and missed analyst expectations on higher research spending. The robotics firm is choosing growth over margins at this stage. That is a familiar trade-off for Gulf tech investors who have seen the same pattern in regional startups. Research spending did to profit what the robots have not yet learned to do: it went in the opposite direction.
Bahrain opens consultancy bids for Khalifa City electricity network
Bahrain has opened consultancy bids for the Khalifa City electricity network. The project will serve 3,000 housing units. It is part of the kingdom's steady push to expand housing and utility infrastructure. The bids are an early step in a project that will eventually involve contractors and equipment suppliers across the region. The bids are for electricity, but the work starts with paper.
