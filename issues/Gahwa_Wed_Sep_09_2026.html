The Lead
​
Houthi missiles reportedly hit four Saudi cities and wounded 73 civilians, and Brent is now within two dollars of triple digits.
Saudi Arabia's Ministry of Energy confirmed the attacks caused fires at several energy sector facilities, according to the ministry. Coalition spokesman Major General Turki Al Malki said the civilian and economic sites were targeted directly. The attack is the third major strike on Jazan in about six weeks, following earlier hits on 27 July and 9 August, according to reports.

The Jazan strike carries particular weight because the city hosts a 400,000 barrel-per-day refinery whose terminal already paused shipments in August. Brent crude is now trading near $99 a barrel, within two dollars of triple digits, as traders build in a higher risk premium. Goldman Sachs has already raised its oil price forecasts assuming shipping disruptions persist into 2027. The Houthis reportedly said they launched dozens of ballistic missiles and drones at Saudi targets in response to more than 120 strikes by Riyadh on Yemen in the past three days.

Why the refinery matters It is one of Saudi Arabia's largest refining assets and the anchor of the kingdom's economic development push in the southwest. The latest hit reportedly suggests the Houthis can strike repeatedly at the same infrastructure, after its terminal was already disrupted in August. The wider war now surrounds Saudi Arabia on two fronts. US forces have destroyed five Iranian oil carriers in the Hormuz standoff, and the Houthis are striking from the south while Iran threatens US assets across the region. For Gulf businesses, this means every energy cost assumption, shipping schedule, and insurance line now carries a war premium that was not there six months ago. The implication for Gulf operators is that the war has now reached Saudi Arabia's energy infrastructure in a sustained pattern, and the risk premium in oil prices and insurance rates will not fade quickly.

02LIV Golf files Chapter 11
LIV Golf bankrupt, players owed $64m
LIV Golf filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy protection on Tuesday, revealing $500 million to $1 billion in liabilities and assets valued between $100 million and $500 million. The filing caps a four-year experiment in sovereign wealth-funded golf. BC Partners is evaluating an acquisition of LIV's assets at $300 million, within a $250-350 million asking price. The Public Investment Fund, which launched LIV in 2022 with a multibillion-dollar investment, decided in May to pull funding after four years.

The liabilities include money owed to 27 players totaling $64.2 million, with Jon Rahm owed $7.4 million, the largest single creditor claim among the athletes. Bryson DeChambeau is owed $5.8 million, Dustin Johnson $5.5 million, and Cameron Smith $4.8 million, per USA Today. PIF extended under $100 million in debtor-in-possession financing this month, described as the fund's last contribution to the league.

How the deal unwinds The reorganized company is expected to be majority owned by the players themselves, a structure that would end the sovereign wealth fund's direct ownership. For the Gulf, the bankruptcy is a clean ending to a venture that spent billions trying to compete with the PGA Tour and ended with a fire sale of its assets at a fraction of the original investment. The outcome leaves the players holding a league they will now own themselves, and it marks the end of PIF's grandest sports bet outside traditional tournament sponsorship.

03L'imad takes AD Ports private
L'imad locks AD Ports buyout for October
L'imad's AED 7.8 billion buyback of AD Ports Group's remaining minority stake has cleared all conditions, and subsidiary ADQ has locked a settlement date no later than October 9. The offer covers the 24.58% of AD Ports that L'imad does not already own, and minority shareholders have until 3pm on September 15 to tender. This is the second major ADX-listed asset L'imad has moved to delist this year, after Abu Dhabi Power Corporation squeezed out Taqa's remaining minority shareholders earlier this month.

The price is the story. AD Ports raised AED 4 billion in fresh equity when it listed in 2022, and L'imad is now paying almost twice that to buy back nearly the same stake. AD Ports revenue rose from AED 3.9 billion in 2021 to AED 20.8 billion in 2025, but net debt stood at AED 22.7 billion at the end of the second quarter. Fixed-asset and investment commitments totaled close to AED 11 billion.

Why this matters for the UAE market L'imad also owns around 85% of Modon and about 40% of PureHealth, which analysts at Arqaam Capital flag as the next test cases for take-private decisions. The pattern is clear: Abu Dhabi's sovereign investment entities are pulling listed assets back under full state ownership when the public market no longer serves their capital needs. The take-private trend leaves fewer liquid assets for outside investors on ADX and concentrates more of the UAE economy under direct sovereign ownership.

04Keep Converting pre-seed
Keep Converting raises $2m pre-seed
UAE-based Keep Converting raised $2 million in a pre-seed round led by Nuwa Capital and COTU Ventures. The startup emerged from stealth in 2025, founded by Mohammad El Mougi and Manuel Prinz. Its platform claims an average 64% conversion-rate lift across active client deployments in the US, Europe, and the Gulf.

Keep Converting is pre-integrated with Shopify, WooCommerce, Adobe Commerce, Zid, and Salla, which means it slots directly into the ecommerce stack of Gulf merchants without custom integration work. The company will use the funding to onboard more merchants and expand its engineering team. That 64% lift figure is the number to watch: it is high by any standard, and if it holds at scale, this startup is underpriced. The funding is a small number in a Gulf roundup dominated by billion-dollar deals, but it sits squarely in the fintech infrastructure layer where regional winners are being built.

05Batwara 1947 stumbles
Batwara 1947 falls short at box office
Batwara 1947, the Preity Zinta comeback vehicle directed by Rajkumar Santoshi and produced by Aamir Khan, has grossed ₹38.80 crore net domestically against a reported budget of ₹150 crore. The film, adapted from Asghar Wajahat's play Jis Lahore Nai Vekhya, O Jamya E Nai, opened to mixed-to-negative reviews. Zinta's response was to say she gives the film her '1000 per cent'.

The math is unforgiving. The box office recovery rate of roughly 26% of budget means the film will struggle to break even even with overseas and streaming revenue. That ₹150 crore outlay is the number that makes the stumble real: a film about Partition made at a price that demands a blockbuster and delivered a niche drama's returns. Zinta's next release is the spy comedy Vibe, hitting theaters September 18, so the comeback campaign has a quick second act. For Gulf exhibitors, a weak Bollywood opening means reallocating screens toward stronger regional and Hollywood titles for the next two weeks.

The AI Race
​
Cognition AI doubles valuation to $48B
Cognition AI raised $2 billion in a Series E led by Andreessen Horowitz and Accel. The round nearly doubles its May valuation of $26 billion to $48 billion. The startup sold AI coding tools at a pace that surprised even the people who funded it. The cash gives it room to hire and build without being acquired.

The deal signals that investors believe AI coding is far from a winner-take-all market. Amazon also made a $60 billion Qualcomm chip pact this week. Meta released Muse, a personal AI agent. The field is now crowded with companies that think they can outrun Nvidia's data center grip. Gulf sovereign funds and tech hubs will see more AI startups priced at these levels, which raises the cost of every local AI bet.

ADGM assets up 54% to a new record
Abu Dhabi Global Market assets grew 54% year on year in the first half of 2026. Active licenses reached 13,900, including 1,800 issued during the half. New entrants in the half collectively manage more than $2.1 trillion in global assets.
Saudi Real Estate Refinance to issue SAR 431m sukuk
Saudi Real Estate Refinance Company is preparing to issue SAR 431.3 million in Islamic sukuk backed by residential mortgages from SNB and Arab National Bank. The deal is the first Saudi-backed RMBS with a published credit rating. SRC signed a SAR 3 billion agreement in April to buy a mortgage portfolio from Al Rajhi Bank.
Qatar keeps AA rating as export risks ease
Fitch removed the negative watch on Qatar's AA rating after risks of further damage to LNG facilities receded. The outlook remains negative on prolonged export disruption. The move signals that the initial war-related downside to Qatar's gas complex has been priced in.
Egypt triples oil imports to $2.2 billion
Egypt's crude oil imports surged to about $2.2 billion in the first half of 2026, up from nearly $606 million a year earlier. LNG imports climbed to nearly $6 billion from $3.5 billion. Egypt is spending heavily to manage the same energy price spike that is pressuring the Gulf.
Amazon could buy $60 billion in Qualcomm AI chips
Qualcomm announced a long-term partnership with Amazon that could see Amazon purchase up to $60 billion in Qualcomm's AI data center chips. Amazon also receives $4 billion in warrants vesting with product purchases at $161.26 per share. The deal is a direct challenge to Nvidia's data center dominance.

The Last Sip

Which Gulf city has more registered cats than cars?
The answer
Kuwait City has an enormous stray cat population, estimated in the hundreds of thousands. Citizens and expats feed them daily, and whole neighborhoods organise around them. The municipality has tried catch-neuter-return programs, but the cats have outlasted every plan. The city has basically become a cat commune with a highway running through it.
Which Gulf airport handles more international passengers than any other?
The answer
Dubai International, every year. It moved over 90 million international passengers before the war and remains the world's busiest for foreign travel. The second is no longer close, because the gap is the size of a small country's entire population. Somewhere in Dubai there is a traffic light that has never seen a moving car.
