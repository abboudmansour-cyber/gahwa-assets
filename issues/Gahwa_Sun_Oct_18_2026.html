02Up to 30 months
Egypt pledges troops to defend Saudi Arabia
Egypt's President Abdel Fattah El Sisi told parliament to hold an emergency session to authorize Egyptian military intervention in defense of Saudi Arabia "if asked." He said Egypt will take any measures requested by the Kingdom to defend its security.

Turkey and Pakistan have also pledged forces for rapid deployment inside the Kingdom under the Makkah Defense Pact, with Ankara ruling out offensive operations. Damascus is weighing defensive support, and Trump said the US "may" join Saudi strikes on the Houthis.

Sisi confirmed his presidential term ends in 2030 and said the Constitution will not be amended to extend his time in office, reportedly. He proposed an Egyptian Military Academy program to prepare presidential candidates, lasting up to 30 months and including over 7,500 hours of training. The public commitment from Egypt, Turkey, and Pakistan turns the Makkah Defense Pact from a paper alliance into a deployable military force.

03UAE property question
Company-rented apartment after resignation: your rights
The company, not the employee, holds the landlord contract when named tenant on Ejari. When the employer is named as tenant on the tenancy agreement and Ejari, the contractual relationship with the landlord is primarily with the company. It is not primarily with the employee. The right to occupy may come from the employment package, according to a property adviser.

The adviser recommends checking three documents: the tenancy agreement, the Ejari registration, and the employment contract or company housing policy.

For off-plan "guaranteed 10 per cent return for three years" schemes, the advice is to establish which legal entity backs the guarantee. Also establish how the 10% is calculated, and whether service charges are deducted before or after payment. The math often hides the catch: a Dh2 million apartment renting at Dh120,000 a year yields 6% gross. A promised Dh200,000 implies an extra Dh80,000 coming from somewhere, usually a higher purchase price. Between the tenancy agreement and the Ejari, the company's name is the only one the landlord is bound to recognise.
Source: The National Business

04Not so extraordinary
iPhone Duo UAE delivery slips to January
Apple's foldable iPhone Duo introduced alongside the iPhone 18 Pro last month has seen delivery estimates push from November to December. By Saturday they had pushed toward the end of January. The UAE platform shows a January 22 estimate across all four storage capacities and two colour options.

The iPhone 18 Pro, by contrast, shows delivery estimates of about two to three weeks. Apple said preorder demand for the iPhone Duo "has been nothing short of extraordinary." Counterpoint Research expected about 6 million Duos sold this year. Bloomberg Intelligence estimates sales could hit 14 million, adding about USD 28 billion to revenue for fiscal 2027.

The foldable is priced just USD 100 above Samsung's Galaxy Z Fold8, which suggests Apple is being aggressive rather than premium-pricing the category. In the UAE, the iPhone Duo ranges from Dh8,499 to Dh13,599. The wait is long, but the pricing signals Apple wants to win the foldable category now, not milk it as a niche.
Source: The National Business

05Saudi FDI paradox
Saudi FDI inflows rise but outflows double
Foreign direct investment into Saudi Arabia reached SAR 159.1 billion in 2025, up 33.5% year on year, according to Gastat. But outflows nearly doubled to SAR 77.7 billion from SAR 39 billion, leaving net inflows up just 1.7% at SAR 81.4 billion.

Construction led with SAR 34.5 billion, or 21.7% of inflows, followed by manufacturing at SAR 33.4 billion and admin/support services at SAR 22.4 billion. The Kingdom targets SAR 388 billion in annual inflows by 2030.

The gap between gross and net matters because it shows the Kingdom is both attracting and losing capital at scale. The headline number feeds the Vision 2030 narrative; the net number feeds the actual balance of payments. The outflows suggest that while capital is coming in, existing investors are repatriating at a rate that offsets most of the new money.

World's oldest shipwreck found off Turkey
Archaeologists diving off southern Turkey found what may be the world's oldest shipwreck, dating back 4,700 years. The wooden ship didn't survive, but dozens of intact clay jars were found, likely used to transport olive oil or wine. One jar had nine different markings and two holes, suggesting sailors produced food at sea. The jars survived long enough to make the wooden ship look like packaging.
