The Lead
​
SAIB exits Amex Saudi at 2x book
SAIB is selling its 50% stake in American Express Saudi Arabia to Amex Middle East for SAR 1.43 billion upfront plus deferred consideration, exiting the venture entirely. The price is more than double the stake's SAR 637.7 million book value as of November 2025. SAIB expects to book a gain of roughly SAR 792.4 million.

The sale comes as American Express Saudi Arabia's net income climbed from SAR 88.2 million in 2023 to SAR 152 million in 2025. The business is growing, which makes the timing notable. SAIB's decision follows Sama's 2025 reforms that capped credit-card fees at 2% for international transactions and 3% for withdrawals.

The move leaves a key question for the Saudi payments market. Electronic payments already account for 85% of retail transactions, and POS terminals topped 2 million in 2024. Amex will now run its Saudi operation directly, which may sharpen its competitive posture in a market where banks and fintechs are fighting for the same cardholder spend. Amex is taking direct control of its Saudi business just as the card fee cap squeezes returns for the whole market.

02Solar import shock
Gulf solar imports collapse in war
The UAE's average monthly solar PV imports fell from 785 megawatts in 2025 to just 98 megawatts since the Iran war began in March, a drop of nearly 88%. Saudi Arabia lost a similar 580 megawatts, falling from 719 MW to 139 MW. The data comes from a Rystad Energy whitepaper published this week.

The shock is hitting input costs across the entire solar supply chain, which are up 7 to 15%. Freight rates on the Asia-Mediterranean route rose 25 to 30% between late February and early April. China's removal of the VAT export rebate on 1 April added roughly 9% to module costs.

The delay is now visible in project timelines. Rystad expects the region's active solar pipeline to be delayed by three to twelve months overall. The UAE has just over 6.5 gigawatts of solar PV installed, about 17% of MENA's total, and EWEC is targeting more than 30 gigawatts by 2035. The Masdar-EWEC project pairing 5.2 gigawatts of solar with 19 gigawatt-hours of storage is now under pressure.

The local manufacturing response is only beginning. Only 20 to 25% of the UAE's solar equipment is manufactured locally, though MENA's module manufacturing capacity is on track to jump from 1 gigawatt in 2020 to 46 gigawatts by 2030. The next test is DEWA's upcoming Phase. Every delayed solar project in the Gulf now sits on the same waiting list for the same Chinese modules, with no short-term alternative in sight.

03Iran-US war latest
Kuwait reportedly intercepts Iran missiles, drones
Kuwait's army said Thursday it was intercepting a missile and drone attack from Iran, according to official statements reported by regional media. Iranian forces reportedly targeted US bases in Kuwait, Jordan, UAE, Bahrain and Iraq's autonomous Kurdistan region. The same day, the UN Secretary-General expressed grave concern after renewed strikes between the US and Iran, condemning all attacks against civilians, according to UN statements. An Iranian Red Crescent report said a US attack at a wedding in southern Sirik county killed four people including a child and wounded more than 50, and, per the report, the military exchange is reportedly already tightening oil and shipping markets. Oil advanced for a third session, with Brent trading around $96 a barrel. Four commodity vessels transited the Strait of Hormuz on Tuesday, down from 10 a day earlier. The pattern of escalating strikes with civilian casualties, officials say, makes any near-term diplomatic exit harder to see. The Strait of Hormuz is now the most dangerous chokepoint for global energy, and every Gulf business is paying the premium.

04G42 seeks outside cash
G42 explores multi-billion dollar raise
G42 is exploring a multi-billion-dollar capital raise from outside investors, according to Bloomberg. The Abu Dhabi AI firm is backed by Mubadala, Silver Lake and Microsoft. It would mark the first time G42 has sought this level of external capital.

The move follows MGX, another Abu Dhabi AI vehicle, which recently raised $49 billion for an AI-focused fund. Washington eased export controls in July, giving G42 and Core42 license-free access to advanced computing equipment. G42 is also building a 5-gigawatt UAE-US AI Campus in Abu Dhabi.

The capital raise signals G42 is scaling beyond sovereign backing into a wider investor base. The UAE is positioning its AI infrastructure as a global platform, and G42's move will test whether the market accepts Abu Dhabi's AI story on commercial terms. When a state-backed AI firm goes to outside investors, the UAE is saying its AI national champion is ready for the market's scrutiny.

Dubai Islamic closes debut $750m facility
Dubai Islamic, the UAE's largest Islamic bank, closed its first syndicated Islamic financing with a $750 million facility. The bank drew about $1.2 billion in commitments, led by HSBC, Mizuho and Standard Chartered. The deal signals Gulf banks are diversifying funding sources beyond deposits. QatarEnergy frontrunners emerge for $4.5bn field
QatarEnergy issued the main EPC tender last year for an estimated $4 billion to $5 billion project to expand the Maydan Mahzam offshore oil field. Frontrunners are now emerging for the Gulf waters project. The field expansion is part of Qatar's push to sustain offshore output. The project will test contractor appetite for offshore work amid war-risk insurance costs.
OQ seeks revised prices for NGL project
Oman's OQ Group is asking preferred contractors to submit revised prices for its NGL facility at Saih Nihayda in central Oman. The facility will extract condensate and transport it to Duqm for fractionation and export. The project is moving through a feed-to-EPC competition. Revised pricing requests often signal that first-round bids exceeded budget.

The answer

What did Kuwait's Future Generations Fund just allow for the first time?
The answer
An Emiri decree now permits the Kuwaiti government to borrow from its sovereign wealth fund. The fund was designed to preserve oil wealth for future generations, and it was long considered politically untouchable. The change is subject to strict caps. Future generations just became reluctant landlords to the present one.
