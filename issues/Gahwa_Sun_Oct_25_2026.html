Riyadh airport reopens after two-week Houthi closure
King Khalid International Airport resumed operations at Terminals 1 and 2 effective Sunday, October 25, 2026, according to airport officials. Terminal 1 handles domestic flights and Terminal 2 serves international and national carriers. The airport had been closed since October 10, when a Houthi attack killed 13 people and injured more than 300, according to official figures.

The reopening came hours after Saudi Civil Defence announced that danger had passed in Riyadh following a fresh alert. Coalition spokesman Maj-Gen Turki Al Maliki said a ballistic missile toward Riyadh was intercepted and destroyed. Two drones were also taken down over the Riyadh region. Flight Radar showed a plane departing Riyadh early Sunday heading to Jeddah.

The Houthis have warned that King Khalid International Airport is a military target and called it unsafe for airlines, staff and travellers. The warning came from Houthi spokesman Yahya Saree, who repeated the threat on the day flights resumed. The resumption is a direct signal that Saudi Arabia intends to keep its main airport running despite the Houthi threat. This is. Three explosions were heard in Riyadh on Saturday. The National Early Warning Platform had issued a fresh alert hours before flights resumed. The coalition said four ballistic missiles and 12 drones were intercepted in the latest round of attacks, according to the coalition. Airlines and passengers are now weighing the official assurance that danger has passed against a threat that has not gone away. Travellers and airlines now face a choice between the official all-clear and a Houthi threat that specifically names King Khalid airport as a target.

02Seven cables under seven knots
Reportedly, tanker hit near Hormuz as US weighs new strikes on Iran
A tanker was hit by an unknown projectile 7 nautical miles northeast of Limah, Oman, according to a UKMTO advisory. It was exiting the Strait of Hormuz at 5:15pm UTC on October 22. The vessel continued its voyage after the impact. The advisory, numbered 181-26, did not identify the ship or the nature of the projectile.

The UK Maritime Trade Operations office issued the alert as the US prepares possible renewed military action against Iran. US envoy Tom Barrack said the US could strike Iran as soon as this weekend, and the USS George Washington carrier strike group is loading munitions, according to the Wall Street Journal. Shipping and energy operators in the Gulf are now navigating a stretch where Iran has explicitly threatened the underwater cables running beneath the Strait of Hormuz, seven major internet cables included, according to two Iranian security officials who told Reuters on October 23 that Tehran has drawn up plans to target undersea communications cables in the Gulf if the US or Israel launch fresh attacks. An IRGC deputy head of political affairs, Yadollah Javani, said those cables could be targeted, and Tehran could delay repairs to prolong disruptions. The British Foreign Secretary is pushing for a diplomatic solution to restore freedom of navigation in the strait. A tanker exiting Hormuz is now a moving target in a standoff. In that standoff, the strait, the cables beneath it, and the oil passing through it are all on the table.

03Deeds for a grand
Saudi proptech Ghanem raises $1.5m extension
Saudi proptech Ghanem raised an additional $1.5 million in a pre-seed extension backed by real estate companies and strategic angel investors. This takes the round total to $8.6 million. The extension follows a $7.1 million financing that included Al-Romaih Group. Waad VC also invested.

The platform, founded in 2025 by Saleh Waheed Al-Ghamdi and Amr Essam, lets people buy fractions of real estate with minimum investments from SAR 1,000. It has issued digital ownership deeds to 580 individuals in its first income-generating property, with monthly dividends distributed with zero delays. The company signed a digital integration agreement with the Real Estate Registry and operates inside the regulatory sandbox of the Saudi Real Estate General Authority.

Fractional ownership is turning Saudi real estate into a product a retail investor can hold for the price of a phone. The sandbox approval means Ghanem can test its model under regulator supervision. The dividend record gives it something to show investors beyond a pitch deck. The first property has already put dividend timing ahead of proptech fundraising announcements.

04US drawdown
US orders some diplomatic staff out of Saudi Arabia
The US State Department ordered family members and nonessential personnel to leave the US Embassy in Riyadh and consulates in Jeddah and Dhahran, according to Axios. The same order reduced personnel at embassies in the UAE, Lebanon, Qatar and Jordan. The move follows a US intelligence memo warning that Iran has substantially increased efforts to target American diplomats and officials. Lethal plots have reached advanced stages.

The memo, obtained by Axios, was sent to Secretary of State Marco Rubio. It describes a security environment in which American personnel across the Gulf are facing a threat level. The US has not seen this level since the escalation began. The ordered departure covers families and nonessential staff, not the entire embassy, which continues to operate.

The drawdown is a signal to Gulf governments and to businesses: the US is treating the region as a conflict zone. This is even as it keeps its military assets in place. American companies with staff in the Gulf will read this as a prompt to review their own evacuation plans and security protocols. When the US moves families out, the private sector takes note: the official security assessment of the Gulf just got more serious.

05War cost
IMF sees GCC non-oil economy shrinking 0.8%
The IMF sees the GCC non-oil economy shrinking 0.8% this year as the war spreads beyond oil. The estimate, reported by, marks a sharp reversal from the growth path the region was on before the escalation. The figure covers the whole Gulf Cooperation Council, meaning some economies will do better and others worse.

The engine of the Gulf's diversification has been tourism, logistics and construction. All of these depend on open airports, safe ports and investors who feel the region is stable. Reportedly, an attack on Riyadh's airport that closes it for two weeks hits visitor numbers, flight schedules and every business that depends on them. The IMF's number captures that damage.

The message for Gulf operators is that even economies with strong balance sheets are not immune. The non-oil economy is where most private-sector jobs and most small businesses sit. When it shrinks, the pain is felt in offices, shops and construction sites, not in oil ministry statements. A shrinking non-oil economy means the Gulf's diversification story, which is where most private-sector growth lives, is now facing a war it did not start.

The AI Race
​
Neom gets a named tenant
Humain signed Nebius as its first foreign anchor customer for up to 50 MW at the AI giga-campus in Neom's Oxagon, developed with DataVolt. That is half of the initial 100 MW under construction. Capacity phases in from Q4 2027. The preliminary framework comes six weeks after ground broke on the site. The first phase is designed for 360 MW.

Humain also expanded its Nvidia collaboration to as much as 84 MW of compute, roughly 27,000 GPUs. It starts with 14 MW of Blackwell HGX B300 systems in Q4 2026. It then adds 70 MW of next-generation Vera Rubin infrastructure from mid-2027. The operator targets 1.7 GW of capacity in 2027 and 3 GW by 2030. CEO Tareq Amin is pursuing a domestic data-center fund seeking an initial $2.5 billion. The Nebius deal is the first test of whether Oxagon can attract the foreign tenants a 3 GW build-out needs. Neom's AI campus now has a named customer, a phased timeline, and a GPU count. A named foreign tenant at Oxagon turns Neom's AI ambitions into a commercial race the Gulf is now actually running.
