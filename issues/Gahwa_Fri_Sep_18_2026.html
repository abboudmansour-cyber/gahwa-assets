The Lead
​
Iran's Revolutionary Guards seized an oil tanker in the Strait of Hormuz, according to officials, the second disruption to Gulf shipping this month.
The UK's Maritime Trade Operations confirmed a security incident 16 nautical miles northeast of Khasab, Oman, placing the seizure within waters that carry a fifth of global oil supply, officials say. The attack follows a month in which Saudi Arabia has reportedly already rerouted crude exports to ship-to-ship transfers near Oman's Sohar port after its East-West pipeline was damaged.

Brent crude sits near $103.59 a barrel, down 1.2% on the day, and Murban crude fell 1.95% to $121. The US has approved, according to officials, a $24.3 billion sale of up to 48 F-35 stealth warplanes to Saudi Arabia, hardening the Kingdom's air defenses as Washington declines to strike Houthi positions.

Gulf shipping operators now face a double squeeze: a contested Strait of Hormuz to the north and a contested Red Sea to the south. Freight and insurance costs will climb first, then pass into the price of every imported good. The White House is set to meet Gulf leaders in New York next week, but officials say the tanker seizure shows the threat is moving faster than the diplomacy. Every escalation here tightens the risk premium on Gulf crude, which feeds directly into fuel costs for operators across the region.

02Power struggle
UAE pours billions into Syria
UAE investors are committing billions to Syrian reconstruction, led by Arada's $7 billion agreement for a 4 million square metre New Damascus development and Mohamed Alabbar's planned $18 billion through Eagle Hills across Damascus and Latakia.

DP World is implementing an $800 million Tartous Port overhaul, while AD Ports agreed to take 20% of the Latakia International Container Terminal. The moves come as Syria's power generation sits near 1.6 gigawatts, against roughly 9.5 gigawatts before 2011.

The bottleneck is not money but execution. Syria's domestic banks may lend only about $5 million per contractor against projects where bonding requirements reach $10 to 15 million. The Finance Ministry is introducing a cross-agency committee to exit the FATF gray list, with a review due in October, and correspondent banks remain cautious. The opportunity is enormous, but the first movers will need to self-finance most of the build.

03Small change
UAE opens second retail T-sukuk
A second sovereign retail T-sukuk is now open with a five-year tenor and a minimum subscription of AED 1,000.

The inaugural offering attracted AED 445 million in orders, nearly nine times its initial AED 50 million target, at a 4.30% annual coupon paid every six months. UAE nationals made up 72% of investors, and 76% of demand came in tickets of AED 10,000 or less.

The new issuance doubles the target to AED 100 million and lists on Nasdaq Dubai. For Gulf retail investors, a government-backed, liquid, Shariah-compliant instrument at roughly 4% is a direct competitor to bank deposits. The small ticket size and national focus make this a savings product to watch for every household in the Emirates.

04Saudi proptech
Rize lands $50m Jadwa facility
Saudi proptech Rize secured a SAR 187.5 million asset-backed Murabaha facility from Jadwa Investment. The company's Rent Now, Pay Later model lets tenants split annual rent into 12 monthly payments while landlords receive rent upfront.

Rize has served more than 200,000 tenants through 3,000 landlords and 1,200 verified real estate brokers, all documented through Saudi Arabia's Ejar platform. The Saudi residential rental market is estimated at SAR 150 billion annually.

The company closed a $35 million Series A in January 2025 and is licensed by the Saudi Real Estate General Authority. For landlords, the facility means more certainty that Rize can front cash across a wider portfolio. The facility is a direct bet on rent-backed finance becoming a mainstream asset class in the Kingdom.

The AI Race
​
DeepMind funds AGI's long game
Google DeepMind launched the DeepMind Institute, led by Demis Hassabis and Shane Legg, to fund interdisciplinary research on how artificial general intelligence will reshape society. The institute will support work across economics, ethics, governance, and the behavioural sciences, joining the sparse field of actors funding AGI-safety research at scale.

The move puts DeepMind's own leadership behind questions the rest of the industry treats as footnotes: what happens to labour markets, institutions, and public trust once AGI arrives. It is a rare admission by a leading lab that the biggest unknowns are not technical. For every other frontier lab, it raises the bar on funding the study of consequences rather than just benchmarks. Gulf AI strategies tied to US and UK labs will face pressure to fund parallel governance research, not only compute and model access.
Business
​
L'imad eyes a stake in $10bn Atlas Air
Abu Dhabi's L'imad Holding is weighing a bid for a stake in Atlas Air Worldwide, the Apollo-controlled air cargo giant valued north of $10 billion. L'imad already pushed its stake in AD Ports past 98.5% to take the company private. The move would extend a pattern of consolidating logistics and freight assets. Atlas Air operates a large fleet of Boeing freighter aircraft.
Meta signs 144MW Texas solar deal
Meta entered a power purchase agreement with Apex for environmental attributes from the 144MW Starling Solar project in Texas. The deal underwrites part of Meta's expanding data center power demand. It is the latest in a series of contracts by big tech firms buying clean power at scale. For energy suppliers in the Gulf, it is a signal of how far buyers will go for green attributes.
QSI completes KAS Eupen acquisition
Qatar Sports Investments completed its full acquisition of Belgian football club KAS Eupen, nine months after taking control of sporting operations. QSI's portfolio already includes Paris Saint-Germain and SC Braga. The deal deepens Qatar's multi-club ownership model. It signals continued appetite for European football assets even as returns are debated.
Dubai inflation rises to 5.54% in August
Dubai consumer prices rose 5.54% year on year in August, up from 5.3% in July. Emirates NBD nearly doubled its year-end inflation forecast to 5.6%.
UAE launches second retail T-sukuk
The UAE federal government launched its second sovereign retail T-sukuk with a five-year tenor and a minimum subscription of AED 1,000. The debut drew AED 445 million in orders, about nine times its original target, at a 4.30% coupon.

Snoopy Beats, a three-day music festival, returns to Fujairah's Sandy Beach Hotel & Resort, timed around Halloween and running from late October into early November. The Last Sip
​
Which Gulf city has more registered cats than cars?
The answer
Kuwait City. Its stray and feral cat population is commonly estimated in the hundreds of thousands, and organised feeding stations are a daily feature. The city has run capture and neuter programmes for years. The cats are largely friendly, fed, and a recognised part of street life. Parking remains the bigger problem. The cat has a spot; your car does not.
Dubai's airport handles more international passengers than any other. Which airport is second?
The answer
London Heathrow. Dubai International held the top spot for international passenger numbers, with Heathrow the long-time second. The gap shifts year to year, but the rivalry is between two airports with barely any domestic market. Both run on connecting travellers. For a Gulf reader, it is a reminder that Dubai's main competitor is not in the region. The real race is who fills an A380 at 3am.
Where does the word 'sukuk' come from?
The answer
It is the Arabic plural of 'sakk', meaning a written note or certificate. Medieval Islamic merchants used such notes to transfer value. The modern financial instrument is a descendant of that old paper trail. A government now sells them at a 4.30% coupon. The word outlived the caravan. The paperwork got better; the idea stayed the same.
