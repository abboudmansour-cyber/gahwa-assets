The Lead
​
Mubadala tilts $39 billion toward Asia
Mubadala, Abu Dhabi's sovereign fund, plans to deploy another $39 billion this year after deploying a similar amount last year, according to CFO Carlos Obeid. The fund's Asia exposure rose from 10 percent to 13 percent of the portfolio, with China, India, South Korea and Japan named as targets. North America remains the largest allocation at 44 percent, while the UAE accounts for 24 percent and Europe 15 percent.

The fund's recent moves reflect that pivot. Mubadala took a minority stake in China's Luckin Coffee for about $1 billion last month, bringing its total China investments to $20 billion since 2015. The coffee chain is China's largest by store count, and Mubadala is effectively doubling down on Chinese consumer recovery. The $39 billion deployment pace matches last year's but now comes as other Gulf funds trim US dollar exposure and look for growth in Asian markets.

For the Gulf, the shift matters because it signals where Abu Dhabi sees returns over the next decade. A 3 percentage point move in portfolio allocation is not a rebalancing, it is a direction of travel. Gulf founders and operators should expect deeper Mubadala involvement across Asia-linked deals. The Boring Company's Abu Dhabi exploration pact, signed separately, shows the fund is still placing bets on local infrastructure too, but the money is clearly tilting east. The fund that long anchored Abu Dhabi's Western portfolio is now writing its largest cheques toward Shanghai, Mumbai and Seoul.

02Ten months to launch
Saudi AI startup Echelon lands backing
Saudi AI startup Echelon has raised an undisclosed round backed by East40, RAED Ventures, Avra and Ben Horowitz. Founded in 2025 by Khalid Bin Bader Al Saud, the company spent about ten months developing its model and has partnerships with Applied Intuition, IFS and Rubrik. It targets eight sectors including energy, industrials, government, telecom, defence, aviation, financial services and healthcare.

Echelon plans to launch Frontier, its engineering arm, and an Institute to train Saudi engineers in the fourth quarter of 2026. Echelon Defense Industries is scheduled for the first quarter of 2027. The company's technologies division is led by former Microsoft executive Omar Saleh.

The interest from US investor Ben Horowitz, whose firm has been openly courting Gulf capital, marks a notable vote of confidence in a Saudi model builder. The market is crowded with Western models, but a sovereign-backed local competitor, aiming at defence and energy, is exactly the kind of bet that sits at the centre of Vision 2030. Echelon's next move, an engineering arm and a defence division, will test whether a Saudi-trained model can win contracts at home and beyond.

03Hormuz watch
Iran weighs Hormuz plan as US holds
Iran is reviewing a US response to its seven-day plan to reopen the Strait of Hormuz, according to Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi. The plan demands an end to hostilities, release of frozen assets, lifting of oil export restrictions and an end to the US naval blockade. President Trump has ruled out new attacks on Iran before the November 3 midterm elections, officials say.

Oil prices jumped 5 percent this week as tanker attacks escalated and a US Gulf hurricane shut in production. The strait remains the single most important chokepoint for global oil, with Gulf producers exporting most of their crude through it. Any reopening would ease price pressure, while failure would push shipping costs and insurance rates higher still.

The US has sent 9,000 more troops and a third aircraft carrier to the region, while the USS Abraham Lincoln returned to San Diego after a yearlong deployment. Gulf states continue to watch nervously, as analysts say any miscalculation could close the strait entirely. The next ten days, before the midterm vote, will be watched closely by oil markets and shipping insurers. A deal would bring lower oil prices and cheaper shipping almost overnight; no deal would keep the war premium built into every barrel leaving the Gulf.

04Electric fleets
Three firms electrify Saudi delivery fleet
EVIQ, Hala Auto and DSV are joining forces to electrify part of Saudi Arabia's logistics fleet. Hala Auto will supply electric cargo vans and trucks for DSV's operations, while EVIQ will develop dedicated depot charging infrastructure in Riyadh. DSV will target total cost of ownership reduction through lower fuel and maintenance costs. The partnership supports Vision 2030 sustainability goals. The test of this partnership will be whether the charging depots arrive before the trucks do, and whether the maths survives a real Riyadh summer.

The AI Race
​
AI agents price by buyer's look
A Cisco and Carnegie Mellon study ran 325,000 tests on 13 AI agents. Eight of the 13 steered wealthier-looking people toward pricier options. Blocking the view of a job made the insurance gap 40 percent bigger for one agent. The findings suggest AI agents adapt pricing based on perceived buyer wealth.

The study lands in the middle of a global debate about whether algorithmic pricing is fair. For Gulf regulators weighing AI consumer protection, the warning is clear: buyers may need more than self-regulation. The same agent tech that promises to make shopping faster can quietly make it more expensive. Gulf regulators will have to decide quickly whether algorithmic pricing needs a rulebook, not a promise.
Business
​
QatarEnergy wins seven Brazil offshore blocks
QatarEnergy took a 30 percent participating interest in the Ceará Basin blocks, with Brazil's Petrobras as operator. The win extends QatarEnergy's growing Latin American footprint. The Ceará Basin is one of Brazil's frontier offshore plays. The deal gives the Gulf firm a seat at the table in a region it has been quietly building.
Empower starts Dubai district cooling plant
Empower has begun works on a Dubai district cooling plant with a production capacity of 47,000 refrigeration tonnes. The plant will serve nearly 80 mixed-use buildings. Empower continues to expand its district cooling network across Dubai. The new capacity is aimed at the emirate's next wave of integrated developments.
Nakheel launches Bay Estate at Dubai Islands
Nakheel has launched Bay Estate at Dubai Islands, comprising 360 three- and four-bedroom villas and townhouses, with beachfront and waterfront plots. The launch adds to Dubai's branded and luxury residential pipeline. The developer continues to roll out high-end inventory across the city's reclaimed islands.
Kuwait retenders Mina Abdullah water pipeline
Kuwait has retendered the water pipeline project from Mina Abdullah. The tender covers three water pipelines from the Mina Abdullah water distribution complex in Al-Ahmadi Governorate. The retender suggests bidders sought revised terms. The original scope did not attract the expected pricing.
Saudi Arabia's TASI closes 9M 2026 down 9.2 percent
The benchmark index ended September at 10,400 points, about 10 percent below its April peak. Foreign investors sold a net SAR 4.33 billion in September alone. The Nomu parallel market has seen just one IPO in nine months versus 21 a year earlier.
