The Lead
​
Riyadh airport hit
A missile reportedly hit King Khalid International Airport on Saturday, killing 12 and injuring more than 300, the third strike on the airport in a week. The attack reportedly hit the complex housing domestic terminals 3 and 4. The dead include four Saudi citizens and eight foreign residents, including one US citizen, an Egyptian, a Jordanian, a Syrian, a Palestinian, a Sudanese national, and two Bangladeshis, according to officials.

Saudi-backed coalition spokesman Turki al-Maliki described the attack as a war crime and vowed a decisive response. The coalition said it intercepted a ballistic missile in Al-Kharj, southeast of Riyadh, where Prince Sultan airbase is located, according to the coalition. A projectile also landed near King Fahd International Airport in Dammam.

Cirium counted around 300 cancellations at Riyadh on Thursday, with scores more at Jeddah and Dammam. Qatar Airways, Turkish Airlines, and flydubai cancelled flights. Lufthansa, EgyptAir, Air Cairo, Philippine Airlines, Singapore Airlines, and three Indian carriers suspended Riyadh service. The UK warned against all but essential travel to parts of the Kingdom.

Why FII still stands Organizers said the Future Investment Initiative, scheduled for 26-29 October in Riyadh, will proceed with comprehensive security measures. Italy's energy minister will address the World Petroleum Council Energy Congress by video instead of in person, a sign of how foreign executives are curtailing travel. The attack killed three Saudi citizens in earlier strikes, including Saudia Captain Hamoud Al-Kalthami, according to officials.

Abu Dhabi mends fences with Riyadh
The UAE has reportedly offered notable public support for Saudi Arabia amid waves of Houthi attacks on the kingdom. The support marks a shift toward closer coordination at a moment of heightened security threat.

Strategic independence remains a watchword for Emirati leaders. The UAE has balanced its support for Riyadh with its own regional and economic priorities. The support arrives as Houthi strikes on Saudi airports reportedly disrupt commercial aviation and test the confidence of foreign carriers. Closer alignment between the two Gulf powers shapes the response to a conflict that is spilling into civil infrastructure. The two Gulf powers are aligning more closely as the security threat grows, but both still guard their freedom to act independently.

03Oil supply shifts
Russia unlocks 500k tons diesel
Russia agreed to release 500,000 tons of diesel into international markets, partially lifting its diesel export ban. The move follows a Putin-Trump agreement aimed at easing global diesel prices near record highs.

Trump announced the deal with Russia to supply 300,000 tons to the US and global markets immediately, with another 500,000 tons in November and 1,000,000 tons thereafter. The US Treasury waived sanctions on Russian diesel until April 2027.

The release comes as Gulf suppliers are urged to halt Hormuz crossings. Any easing of fuel prices helps Gulf economies that rely on diesel for transport and industry, but it also signals how energy diplomacy is being used as a lever in the conflict. The release comes as Gulf suppliers are urged to halt Hormuz crossings, making every barrel of non-Gulf diesel matter for price stability.

The AI Race
​
Nadella: brake on AI models
Microsoft CEO Satya Nadella called for an emergency brake on frontier AI models this week. He wrote that it is time to step back and assess the trust architecture of AI. The statement is remarkable because Microsoft is one of the largest investors in AI infrastructure globally. Nadella is the most senior executive at a top AI lab to publicly suggest a pause-like measure since the 2023 letter signed by Elon Musk and others.

The call lands as regulators in Washington and Brussels argue over how to govern the most powerful models. Nadella did not specify a mechanism, but the words matter. A CEO who is building the data centers that train these models now wants a safety valve installed before the next generation launches. It puts pressure on OpenAI, Anthropic and Google to explain why they are not asking for the same thing. The debate is moving from think tanks to the CEO suite. Gulf governments building their own AI strategies now have cover to demand guardrails without looking anti-innovation.

The ghost story that outlived most West End shows
The Woman in Black, Stephen Mallatratt's stage adaptation, ran in London's West End for more than three decades until 2023. It is second only to The Mousetrap among London's long-running plays. The 2012 film starred Daniel Radcliffe in his first major role after Harry Potter. A reminder that some stories refuse to die, even after the theater goes dark.
