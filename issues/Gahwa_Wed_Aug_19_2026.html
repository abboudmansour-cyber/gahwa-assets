The Gahwa Brief

Today the Kingdom is laying claim to ten percent of its own land for metals. Meanwhile its oil is taking the long way around Hormuz. Both stories share the same quiet bet: the region is rewiring itself around a strait nobody can trust. Saudi Arabia is making its biggest physical bet yet on the minerals feeding the AI and energy transitions. Aramco and Maaden signed a deal to explore 182,000 square kilometres, roughly 10% of the Kingdom, for copper, zinc, lead and rare earths. At the same time, Aramco is rerouting crude through Sohar, Yanbu and Fujairah as traffic through the Strait of Hormuz slows to a trickle. PIF doubled its profit and signalled a shift from borrowing to exits and listings. Further down, Dubai's luxury hotels are suffering while midscale rooms hold firm, and the UAE aims to put half of government work on agentic AI.

The Lead
Aramco and Maaden just signed up to mine nearly 10% of Saudi Arabia's landmass.
​
Maaden holds 51% and Aramco 49% of the venture, which has a 182,000 square kilometre exploration area in Zone 4, a 100-km-wide corridor running parallel to the Arabian Shield. The plan was first announced in January 2025 and now moves from heads of terms to a formal company.

Saudi Arabia has raised its estimate of mineral resources to SAR 9.4 trillion, or USD 2.5 trillion, up from SAR 5.2 trillion. Exploration spending rose from USD 54 million in 2023 to USD 236 million in 2025, per S&P Global's Kevin Murphy, while active exploration companies went from six in 2020 to 226 in 2024. Aramco brings one of the world's largest geological data collections, spanning more than 90 years.

China's overcapacity has pushed treatment and refining charges negative over the past 18 months, so the venture enters a market where processing economics are already squeezed. Maaden is also pursuing rare-earth value chains including potential cooperation with MP Materials. Typical deposits take around 16 years from discovery to production, which means this is a multi-decade bet. Saudi Arabia is quietly laying claim to the minerals that will feed the electric grids and data centres of the next decade, and the exploration map now has a new anchor in the Arabian Shield. Explorers and service providers should map Zone 4 now, because 182,000 square kilometres of new ground will generate a long tail of contracts long before the first mine opens.

UAE reportedly halts Iran trade amid missile claims
The UAE has halted all trade and financial transactions with Iran, according to official statements. The order came after air defence systems reportedly detected two ballistic missiles launched from Iran, one of which fell inside UAE territorial waters. No damage or casualties were reported.

The escalation reportedly follows attacks on ADNOC tankers in the past week and the expiry of the US-Iran ceasefire. Iran has pledged a fully offensive military posture, and Strait of Hormuz traffic has slowed sharply. US President Donald Trump said no talks with Iran are planned, contradicting a claim by Jared Kushner that active diplomatic conversations were underway.

For Gulf operators, the suspension of trade with Iran removes a long-standing, if politically sensitive, commercial channel. Re-export and logistics firms that handled Iran-facing cargo through UAE ports now face immediate compliance updates. UAE businesses with historical exposure to Iranian counterparties should review their transaction flows this week, because the compliance expectation has shifted overnight. Compliance teams should treat Iran exposure as an immediate stop, not a grey zone, until further notice.

PIF doubles profit, defers debt
PIF's net income more than doubled in 2025, rising 152.4% year on year to SAR 65.2 billion. Assets under management grew 5% to SAR 4.54 trillion, and total shareholder return since 2017 reached 5.8% annualised. PIF now says it will not borrow in 2026 and will return to debt markets in early 2027.

CFO Yasir bin Abdullah Al Salman said international debt markets remain the primary financing source, with four channels now in use: divestments, capital markets financing, bank financing and reinvestment of returns. PIF also plans to list more portfolio companies on Tadawul. The fund contributed more than SAR 342 billion to Saudi non-oil GDP between 2021 and 2025.

The 2026-2030 strategy is organised around a Vision Portfolio with six ecosystems: tourism, urban development, manufacturing, logistics, clean energy and Neom. For contractors and suppliers, the pause in borrowing is not a pause in spending. PIF is signalling that the next phase runs on exits and asset sales, and every Gulf bank should be watching which portfolio names get prepared for the public market. The pipeline of PIF-backed listings is about to become the most important deal flow in the Gulf, and advisers who position early will be first in line.

UAE targets 50% agentic AI
The UAE aims to move 50% of government services and operations to agentic AI, and Core42 is positioning itself as the secure deployment layer for that shift. Raghu Chakravarthi, Core42's Chief Product and Technology Officer, outlined a platform with access to NVIDIA, AMD, Qualcomm and Cerebras silicon.

Core42's Compass tool is developing a workload classifier that routes tasks across models, accelerators and deployment paths, with more than 60 open and proprietary models in its library. The company cites more than 170 security policies and SOC 2 Type II certification.

The signal for Gulf operators is that government AI procurement is moving from pilots to production. Agentic AI in government means vendor lock-in debates, data sovereignty rules and a tight group of approved platforms. Any company selling AI services into the UAE public sector should know where it sits relative to Core42's stack, because the government is building a preferred path and Core42 is clearing the road. Technology vendors targeting UAE government contracts should map their compatibility with Core42's platform before the next tender cycle.

Dubai hotels see Q4 rebound
Cavendish Maxwell sees Dubai's hospitality market recovering from the fourth quarter, with full-year occupancy forecast at 60.4% to 66.2%. That follows a brutal first half when citywide occupancy fell 30.3% year on year to 56.4%. Average daily rates dipped just 7% to AED 701, but revenue per available room is down 35.2% year to date.

The pain is not evenly spread. Upper-midscale hotels held up best at 65.7% occupancy, while luxury properties languished at 51.2%. Dubai has 727 hotels with about 152,100 rooms, and roughly 3,200 more rooms are due by year-end. More than 80% of rooms scheduled for 2027 and 2028 are luxury.

Dubai's recovery is not expected to reach a full tourism rebound before 2029, and DXB passenger traffic is forecast at 67.6 million to 79.3 million, below 2025 levels. The emirate has already rolled out AED 2.5 billion in hospitality support, including fee suspensions. Hotel owners sitting on luxury pipelines are about to open their biggest properties into the weakest segment of the market. Operators should focus on the midscale and upper-midscale segments where occupancy is still holding, and delay uncommitted luxury openings until demand returns.

Arab Therapy raises $2m for Arabic mental health
Jordanian-born startup Arab Therapy raised $2 million to build a digital platform connecting Arabic-speaking patients with licensed therapists in their own language. The company, now based in Berlin, says its waitlist and early user base stretch across the Gulf, Levant and North Africa. The region has some of the lowest mental health service utilisation rates globally, partly due to language and cultural barriers. Stigma and a shortage of Arabic-speaking professionals keep most people from seeking help. Gulf HR leaders should evaluate Arabic-first mental health platforms now, because employee demand is outrunning supply.

Nvidia backs OpenAI's $105bn Ohio campus
Nvidia is backing up to $105 billion in financing for OpenAI's Pike County, Ohio data center project, built on a decommissioned uranium enrichment site. SB Energy, SoftBank's power subsidiary, will build and run the 10-gigawatt campus. Total cost including chips could top $500 billion. The project shows that AI success now depends on power and land, and Gulf sovereigns have both but are watching the US lock in first movers.
