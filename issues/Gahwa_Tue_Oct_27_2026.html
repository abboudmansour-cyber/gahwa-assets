The Lead
​
Apollo buys Mubadala
Apollo is taking a minority stake in Mubadala Capital, Abu Dhabi's asset management arm, that CNBC reported could be nearly 10%. The deal brings $5 billion in combined commitments from Apollo and other incoming shareholders at initial close.

Mubadala Capital's core alternatives business manages more than $60 billion across five strategies. Its wider operations manage, advise, or administer more than $755 billion.

Why Apollo wants in The two firms have worked together on private-market financing since 2020. This includes a $2.5 billion private credit joint venture launched in 2023. Apollo brings roughly $1.05 trillion in assets under management as of late June, and the deal hands it a front-row seat to Gulf capital deployment.

What changes for the region Mubadala Investment Company stays majority shareholder, with Mubadala Capital keeping its own governance. The deal is expected to close in the fourth quarter, pending regulatory approvals. It signals that even the Gulf's sovereign capital is open to selling minority positions to global managers. Apollo gets a front-row seat; Mubadala gets $5 billion and a shareholder who used to just lend it money.

02Going underground
Aramco plans Singapore crude storage
Aramco plans to store Saudi crude at Singapore's Jurong Rock Caverns starting in the second half of 2027. The caverns sit 150 metres below Jurong Island and hold approximately 9 million barrels of crude and condensate.

The Singapore MoU adds a third Asian storage host for Aramco. It follows Okinawa, where it stores 8.2 million barrels, and Ulsan in South Korea, where it holds 5.3 million barrels. Saudi Arabia aims to lift crude pipeline capacity outside the Gulf to 10 million barrels per day within two to three years.

Financing the buildout Aramco is also in final talks on a $10 billion revolving credit facility at 60 basis points above SOFR. Lenders include Bank of China, Citigroup, HSBC, ICBC, JPMorgan and Mitsubishi UFJ, as the company pushes capital into infrastructure that bypasses the Strait of Hormuz. Aramco has already awarded SAR2.4 billion in contracts to pipe makers this year, about 80% of the sector's total bookings. Reportedly, Aramco is paying for insurance against a Hormuz closure by storing more crude outside the Gulf. The logistics of where oil sits is becoming as important as how much is pumped.

03Bitcoin meets Abu Dhabi real estate
Standard Asset opens ADGM office for $1.5bn strategy
A $1.5 billion Bitcoin-and-real-estate strategy now runs from Abu Dhabi. Standard Asset Company opened its headquarters in Abu Dhabi Global Market to lead a $1.5 billion asset management strategy. The plan pairs a managed Bitcoin reserve with UAE real estate acquisitions, with Coinbase Asset Management engaged as exclusive asset manager for the Bitcoin holdings.

Who&#39;s behind it The firm was founded by Sakhib Waseem, Adam Ali and Mason Borda. Advisers include Sheikh Mohammed Bin Maktoum Bin Juma Al Maktoum and Coinbase Institutional Co-CEO Brett Tejpaul. The team has advised on more than $7 billion in transactions and facilitated over $1 billion in digital asset offerings. Bitcoin-backed real estate strategies are arriving in Abu Dhabi's regulated financial centre. The pairing of crypto reserves with UAE property gives investors a hedge that crosses two asset classes at once.

04Underground, with backups
UAE's AI power advantage faces biggest test
The UAE consumed approximately 173TWh of electricity in 2025, with data centres using about 3TWh, or 2%. Stargate UAE's full 5GW campus at full load would equal roughly 44TWh per year, a quarter of current national demand.

The grid is racing to keep up Masdar and Ewec reached financial close on a $6.1 billion project pairing 5.2GW of solar with 19GWh of batteries to deliver 1GW of round-the-clock power by 2027. Security changes the calculus: March Iranian strikes reportedly damaged two AWS data centres in the UAE. September Reuters reporting indicated the UAE is reworking its 5GW plan into dispersed smaller sites with underground facilities, air defences, and duplicated power and cooling. The UAE's AI infrastructure lead now depends on securing the power grid as much as the data centres themselves. The dispersed-site strategy is the quiet response to the region's new risk profile.
Source: The National Business

05Still no majority
Israel votes in first election since October 7
Reportedly, Israelis vote Tuesday in the first general election since the October 7, 2023 attacks. Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu faces centrist rival Gadi Eisenkot, with polls showing them running neck and neck.

About 7.3 million eligible voters, including 1.2 million Palestinian citizens of Israel, can cast ballots. Neither major bloc is projected to secure a governing majority, raising the prospect of coalition negotiations after the vote. The election outcome will shape Israel's next military decisions and its approach to the Gulf. Security policy and regional relations hang on which bloc forms the next government.

The AI Race
​
Anthropic's IPO reveals $16.3bn revenue, $80bn losses
Anthropic's filing for a Nasdaq listing disclosed that first-half revenue soared 14-fold to $16.3 billion. Net losses for the period exceeded $80 billion, but the filing says most of that is paper losses on convertible debt, not cash leaving the door. Computing costs nearly quadrupled to $8.9 billion. Abu Dhabi's MGX is already a backer. It co-led a $30 billion Series G in February and joined a $65 billion round in May.

The filing values Anthropic at more than $1.5 trillion, which makes the losses look like the entry fee to a very expensive club. The company is listing under ticker ANT as soon as mid-November. For the AI race, this is the biggest public market test yet of whether investors will accept enormous losses in exchange for frontier model access. MGX's $30 billion Series G stake now has a public price, and a November date to test it against.
Business
​
QatarEnergy lines up US LNG from Cheniere
QatarEnergy agreed to buy up to 4 million tonnes per annum of LNG from Cheniere's US terminals starting 2031. Reportedly, the deal gives Doha a supply backup if the Strait of Hormuz is closed. It is a direct hedge against disruption to Gulf exports.
Taqa prices green and conventional notes
Abu Dhabi utility Taqa priced green and conventional bonds to finance grid expansion. The issuance taps investor demand for Gulf utility credit. The money will support power infrastructure for the UAE's growing data centre and industrial needs. A utility has deigned to enter the public benchmark market once more.
LVMH fashion and leather goods revenue falls 5%
LVMH's Fashion & Leather Goods division, which includes Louis Vuitton and Dior, reported a 5% organic revenue decline in Q3 2026. Reportedly, Louis Vuitton also appointed Hector Muelas as its first-ever chief brand creative officer. The move shows the world's largest luxury group is reworking its flagship brand to chase younger buyers.
SpaceX closes $40 billion GPU debt deal
SpaceX closed $40 billion in callable debt for GPUs, with chips arriving in Q1 and Q2. The company guided fourth-quarter capex to between $35 billion and $40 billion. The scale of the raise shows how much compute the AI race is demanding. It also puts pressure on every other AI infrastructure player to find capital. Q1 and Q2 chip deliveries now come with a $40 billion price tag attached.
DP World eyes Canada's Arctic gateway
DP World is set to sign an MoU with Arctic Gateway Group to turn the Port of Churchill into an export hub to Europe and the Middle East. Canada's prospectus puts the full buildout at $57 billion, with preliminary requests of $3.9 billion. The port sits on Hudson Bay and offers a shorter route to some markets. It would give DP World a new access point far from congested and contested shipping lanes. The Port of Churchill would give DP World a rail line that ends somewhere uncrowded.
Rently lands $12 million for UAE rentals
Singapore proptech Rently secured a $12 million growth credit facility from January Capital. The company has processed more than AED100 million in rental contracts in the UAE. Its transaction volume is up more than fivefold year over year. The UAE rental market is digitising fast, and Rently is one of the platforms winning that shift.

Youth Orchestra Abu Dhabi is opening at Mina Zayed in January 2027. Children aged six to nine can get free music lessons, with a 600-seat concert hall and 18 rehearsal rooms waiting for them. Eighteen rehearsal rooms are standing by for six-year-olds who have not yet opened a case.
Bahraini farmers get 10-year contracts on Buri plots
Bahrain's Buri region agricultural project has completed 87% of its infrastructure. The project spans 82,000 square metres, with 12 plots allocated to Bahraini farmers on 10-year contracts. The 10-year contracts now have 82,000 square metres in which to take root.
The world has fewer remote jobs than you think
Only 4% of new remote jobs on Jobgether in September were open worldwide, down from 4.8% in August. Each worldwide posting drew 8.8 times the average number of applications. The fully remote job is becoming the working parent's lottery ticket, and the odds just got worse.
