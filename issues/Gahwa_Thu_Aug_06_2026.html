The Lead · GCC & SAUDI
Aramco just posted $33 billion in profit on a war premium it cannot control, and its CEO says the oil market stays tight into 2027 even if the Strait of Hormuz opens tomorrow.
​
The company's average realized crude price touched $108.10 a barrel, a record premium over $10 above Brent. Revenue from that single spike lifted oil income to the Saudi government by 22%, pumping nearly $50 billion into state coffers in one quarter.

Production, however, slid to 9.5 million barrels of oil equivalent per day from 12.8 million a year earlier, because the Strait of Hormuz blockade continues to strangle export volumes. Exports are now running at roughly 5 million barrels a day through the East-West pipeline to Yanbu on the Red Sea, while traffic through the strait itself has dropped to about a tenth of pre-war levels.

Free cash flow fell 19% year on year to $12.3 billion, dragged by a $13.6 billion working-capital build. The CFO called that a timing lag on government domestic price-equalization payments and expects it to reverse next quarter. Strip it out and free cash flow was $25.9 billion, up 42%. The base dividend held at $21.9 billion, payable 27 August. Capex guidance stayed at $50-55 billion for the year, with the Zuluf crude increment and Fadhili gas expansion still on track for 2026 and 2027.

US Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent told CNBC a deal to reopen Hormuz could come within a day or two, but Nasser's own supply math tells a different story. He says even if the strait opens tomorrow, it would take 18 months at 2.1 million barrels a day to rebuild inventories, keeping demand and prices firm into 2027. A previous US-Iran framework signed 17 June collapsed within weeks over a dispute about which ships use which routes.
What to do with it
If you are pricing freight, fuel, or project debt in the Gulf, Nasser's 18-month inventory thesis is the number to plug into your model, because it overrides any short-term deal optimism.

02GCC & SAUDI
Riyadh Offices Hit 97% Occupancy
Prime rents in the King Abdullah Financial District climbed 3% year on year, and grade A office rents rose 1%, according to CBRE's latest market review. More than 780 companies are now licensed under the Regional Headquarters Program.

Technology and healthcare companies together accounted for over half of all office requirements in the first half of the year. Knight Frank had already flagged that Riyadh is among a tiny group of global cities that have effectively run out of prime office space.

While the war upends shipping routes and GDP, the RHQ rule keeps forcing multinationals to put an actual lease and a nameplate in the capital. A chair and a desk in Riyadh is now a compliance cost that a global firm cannot skip. Any company still running its Saudi operations from Dubai should check its RHQ license deadline, because it needs a physical lease, not a serviced-office mailing address.

03AI & TECH
Moove Hits $2.1B Valuation with Mubadala
The vehicle-financing platform raised $250 million in a Series C round led by Mubadala Investment Company and co-led by Woven Capital, Toyota's growth fund. Moove operates roughly 42,000 vehicles across 29 cities in 13 countries and generates $420 million in annual recurring revenue.

The capital will push its autonomous-vehicle fleet business forward. Moove already manages AVs through a partnership with Waymo in Phoenix and Miami, and London is next. Its AV workforce is set to jump from around 150 to roughly 500 employees by year end.

Mubadala is backing a firm that turns cars into a recurring subscription line item, exactly the kind of asset-light, revenue-visible business that reads well in a late-cycle funding environment where everyone claims to be an AI company but this one actually just puts cars on the road. A startup that finances a car for a driver and then gets paid per trip has a revenue model that is easier to underwrite than the typical equity story, so it is worth a look from any Gulf family office seeking yield.

04CULTURE
My Syria Super App Launches for Tourism
121 Living, founded by four Syrian entrepreneurs, launched a platform that integrates hotel bookings, restaurants, transportation, tourist attractions, experiences, and food delivery. It is the first platform in Syria to support cross-border digital payments through Apple Pay, Google Pay, Visa, Mastercard, and American Express.

Visitor arrivals to Syria rose 111% in the first half of 2026 to 3.52 million, up from 1.67 million a year earlier. The app plans to expand to more than 40 verified tourism and hospitality providers.

An eight-in-one super app for a country where the tourists are arriving faster than the hotels can put up a fresh coat of paint is either brilliant timing or the most optimistic product launch of the year, and the founders seem perfectly happy with both readings. Hospitality and retail brands looking for the next under-mapped market should track Syria's visitor numbers, because 3.5 million arrivals in six months creates demand that existing supply cannot yet meet.

05MACRO & GEO
UAE Q1 GDP Slows to 3%
The UAE economy recorded 3% growth to AED 485 billion for the first quarter, down from 3.9% a year earlier. Non-oil GDP grew 4.8% and its share of the economy rose to 79.4% from 77.3%, according to the Federal Competitiveness and Statistics Center.

Financial services and insurance led with 17.3% growth, adding 2.44 percentage points to the headline number. Construction grew 8.1%, healthcare 7.7%, and ICT 5.9%. Wholesale and retail trade managed only 2.6%.

The slowdown is real but the direction of travel is still toward a less oil-dependent economy. S&P had forecast a 2.7% contraction for the full year, so a 3% print in Q1 actually beats the most bearish case. If your business depends on consumer discretionary spending in the UAE, the wholesale and retail trade figure of 2.6% growth is the number to put into your rest-of-year model.

06GCC & SAUDI
PIF Leads $55B EA Buyout
The Public Investment Fund’s $55 billion consortium takes US games publisher Electronic Arts private in what is reportedly the largest leveraged buyout in history. Silver Lake and Affinity Partners are also in the group.

The deal is backed by roughly $36.4 billion in equity and $20 billion in fully underwritten debt. PIF is rolling over its existing 9.9% stake. JPMorgan issued $15 billion of the acquisition debt in March and drew over $50 billion in orders.

A sovereign fund buying the company that makes FIFA is, according to people familiar with the matter, either a long bet that the world's biggest gaming franchise keeps growing or a very expensive way to put a trophy on the wall, and at $55 billion the answer matters a great deal. Anyone raising a gaming or entertainment fund in the region should note that PIF just set the floor for what a scaled content asset costs.

A Dubai spa opens and refuses to sell you a candle
Sohum Wellness Sanctuary opened in Dubai, positioning itself as a wellness space that pointedly does not push lifestyle products or rituals. The global wellness industry is built on selling you a $40 candle and telling you it cures anxiety, so a sanctuary that explicitly declines to monetize your calm is either the most authentic brand in town or the most subversive marketing strategy a spa has ever run.
