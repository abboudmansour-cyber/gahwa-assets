The Lead
​
​
Trump is seriously considering strikes on Iranian energy targets, and this time the Israelis might reportedly join in.
Saudi Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman got on a call with President Trump to express his direct concern over the plans, a sign of how seriously Riyadh views the risk of a wider war that could engulf its own energy infrastructure.

A U.S. official says Trump is seriously considering launching attacks against energy targets inside Iran in the next few days. According to officials, for the first time in many weeks, the Israeli military could be brought in to participate. The threat follows an Iranian missile attack on a U.S. base in Jordan on Wednesday, which Tehran reportedly launched alongside assaults on other U.S. forces in the region. Iran's Foreign Minister Abbas Araqchi has since warned Turkey, Pakistan, and Saudi officials that any fresh U.S. strike would, according to him, be met with decisive retaliation.

The Strait of Hormuz, through which a fifth of the world's oil passes, is already a live fault line. Kuwait's army confirmed it destroyed hostile drones launched by Iran at its vital facilities, the kind of spillover attack that, officials say, turns a shipping chokepoint into a theater of war. Brent crude is trading at $90.12 a barrel, but the real story is the scramble for alternative export corridors. Turkey and Iraq just signed a one-year deal to keep the Ceyhan pipeline flowing at 750,000 barrels a day, and Ankara is pushing to unlock the line's full 1.5 million bpd capacity. For Gulf producers, the math is brutal: a Hormuz closure, according to analysts, means every barrel that cannot reach a Red Sea or Turkish port is a stranded asset.
Logistics teams should stress-test alternative export routes now; reportedly, a Hormuz closure could strand millions of barrels and leave Turkey's Ceyhan pipeline as the only available outlet.

Fertiglobe Q2 Profit Hits $145 Million
Revenues jumped 92% year-on-year to $1.1 billion, and adjusted EBITDA climbed 111% to $371 million. For a company that sells nitrogen fertilizer, the numbers are as straightforward as the product: a tight global supply picture and rising agricultural demand are doing the work.

Fertiglobe, a joint venture between ADNOC and OCI, is also hunting acquisitions across four continents, a signal that it sees the current cycle as a buying window. The $145 million profit is a reminder that the Gulf's commodity strength extends well beyond crude, and that fertilizer is the quiet beneficiary of every supply-chain disruption that sends food prices higher. Agri-commodity traders should note that Fertiglobe's expansion plans will increase supply from a low-cost Gulf base as global food security concerns mount.

FINTECH & CRYPTO
UAE's Epic Markets Raises $10 Million Pre-Seed
Karatage is the sole investor in a $10 million pre-Seed round for a multi-asset brokerage that has not yet launched. That is a very large check to write on a founding team that only incorporated this year, but the pitch, institutional-grade trade execution for retail investors, is the same one that made Robinhood a household name. Kevin Kimmel and Brian Seegers are betting the MENA retail trader is tired of clunky interfaces and delayed fills. A $10 million pre-Seed is the market agreeing, loudly, that retail brokerage in the Gulf is still a product problem, not a demand problem. Fintech founders should watch Epic's execution; a successful launch would validate the thesis that MENA retail traders will pay for speed.

GCC & SAUDI
Marafiq to Supply Water for Oman Green Iron
Marafiq will build and own a desalination plant in Duqm to supply industrial water to Meranti Green Steel's 2.5-million-tonne-per-year green iron complex. The deal answers a question the hydrogen economy keeps asking: where does the water come from?

Green iron needs green hydrogen, and green hydrogen needs vast quantities of desalinated water. By locking in a dedicated plant rather than pulling from the municipal grid, the Meranti project is building a closed-loop industrial system in the desert, and Marafiq gets a anchor customer that needs water as urgently as it needs iron ore. Industrial project developers should study this deal structure; dedicated desalination tied to a single offtaker is a model that unlocks financing.

GCC & SAUDI
L&T Joins PDO's $6 Billion EPC Pool
Larsen & Toubro has been selected to join Petroleum Development Oman's pool of approved EPC service providers, a roster that will bid on up to eight projects with a combined value of $6 billion. The Indian engineering giant is not new to the Gulf, but getting onto PDO's shortlist is a license to compete for work that will define Oman's upstream oil and gas infrastructure for the next decade. For the other contractors already in the pool, a heavyweight like L&T walking through the door means the fee negotiation just got less comfortable. Contractors in Oman should price upcoming bids assuming L&T will be aggressive; the $6 billion pipeline is now a more crowded race.
