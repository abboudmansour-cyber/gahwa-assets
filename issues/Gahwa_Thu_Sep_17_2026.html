The Lead
​
Saudi Arabia's East-West pipeline could be half back online within days, but Aramco is already moving 4 million barrels a day through the Gulf.
The faster fix is a direct response to the Kingdom's need to keep crude flowing to Red Sea terminals where western export stockpiles stand at 14 million barrels. Aramco has already doubled daily loadings from Ras Tanura and Juaymah to two VLCCs, about 4 million barrels, over the past week, and sold around 20 million barrels to Asian refiners for September and October loading.

The damage to the pipeline, which crosses the Kingdom from eastern fields to the Red Sea, reportedly forced a scramble to reroute crude through the Gulf. Another 12 million barrels sit in storage at Egypt's Sidi Kerir and Ain Sokhna, positions that let Aramco keep serving European and American buyers without waiting for pipeline repairs. TankerTrackers estimated 8.7 million barrels loaded on Monday alone, with Gulf and Red Sea exports up 77% over two weeks before the pipeline was hit.

The Houthis told US officials in Oman they would not target American or Israeli ships, pledging to spare commercial vessels except Saudi-owned ones and uphold the 2025 ceasefire. But the Kingdom is not taking that promise at face value. Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman asked Washington for direct military support against the Houthis, and the US declined direct action, offering intelligence-sharing and targeting support instead.

The pipeline restoration and loading surge matter because Saudi Arabia is the world's largest crude exporter and the East-West line is its bypass route around the Strait of Hormuz. If half the line returns quickly, buyers in Europe and the Atlantic basin face less risk of losing Saudi barrels. Aramco's move to move 4 million barrels a day through the Gulf without the pipeline working at full capacity shows the Kingdom has a workaround that is already functioning. The faster repair timeline and doubled Gulf loadings mean Aramco is telling the market not to price in a prolonged loss of Saudi barrels.

The AI Race
​
Dubai releases free deepfake detector
Dubai's Electronic Security Center released SARAAB, an open-source AI model that detects deepfake videos. The Emirati-built model is free to download from developer platforms and is the first such tool developed by a government entity in the Arab region. DESC also launched a Cyber Skills Framework and an ISR Auditor Certification Program at GISEC Global 2026.

Deepfakes have become a real weapon in regional disinformation and fraud. SARAAB's open-source licence means banks, media outlets and corporate security teams can embed it without paying a vendor. A free detection tool flips deepfake defence from a premium service into a basic utility. The auditor certification adds a governance layer, so compliance teams can be tested on the same standard. Dubai just turned deepfake detection into public infrastructure, which lowers the cost of every Gulf KYC review.
Business
​
Modon seeks EGP 35 billion for Ras El Hekma
Modon Holding is seeking an EGP 35 billion (USD 672 million) syndicated loan for Wadi Yemm infrastructure in Ras El Hekma. FAB Misr is arranging the facility. The developer has already tendered over EGP 80 billion in utilities, infrastructure and residential construction within Wadi Yemm. Contracted sales reached about EGP 65 billion in the first eight months of 2026.
Aldar and Mubadala buy Masdar City Square
Aldar and Mubadala acquired Masdar City Square for AED 918 million. The deal lifts their combined Masdar City portfolio to AED 4.7 billion. The acquisition deepens the two Abu Dhabi entities' exposure to sustainable urban development assets. Masdar City remains the UAE's flagship clean energy project.
ExxonMobil approves $5 billion Rose CCS project
ExxonMobil secured regulatory approval to proceed with the Rose carbon capture and storage project in Texas. The project is valued at more than $5 billion. It is one of the largest CCS investments in the US. The approval signals continued oil major commitment to carbon management infrastructure despite the Gulf supply shock.
Dubai tenders Abu Hail stormwater upgrade
Dubai tendered the Abu Hail stormwater upgrade project. It includes a pumping station with total discharge capacity of 26 cubic metres per second. The project is part of the emirate's broader infrastructure hardening after record flooding events. Bids are now open for contractors.
CMA caps money market funds' offshore holdings at 5%
Saudi Arabia's Capital Market Authority capped public money market funds' offshore investments at 5% of net assets. Managers have two years to comply, with funds above 20% offshore exposure forced below that threshold within six months. Morgan Stanley projected the move could draw USD 4.3 billion in additional inflows if foreign ownership caps rise to 75%.

03
Off Paper
​
Tarabut's 5 billion API calls
Tarabut has processed more than 5 billion API calls across Saudi Arabia, the UAE and Bahrain. The open banking platform started in Bahrain in 2017 and moved its headquarters to Riyadh in 2025. Its clients include American Express Saudi Arabia, Saudi National Bank and SAB. Five billion calls means Gulf consumers are already sharing their bank data with apps they trust.
Saudi SME strategy promises 500,000 jobs
Saudi Arabia's Cabinet approved a national SME strategy with 13 initiatives. The plan aims for 500,000 jobs and a 35% share of GDP for small and medium enterprises by 2030. This comes as the Kingdom pushes to make the private sector the main engine of employment. This is a real jobs promise for the generation now entering the Saudi workforce.
Rize reaches 200,000 tenants
Saudi proptech Rize signed a $50 million facility from Jadwa Investment to fund its Rent Now, Pay Later model. The company works with over 200,000 tenants and 3,000 landlords. The Saudi residential rental market is estimated at about SAR 150 billion a year. Paying rent in instalments is becoming a normal tool for Saudi tenants, not a sign of distress.
​
O Beach Dubai shuts Marina venue
The Ibiza-born beach club closed its Dubai Marina venue after redevelopment around the location made it impossible to operate as planned. The brand said it preferred to shut rather than compromise on the experience. No word yet on a new Dubai location, but the current site is not coming back. Dubai's hospitality map keeps getting redrawn by construction, and even Ibiza pedigree cannot outrun the cranes.
The Last Sip
​
Which Gulf city has more registered cats than cars?
The answer
Kuwait City is often said to have more stray cats than registered cars, with estimates in the hundreds of thousands. The city struggles to feed them all, and some neighbourhoods have volunteer feeding stations. The government has run several spay and neuter campaigns but the cats keep winning. The cats figured out Gulf residential parking before any human driver did.
