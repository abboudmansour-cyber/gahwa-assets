The Lead · MACRO & GEO
Saudi Arabia reportedly locked in a three-way defense pact that says an attack on one is an attack on all.
​
Saudi Arabia, Turkey, and Pakistan signed the Mecca Joint Defence Agreement at Al-Safa Palace in Makkah on Friday. The pact, according to the document, states that an armed attack against any one of the three nations will be treated as an attack against all. Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman, Turkish President Recep Tayyip Erdoğan, and Pakistani Prime Minister Muhammad Shehbaz Sharif attended the signing, according to officials.

The agreement does not specify the military commitments each country would undertake if a member is attacked. Saudi Deputy Minister for Public Diplomacy Rayed Krimly said the pact does not represent a military axis or a sectarian bloc, and is not linked to any nuclear ambitions or an arms race. Turkey stressed the deal is not aimed at any particular state and does not contradict its NATO obligations.

The signing comes as the US-Iran war enters its fifth month. Gulf states have reportedly absorbed drone and ballistic missile strikes on airports, financial districts, and oil infrastructure. Saudi Arabia has deployed 108 Patriot launchers and four THAAD batteries to intercept incoming fire. Officials from several countries described the pact as a historic step toward collective security. Gulf officials have told Washington they are running low on interceptors and must increasingly choose which threats to engage. The deal diversifies Saudi security partnerships at a moment when the Pentagon is stretched across two theaters.

03GCC & SAUDI
RAK Properties Profit Halves, Backlog Grows
RAK Properties’ net profit after tax fell more than half year on year to AED77 million in the first six months of 2026. Revenue dropped just under a third to AED533 million. In the second quarter alone, net profit after tax fell more than 60 percent, while revenue declined more than a third to AED255 million.

Yet the company's sales backlog rose 26 percent to a record AED3.3 billion. The developer handed over 264 homes in the first half and, according to the company, is on track to deliver 1,400 units by the end of the year, its largest annual handover total to date. CEO Sameh Muhtadi called the six-month period a transitional phase, not a downturn.

The Ras Al Khaimah property market has slowed since the US-Iran war began. The Nobu Residences and W Hotel projects pushed back their completion dates. The Wynn casino resort delayed its opening to September next year, and executives added $600 million to its budget, half of which they attributed directly to the war. The stock is down 19 percent on the Abu Dhabi Securities Exchange year to date. Watch the handover pace in H2; hitting 1,400 units would convert a large chunk of the AED3.3 billion backlog into revenue and reset the narrative.

04FINTECH & CRYPTO
US Sanctions Two More Iranian Crypto Exchanges
The US Treasury Department sanctioned two more Iranian cryptocurrency exchanges under its Economic Fury campaign. Treasury traced more than $3 million in transfers between one exchange, Shelbit, and wallets linked to Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps.

The sanctions extend a pattern that has accelerated since the US-Iran war began. Tehran has turned to crypto exchanges to move funds across borders as its access to the dollar-based financial system remains severed. The Treasury has now blacklisted a string of platforms that it says act as conduits for sanctions evasion.

For compliance officers at Gulf exchanges, each new designation redraws the map of counterparty risk. The tracing of on-chain transfers directly to IRGC-linked wallets means blockchain analytics firms can flag exposure within hours. Exchanges with any indirect Iranian flow now face a binary choice: cut the wallet or risk secondary sanctions. Update your compliance screening rules to capture the newly designated wallet addresses and any platforms interacting with them.

World Bank Slashes Gulf Growth Forecast
The World Bank lowered its 2026 growth forecast for the Gulf from 4.4 percent to 1.3 percent, citing the US-Iran war. Kuwait International Airport's passenger terminal was damaged by drones and a fuel-tank fire. Qatar's forces intercepted 96 percent of incoming fire on the first day of war using F-15s and NASAMS. Revisit your Gulf revenue assumptions for 2026; a 1.3 percent growth print is effectively a recession for economies accustomed to 4 percent-plus expansion.
