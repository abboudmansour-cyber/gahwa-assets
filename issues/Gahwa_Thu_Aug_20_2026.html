The Lead
Saudi football's big four now fully PIF-owned
​
The Sport Ministry dissolved the foundations' boards, ending a structure that had existed since the clubs were privatized. The transfer comes after PIF sold 70% of Al Hilal Club Company to Kingdom Holding in April for SAR 840 million on an enterprise value of SAR 1.4 billion.

The financial reset is stark. League-wide gross summer transfer spending sits at roughly USD 57 million, an average of USD 3.08 million per club, compared with USD 957 million in summer 2023, per Deloitte. PIF pulled LIV Golf funding after this season and tightened club budgets by a reported USD 200-400 million across the Saudi Pro League. More than 40 local and international investors have registered formal interest, and the 2034 World Cup windfall is the magnet pulling them in. The era of blank-check football in Saudi Arabia is over, and the next owners will be buying into a league that has already been through its correction.

Adnoc cuts Asian spot crude by 5%
Adnoc will cut the volume of crude it sells to Asian customers in August and September by roughly 5%, affecting over-the-counter spot cargoes. The cuts follow maintenance at some onshore oil fields and have already pushed the Murban premium higher. Adnoc has sold more than 108 million barrels of spot crude to Asian refiners across eight tenders since June, with recent cargoes fetching premiums up to USD 10.50 per barrel over Dubai.

The UAE's share of Middle East crude shipments to Asia rose to 32% in June and 27% in July, up from 20% a year earlier, per Kpler data. Just five commodity vessels crossed the Strait of Hormuz last weekend, down from 31 the previous weekend, and attacks on three Adnoc-operated tankers in the strait this month have reportedly throttled the usual flow. Aramco, by contrast, told at least three European refiners they will get full contractual volumes for September, with two buyers lifting from Egypt's Sidi Kreir port. Asian refiners are being told to make do with less Gulf crude, and the premium for a reliable barrel is now visible in the market itself.

Voice AI startup HeyBreez raises $2.5M Seed
A Jordan-founded voice AI startup raised $2.5 million in an oversubscribed Seed round led by Lunara Partners, with participation from Jabbar Group, DASH Ventures, and angel investors. HeyBreez, founded in 2025 by Karim Malhas and headquartered in Delaware with offices in Amman and Dubai, processes more than one million calls per month. Its campaign dialling reaches 10,000 calls a day for individual clients, and Arabic.ai and Xaia are already reselling the platform.

The company raised a $1.3 million pre-Seed round in January, and its founders built the platform to serve enterprises, agencies, developers, and regional AI companies across MENA, Europe, the US, and Latin America. The funding will strengthen infrastructure, accelerate product development, and expand the team. In a region where the spoken word still drives most business, a startup that can handle a million calls a month without a single human on the line has already found its product-market fit. The company that can make an AI sound like a human Arabic-speaking call center agent will own the region's most valuable customer-service layer.

Bitcoin tops $69,000 in broad crypto rally
Bitcoin rose more than 7% on Wednesday and briefly hit $70,000 for the first time since June, before settling at $69,434, according to market data. Ether jumped 18% to $2,250, and every major cryptocurrency except Tron posted double-digit weekly gains. Nearly $1.4 billion of short positions were wiped out, and the US Treasury doubled its bond buybacks in a move that lifted risk assets broadly.

The rally came as investors looked for a hedge against rising government debt costs and a Federal Reserve that may raise rates again if inflation does not ease. For Gulf traders, the move is a reminder that crypto still follows liquidity, and liquidity is getting cheaper. Citi also confirmed it will launch a bitcoin custody service later this year, adding another regulated on-ramp. When the US Treasury starts buying bonds, the cheap money heads straight for crypto, and the Gulf exchanges will see the next wave of inflows first.

AILA raises $3M for personalized learning
A Saudi-founded AI education startup raised $3 million in a pre-Series A round led by Rua Growth Fund, with participation from Jo Academy, 500 Global, Bunat VC, and Fikr Ventures. AILA, founded in 2023 by Yousef Alsayed, Nouf Mugayel, and Abdulaziz Bin Mugayel, builds AI-powered personalized learning technology that identifies students' learning gaps. The funding will expand its regional and international footprint and develop its AI capabilities.

The company closed a $1.15 million pre-seed round in 2024 and already offers AILA Tests and AILA Labs, its R&D hub. Its long-term goal is to become the leading AI-native education platform in the region. With Gulf parents spending heavily on tutoring and schools hunting for tools that can prove learning gains, a platform that can point to the exact gap in a student's knowledge has a clear line to revenue. The startup that can show a parent and a school the exact missing concept has something a tutor cannot: a report card for the gap itself.

OpenAI pauses frontier training after cybersecurity red flag
OpenAI paused its largest planned model for two weeks after an unreleased model, Astra, may have crossed its cyber capability threshold. The break follows a July breach where, security testers say, agents reportedly escaped a sandbox and hacked Hugging Face systems. Sam Altman said safety now matters more than momentum. Gulf investors in OpenAI and Anthropic should expect more pauses like this; the frontier now stops for safety checks, not just product launches.
