The Lead
​
UAE pumps record
The UAE pumped a record 5.3 million barrels a day in September, up 400,000 barrels from August, according to IEA figures cited by Bloomberg. Crude alone exceeded 4.4 million barrels a day.

The surge puts Abu Dhabi well on its way to its 5 million barrel daily capacity target by 2027. That is three years after leaving OPEC to escape production limits. The Adcop pipeline is now carrying roughly 1.8 million barrels a day to Fujairah, bypassing the Strait of Hormuz entirely.

The extra volume is not coming from new fields alone. In September the UAE drew down above-ground inventories by more than 270,000 barrels a day, to an all-time low. With Houthi attacks on Saudi infrastructure and missiles landing near Riyadh's airport, the UAE is reportedly proving that Gulf oil can still reach buyers. That holds even when the region is on fire. The real test is whether it can sustain these levels without emptying its buffer, and reportedly the first casualty will be any remaining OPEC discipline.

02Chips in New York
GlobalFoundries lands Japan-funded New York chip plants
GlobalFoundries, the Mubadala-controlled chipmaker, will operate two new semiconductor plants in Malta, New York, under a US-Japan initiative worth up to $21 billion. Japan supplies the funding, while GlobalFoundries provides the technology and runs the fabs.

The first plant is set to come online in 2029 with roughly $10 billion in initial funding, adding about 900,000 300mm wafers of annual capacity. The US Commerce Department will take a roughly 4 percent equity stake in return for amending its CHIPS Act award. It is reallocating $955 million to silicon photonics, silicon-germanium and advanced packaging work in New York and Vermont.

The deal marks a striking moment for an Abu Dhabi sovereign fund. It now sits inside the American industrial policy machine, at exactly the moment Washington is trying to decouple chip supply chains from Asia. Mubadala already raised $1.9 billion in June by selling GlobalFoundries shares at nearly double the price of a March offering. So the company is funding itself.

The real prize is relevance. GlobalFoundries becomes the only Middle East-owned firm running US national-security chip plants. That position gives Abu Dhabi leverage in Washington far beyond anything a lobbying budget could buy.

03Sanctions waiver
US extends Lukoil waiver until 2027
Lukoil gets a sanctions lifeline to April 2027. The US Treasury extended a sanctions waiver on Russian oil giant Lukoil, allowing service operations outside Russia to continue until April 25, 2027. The general licence lets Lukoil petrol stations keep serving customers, including in the United States.

The licence bars any transfer of funds to Russian entities, including the central bank, national wealth fund and Ministry of Finance. A separate licence covers Lukoil's Bulgarian subsidiaries for the same period.

The extension comes a week after President Trump announced a deal with Vladimir Putin. It allows Russia to supply 300,000 tonnes of diesel to US and global markets immediately, with 500,000 more in November. A million tonnes each month follow after that. Diesel prices in the US still sit at $6.31 a gallon, against $3.65 a year earlier.

The IEA meanwhile expects oil market balances to stay in deficit well into 2027. That means these waivers are less about generosity than about keeping the global diesel supply from collapsing entirely.

04Receipts, not résumés
Foodics backs Funding Souq for restaurant lending
Restaurant technology firm Foodics has taken a significant stake in Saudi fintech Funding Souq, a Shariah-compliant SME lending platform regulated by SAMA and DFSA. The strategic investment is of undisclosed size. The deal aims to create what Funding Souq founder Martin Jaouni calls Saudi Arabia's first fully automated business loan.

Foodics plans to deploy $100 million in financing through the platform in its first year. Funding Souq claims 10,000 investors already on board and a goal of $1 billion in SME financing over three years.

The partnership closes a gap food-tech firms have long complained about. Restaurants have sales data but no credit history, and lenders have credit models but no access to real-time revenue data. By combining Foodics's point-of-sale data with Funding Souq's lending rails, the two are building a credit engine that runs on actual receipts, not balance sheets.

For the Gulf, small restaurants are often exactly the kind of cash-flow-positive but collateral-poor businesses that conventional banks ignore. This is both a product and an argument.

05Overseas citizen, no longer overseas
Bollywood's Imran Khan faces India deportation deadline
Indian authorities have given actor Imran Khan until October 28 to leave the country. This follows a Foreigners Regional Registration Office notice alleging he violated the Foreigners Act. The notice stems from his participation in an October 10 protest in Mumbai calling for the chief election commissioner to resign.

Khan holds US citizenship and an Overseas Citizen of India card. He was detained by Mumbai Police at the demonstration along with his partner Lekha Washington. The notice followed a complaint by a BJP leader.

His legal team is preparing a petition to the Bombay High Court, expected to be registered by Friday or Monday.

The immigration case lands just as Khan stages a comeback. His new film, directed by Danish Aslam and reportedly streaming on Netflix, has wrapped production but no release date. Whether any studio will now schedule it is itself a question.

The AI Race
​
Jais 3 speaks the Arab world
MBZUAI and G42's Inception42 launched Jais 3, an open language model suite adding speech recognition, regional dialect support and multi-step task handling. Six models span from 900 million to 375 billion parameters. The speech model covers dialects from 19 Arab countries.

The release marks the first time a major open Arabic model has attempted full dialect coverage at this scale. It does so rather than defaulting to Modern Standard Arabic. The team tested the models across 24 benchmarks. For Gulf developers and enterprises, this lowers the barrier to building voice assistants that actually understand Gulf Arabic. Abu Dhabi is investing in sovereign AI capability precisely so Gulf businesses do not have to send their data to Silicon Valley or Beijing. A homegrown model that finally speaks the region's dialects is the whole argument for sovereign AI made in silicon.

Business
The deals and the money moving markets, in the Gulf and beyond.
Foreign investors pull $2.8 billion from MENA equities in Q3
Foreign investors withdrew $2.8 billion from MENA stocks in the third quarter, more than five times the outflow in Q2. Saudi Arabia alone lost $1.5 billion. War risk and rising global rates kept overseas funds on the sidelines. The pullback suggests regional markets are still trading on geopolitics rather than fundamentals.
DFSA fines Hayman-Woodward $454,367 for six years of unlicensed work
Dubai Financial Services Authority fined immigration firm Hayman-Woodward Global Mobility Services $454,367. The firm provided unauthorized services to 71 clients between 2018 and 2025 from the DIFC without proper registration. The penalty was cut 30% after the firm agreed to settle.
MENA startup funding jumps 108% to $1.65 billion in Q3
MENA startup funding surged 108 percent quarter-on-quarter to $1.65 billion in Q3. Saudi Arabia attracted more than $1 billion of that. Fintech dominated at $1.03 billion. The rebound reverses a long funding slump and points to renewed investor appetite despite regional instability.
Aramco's decade-old contingency plan kept oil flowing through war, Al Kindy says
Adnoc board director Abdulmunim Al Kindy said the company began preparing for major disruptions 10 to 15 years ago, investing in backup facilities that made no commercial sense at the time. That investment is now paying off as Adnoc targets 5 million barrels a day by 2027. The admission underscores how Gulf oil producers have quietly built resilience into their systems.
Sea freight rates collapse as Hormuz crisis forces land bridge rethink
Shipping brokers report that hiring an oil tanker from the US to China now costs around $80 million, compared to roughly $74 million to launch a SpaceX Falcon 9. The numbers show how war risk premiums are redrawing global energy logistics. Gulf exporters are increasingly turning to east-coast ports and rail links to bypass the strait entirely.

Off Paper
​
Oman's Pink Step festival raises breast cancer awareness
The Pink Step 2026 festival, a three-day event in Nizwa, has kicked off to raise awareness about breast cancer in Oman. The festival combines health education with public engagement. It is the kind of community health push that Gulf states are accelerating as they shift from treating disease to preventing it. Nizwa is treating a health campaign the way it might treat a heritage season.
Siemens grants power-grid software to four Omani universities
Siemens granted power-grid planning software licenses to four Omani universities. The move hands students the same tools grid operators use to plan and run a network. It is a small investment in the people who will keep Oman's lights on as it becomes a logistics alternative to Hormuz. Siemens has entrusted the national grid's intellectual machinery to students who have not yet graduated.
K18 launches a scalp serum with a hair retention claim
K18's new FutureIQ scalp serum helped testers retain roughly 19,000 more strands of hair after three months. The brand is positioning the product around what it calls hair longevity. A serum that counts retained strands the way a central bank counts reserves.

Louvre Abu Dhabi opens Stories of Paper
The Louvre Abu Dhabi will open its next exhibition, Stories of Paper, showcasing 100 artworks and objects across a medium that spans every civilisation. The museum holds roughly 700 works plus 300 loans from French partners. Its iconic dome, 180 metres across, is a geometric wonder of 7,850 stars. Louvre Abu Dhabi has assembled the flimsiest material known to art and given it a dome of 7,850 stars.
