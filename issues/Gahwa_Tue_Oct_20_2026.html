The Lead
​
Riyadh Postpones Davos
Saudi Arabia has postponed the Future Investment Initiative, the October summit known as Davos in the Desert, to 2027. CEO Princess Maha bint Mishari Al Saud announced the decision, citing a commitment to a full-scale event. The postponement follows a week of Houthi missile and drone attacks, including strikes on Riyadh's King Khalid International Airport, reportedly. Intersec moved to October 2027, and Saudi Build and Saudi Elenex pushed to November 2027. The EU-GCC summit originally set for 24 October has been postponed. The Asian Tour's PIF Saudi International golf event, due 18-21 November, is shelved indefinitely over what organisers call organisational and logistical challenges. The Urban Cycling World Championships, scheduled for 3-7 November in Riyadh, will not proceed, per the Union Cycliste Internationale.

Airline schedules tell the sharper story. Gulf carriers have cut flights to Saudi Arabia through Saturday 24 October. Flydubai suspended Riyadh, Dammam, and Abha routes. Qatar Airways cut Riyadh and Dammam, and reduced Jeddah to four daily flights. Etihad suspended all Dammam and Riyadh flights and some Jeddah operations. King Khalid International Airport is requiring airlines to request permission before landing or takeoff.

Global capital had already been slow to commit to the Kingdom this autumn. The summit was due to draw BlackRock's Larry Fink and JPMorgan Chase's Jamie Dimon. Its disappearance, even temporarily, leaves Saudi Arabia without its most effective stage for reassuring international investors that Vision 2030 is still on track. The Kingdom can rebuild a terminal faster than it can rebuild that confidence.

02Taqa's return
Taqa Taps Debt Markets After Going Private
Taqa is marketing a two-part USD bond, its first debt market tap since leaving the Abu Dhabi exchange on 1 September. The long five-year green tranche prices at about 130 basis points over US Treasuries. A 10-year tranche sits at about 140 basis points. Moody's rates the name Aa3 and Fitch AA-.

The proceeds help fund a AED 75 billion capital spending plan by 2030. AED 40 billion goes to transmission and distribution, AED 35 billion to power generation and desalination. Settling 26 October, the bonds mature April 2032 and October 2036.

Taqa is testing investor appetite for Abu Dhabi paper after the L'Imad subsidiary Abu Dhabi Power Corporation bought out remaining shareholders. A clean issue at these spreads would signal that global credit markets still see UAE utility risk as close to sovereign quality.

03Rifles to Ras Laffan
Qatar Plans Defence Production Push
Qatar will announce a defence production plant. Defence Minister Sheikh Saoud bin Abdulrahman said the country would team with a regional partner and countries including the US, UK, and France. Qatar was the world's fourth-largest arms importer between 2021 and 2025. It has had little meaningful localised defence manufacturing, producing rifles and small calibre ammunition.

The move comes after parts of Ras Laffan Industrial City were destroyed during the Iran war, and Qatar declared force majeure on some LNG contracts. Doha is now trying to build what it has spent a decade buying.
Source: The National Business

04Supply chain fix
UAE Eases E-Invoicing Rules Ahead of Rollout
The UAE Finance Ministry has introduced a temporary 24-month exclusion for intra-tax-group transactions, starting 1 January 2027. The amendment removes the requirement to store e-invoice data within the UAE, shifting focus to retention and accessibility. It also removes the requirement to notify the Federal Tax Authority of system failure within two business days.

The deadline for appointing an accredited service provider has been extended to 30 October from 1 July. This applies to businesses with annual revenue of Dh50 million or more. The ministry is giving the first phase more breathing room before enforcement begins.
Source: The National Business

05Stablecoin defence
Tether CEO Pushes Back on Iran Accusations
Tether CEO Paolo Ardoino is defending against accusations that the firm's stablecoins fueled financial activity in Iran. Ardoino claims the senator's investigation misses positive work that has led to lower Iran volumes. The fight comes as Washington scrutinises stablecoin flows across the Gulf.

The AI Race
​
Anthropic Buys Compute, Not Just Goodwill
Nscale is negotiating to sell Anthropic about $45 billion of computing power. The contract would fill a second facility at the Monarch data centre campus in West Virginia with 460 megawatts of Nvidia Vera Rubin AI chips. For Nscale, the deal arrives at a delicate moment. Microsoft has already cancelled its Essex UK contract after grid connection slipped into the 2030s. Neocloud names like CoreWeave, Nebius, and IREN each fell more than 20% last week.

Anthropic is the anchor that makes the rest of the pitch sound real. A $45 billion compute bill shows the company is not renting goodwill. It is building a moat of chips while its smaller rivals are still praying for power. The deal raises the price of staying competitive in AI, and it makes Gulf-backed clouds and their choice of anchor tenants the deciding question, not the availability of capital.

Off Paper
​
Abu Dhabi's quiet asset manager boom
Fourteen financial firms with over $5 trillion in combined assets plan to set up in the Abu Dhabi Global Market. They follow Pantheon, Blue Owl Capital, and Vista Equity Partners, while Capital Group has already secured a licence. This is less a trickle than a modest announcement of several trillion dollars seeking a new postal code.
Bahrain's Sitra grid gets quietly stronger
Bahrain has awarded two contracts for new 400kV grid substations in Sitra. One covers transformer works and the other covers feeder cables. The work is invisible and unglamorous. Bahrain is spending on transformer works and feeder cables, which is as close as a government gets to buying silence.
​
Saint Levant Headlines Dopa World
A new Dubai festival called Dopa World is betting that Arabic R&B, hip-hop, and trap can fill a December night in Expo City. Saint Levant, the Palestinian-Jordanian artist, is the headliner. The pitch is that Gen Z wants its own scene on the bill, not just imported global residencies. Dopa World is placing a headliner on the stage and a bet that Gen Z knows the difference between imported and homegrown.
