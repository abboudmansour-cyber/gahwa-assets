The Lead
​
Brent posts sharpest weekly drop since Covid start
Brent settled at $98.32 a barrel on Friday, but the week told the real story: a fall of more than 13%. That was the steepest since the Covid pandemic began. WTI sank 12.7% on the week after both benchmarks tumbled more than 8% on Wednesday's midterm results.

The selloff reflects a market suddenly pricing an end to the Iran war, or at least a pause. Tehran says three more ships were struck in the Strait of Hormuz on Thursday, and the Houthis declared a blockade on Saudi shipping in July, keeping the supply threat very much alive.

Reportedly, For Gulf producers, the political signal is the one to watch. A Democratic Congress has already signalled it may curb the war. Any diplomatic opening could pull the risk premium out of prices faster than any Opec+ decision. A market betting on peace is pricing in exactly the outcome Gulf budgets were built to fear: normalcy, and the lower prices that come with it.
Source: The National Business

02Energy security
Reportedly, Siemens redesigning Gulf power plants for drone attacks
Reportedly, Siemens Energy is redesigning Gulf power plants to withstand drone and missile attacks, with protective shelters and barriers. Parts of new plants may possibly move underground. The company has about 100 gigawatts of installed capacity in the region and 15 gigawatts of potential projects in the pipeline.

The redesign is a direct response to the US-Iran war that began in February. Shipments through the Strait of Hormuz have been disrupted, pushing Siemens to move large equipment in smaller sections for on-site assembly. The company is sold out of gas turbines and expanding factories, including a $150 million transformer plant in Dammam.

Regional power generation capacity will need to increase by about 50% over the next five to six years. This is according to Middle East and Africa MD Hussein Shoukry. That means the redesign is not a one-off fix but the new baseline for every utility build in the Gulf. Every new power project in the region now carries a hard security line item. That cost will show up in the price of electricity and the tender specifications for years.
Source: The National Business

03Buyer's market, honestly
Dubai buyers find more choice, better deals
Eighty percent of the villa communities ValuStrat tracks recorded stable property values in September 2026. 41.4% of Dubai home sales in the third quarter involved properties priced below Dh1 million. Dubai's population has grown by 226,885 this year to 4.8 million.

Developers are responding with more flexible terms: some plans ask for 20% down and 80% at handover. Others stretch instalments over seven to eight years with monthly payments from Dh6,799. Savills recorded 669 transactions above Dh10 million in the quarter.

The market has shifted from a seller's sprint to a more balanced field, with one broker describing it as a buyer's market in some pockets. The Dubai Land Department's First Time Home Buyer programme now covers eligible homes below Dh5 million with priority access to launches. First-time buyers now have a window where choice is up and monthly payment plans are being written around them, not around the investor.
Source: Gulf News

04Down round, relatively
OpenAI reportedly raises $11bn at $1.25tn valuation
OpenAI has reportedly raised an additional $11 billion as part of a funding round that is not yet closed. The round is expected to finalise in the coming weeks. The round values the company at $1.25 trillion, according to reports.

That figure sits well below the $30 billion raise Bloomberg reported OpenAI was targeting in late September. The gap suggests investors are now pricing AI companies more carefully after this week's brutal tech selloff, which wiped nearly $2.5 trillion off the market.

For Gulf sovereign funds with AI exposure, the message is that the top of the market may be lower than it looked in September. PIF's identifiable AI exposure is estimated at $12 billion and Mubadala's at $24.3 billion. The reported valuation is closer to the $12 billion of PIF's AI exposure than it is to the $30 billion target from September.
Source: TechCrunch AI

05Neutral ground rules
Russia-US-Ukraine talks set for Tuesday in Abu Dhabi
Vladimir Putin confirmed the next round of trilateral Russia-US-Ukraine talks is expected to begin on Tuesday in Abu Dhabi, according to the Tass news agency. The Russian security council meeting on the talks included senior officials Vladimir Medinsky, Yuri Ushakov and Sergei Ryabkov.

The announcement comes as President Trump predicted an agreement with Russia on Ukraine could encompass all of Europe. The Abu Dhabi venue cements the UAE's standing as the region's principal neutral table for talks that move global security.

For Gulf diplomats and businesses, the talks carry a second signal: the UAE is now the default address for the world's hardest conversations. That brings both prestige and the quiet expectation that it can deliver. Every major negotiation that lands in Abu Dhabi adds to the argument that the Gulf, not Geneva, is where the world now goes to talk.
Source: Middle East Monitor

The AI Race
​
Aramco buys into the AI grid
Aramco has taken a 49% stake in HUMAIN, the Saudi AI firm backed by PIF, through a wholly-owned subsidiary. The Public Investment Fund keeps 51% and stays in control. HUMAIN is targeting 3 gigawatts of total data centre capacity by 2030. It will use Aramco Digital and existing infrastructure to speed up cloud centres and enterprise products.

The deal plants the oil giant firmly in the physical layer of the AI economy. The move says the next barrel is a server rack. Aramco has the balance sheet to outlast most of the tenants. It is the clearest sign yet that Gulf energy is converting petrodollars into compute. It puts Saudi Arabia's largest company behind the kingdom's AI champion, giving its data centre build a balance sheet most rivals cannot match.
Business
​
Dana Gas profit jumps 50% on Kurdistan output
Dana Gas posted a $45 million third-quarter net profit, up from $30 million a year earlier. Revenue rose 42% to $119 million. Its share of Kurdistan production climbed 18% to 47,550 barrels of oil equivalent a day. The $1.1 billion KM250 expansion lifted Khor Mor gas processing capacity by half. The barrels were lifted; the cash was not.
Taqa shortlists 17 for Mirfa-Liwa water scheme
Taqa has shortlisted 17 contractors for the Mirfa-Liwa water transmission project in Abu Dhabi's Western region. The scheme covers distribution networks for Liwa East, Liwa West, Madinat Zayed and Seih Al-Kheir. A shortlist this large suggests the local pipeline is deep.
Kraft Heinz exits North American coffee
Kraft Heinz agreed to sell its North American coffee division, including Maxwell House and Gevalia, to One Rock Capital Partners for $650 million. Kraft Heinz keeps a 30% stake and will use roughly $500 million of after-tax proceeds to pay down debt. Maxwell House is being sold to pay down debt, a decision with the aftertaste of arithmetic.
MakeMyTrip buys into Quick Ride
Indian travel giant MakeMyTrip acquired a stake in Quick Ride, a daily commute platform. The move pushes it into high-frequency ground transport as its core air business softens. The daily commute is where the frequency is, even if the ticket price is small.
Saudi nine-month deficit overshoots 2026 budget by SR79bn
Saudi Arabia's deficit has already run SR79 billion above the full-year budget with three months of the year still to go. Oil income is weakening while financing costs climb.
