The Lead
​
Iran and Oman just sketched an 11km-wide shipping corridor through the Strait of Hormuz, and the entire oil market caught its breath.
On Tuesday, five commodity vessels transited the strait, still far below the ten-day average of fifteen, so the framework is a blueprint, not a reopening.

Both crude contracts tumbled more than eight per cent this week, with Brent trading near $86 a barrel and West Texas Intermediate near $80, as traders priced in the first real de-escalation signal. Iran's deputy foreign minister Kazem Gharibabadi said no military vessels would be permitted through the strait under the framework, and Iran's Revolutionary Guards have agreed with Oman to share revenues from transit fees. Tehran has also warned the waterway will not reopen unless the US ends its war.

For Gulf states, a working corridor would do something no diplomatic statement has managed since the conflict began: restore the chokepoint's flow without resolving the fighting. That is why Washington's Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent is still threatening Iran with what he called economic asphyxiation, announcing fresh sanctions even as the corridor talks advance. The two tracks move in opposite directions, and only one of them moves oil. The corridor is a signal, not a solution, so any bet on cheap freight still rests on a war that has not ended.

02Swvl's PIPE
Swvl raises $13m from Sawiris-backed Coefficient
Swvl secured a $13 million private investment, led by Coefficient LP, which is backed by the Sawiris family, with Coefficient putting in $10 million and an existing shareholder adding $3 million. The company will issue 8.99 million Class A shares at $1.446 each, and the deal is expected to close on August 27. Coefficient's founder and managing partner Abdalla Ali will join Swvl's board.

Q1 2026 revenue grew 68 per cent year-over-year to $8.2 million, with GCC revenue up 111 per cent and recurring revenue at 88 per cent of the total. Net dollar retention came in at 114 per cent, while operating expenses fell to 23 per cent of revenue, putting the company near operating breakeven. Dollar-pegged revenue is now 44 per cent of the total, a hedge against currency swings.

The proceeds, according to the company, will go toward US expansion and a lending offering for transport operators. Swvl runs in Egypt, Saudi Arabia, the UAE, Kuwait, Qatar, the UK and the US, and the GCC is the fastest-growing part of that map. A PIPE at $1.446 per share with 111 per cent GCC growth is a vote of confidence in regional mobility spending, not just in Swvl. GCC mobility demand is the engine of this deal, so it is a signal that transport tech in the Gulf is still attracting global capital.

03Bitcoin Custody
SEC revives crypto custody rule
The US Securities and Exchange Commission is resurrecting a crypto custody rule that the previous administration failed to land in 2023. The earlier attempt would have narrowly restricted where investment advisers could park clients' crypto assets, forcing qualified custodians to hold them instead of the advisers themselves. The new approach is still largely under wraps, but the regulator is signalling it will try again.

For Gulf crypto platforms and family offices, the rule matters because US investment advisers have become a meaningful source of demand for custody and prime brokerage services. If the SEC tightens custody standards, every offshore platform that wants US advisers as clients will face new compliance costs or lose access.

The region has been racing to become a crypto hub precisely by offering regulatory clarity. A stricter US regime pushes more of that custody business offshore, and Dubai and Abu Dhabi are among the few places with the licences and the capital to absorb it. US advisers need custodians that meet the new standard, and Gulf platforms with strong licences are positioned to win that flow.

The AI Race
​
Nvidia's quarter is the Gulf's demand forecast
Nvidia reported $96.2 billion in Q2 revenue, up 106% from a year earlier. Net income jumped to $59.7 billion from $26.4 billion. The company forecast $108 billion for the current quarter, above the $104.2 billion analysts expected. Data centre revenue hit $75.2 billion, up 92%, but three customers made up 21%, 17% and 16% of revenue.

Those same three customers account for 64% of accounts receivable, so Nvidia's balance sheet leans on three names. The company also joined with investment firms to build financing platforms meant to mobilise more than $500 billion for AI infrastructure. It carries a $5.1 trillion market capitalisation and trades at 16 times forward earnings. The market is pricing perfection, and any supply shock becomes everyone's problem. Gulf AI investors have tied their data centre plans to Nvidia's supply chain and pricing power, so this quarter's numbers are the region's own demand forecast.

Off Paper
​
Saudi adults hit their fitness target early
Saudi Arabia reported that 59.1% of adults met the recommended 150 minutes of weekly physical activity in 2025, above the 55% target. Physical activity among children aged 5-17 hit 19%, a goal originally set for 2030. Vision 2030's quality-of-life agenda is turning exercise into a state-backed habit.
Keyper turned rent into a monthly salary decision
Keyper, which raised $11 million in June, caps rent at around 45% of monthly salary for underwriting. It has financed more than $44 million in rent value since launch. Household cash flow is now the product, and Dubai's flood of new supply is the sales pitch.
​
A jiu-jitsu chain raises $2m for Riyadh and Monaco
GMNSM, a Brazilian jiu-jitsu academy network, raised $2 million from VEYRA Capital and a private investor. The money opens its first academies in Riyadh and Monaco. It already runs 11 academies across the UAE, Qatar and Cyprus, serving more than 700 children and adults. Saudi fitness targets are pulling foreign sports academies into Riyadh before the first wave gets crowded.
