The Lead
​
Dubai International Airport lost a third of its passengers in six months, and the war did it.
Aircraft movements fell 32.1 percent to 150,600, and air freight volumes dropped 28.7 percent to around 751,000 tons. The numbers come from Dubai Airports and cover the six months since the Iran war began in February.

The airport still connects to 217 destinations in 99 countries through nearly 50 international airlines. But the recovery is uneven: guest volumes climbed from 3.5 million in April to 4.5 million in May and 5 million in June. Emirates is flying at 90 percent capacity in July, and it ranked first globally for international capacity in August despite a 5.4 percent year-on-year capacity drop.

The wider tourism sector took the same hit. Hotels run by Marriott, Hilton and Hyatt reported double-digit declines in revenue per available room in the Middle East during the second quarter. Accor's chief financial officer said UAE activity was down about 80 percent year on year in April, recovering to a 40 to 45 percent decline by June. The World Travel and Tourism Council forecasts the UAE will lose 46,000 tourism roles this year, with Gulf-wide losses estimated at 137,000.

The second half looks stronger. Dubai Airports expects momentum to build as airlines restore route networks and transit traffic recovers. UAE entry permits issued fell from 1.94 million in February to 647,944 in April, then climbed back to 1 million by July. The airport's recovery path is set, but it depends on the war not escalating again. The Gulf's biggest travel hub is now a barometer for the region's war risk, and the first half reading was brutal. 02Adnoc blacklisted
Iran blacklists Adnoc-linked tankers
Iran named Adnoc-linked vessels among 45 tankers blacklisted for breaking rules on crossing the Strait of Hormuz. The list includes vessels owned by Adnoc L&S and Navig8 Tankers, in which Adnoc L&S took an 80 percent stake for $1.04 billion last year.

The blacklisted vessels risk fines, detention and cargo confiscation. Adnoc has sold more than 100 million barrels via tenders using a shuttle model workaround. This week Adnoc and Kuwait's KPC chartered four VLCCs on the spot market to load crude at Fujairah for Asia, each carrying about 2 million barrels.

The blacklist is, according to Tehran, a pressure tactic aimed at Gulf exporters who are rerouting around the chokepoint. For Gulf shippers, the risk calculation is now explicit: every voyage near Hormuz carries a political as well as a physical hazard. The response is already visible in the chartering decisions being made at Fujairah. The response is already visible in the chartering decisions being made at Fujairah.

03Oro raises
Oro raises $3 million for AI finance platform
UAE-based financial AI platform Oro raised $3 million in a strategic round co-led by MH Ventures and Mapleblock Capital. Participants included M2M Capital, Archer Capital and X21 Digital, with follow-on backing from Disrupt.com and ZIGLabs. The round brings Oro's total funding to $4 million.

Founded in 2024 by Varun Choudhary, Oro has onboarded more than 350,000 active users and supports over 80 languages. An educational campaign backed by Amazon Web Services saw over 250,000 verified user completions. Live integrations include Morpho, Kamino, Lido, Aave, Uniswap and Raydium. The company aims for 10 million active users over the next 6 to 12 months.

The funding is a bet that AI-powered financial execution can scale across emerging markets, including the Gulf. Native iOS and Android apps are in the works, and new advisory board members include AI and DeFi veterans. For Gulf fintech watchers, Oro's growth is a signal that AI-native finance is the next battleground for user acquisition in the region. 04Aramco French deals
Aramco signs $3.7 billion French agreements
Aramco signed agreements worth $3.7 billion with French companies during the French-Saudi Investment roundtable in Paris. The agreements were signed by Aramco and Aramco Digital, and the event was attended by Amin Nasser, president and CEO of Aramco.

The deals span multiple sectors, reinforcing the Saudi-French commercial corridor that has deepened since the kingdom's Vision 2030 push. Aramco has used French partnerships to build out its downstream and technology capabilities, and Aramco Digital is the vehicle for its AI and digital infrastructure ambitions.

For Gulf operators, the deal flow is a reminder that Saudi Arabia's industrial policy is still running at full speed, even as war dominates headlines. The kingdom is using its oil giant to build a technology and industrial base that outlasts any single conflict. That strategy is now a decade old, and it is still the most reliable way to read where Saudi capital will flow next. Swvl investment
Swvl secures $13 million from Sawiris-backed fund
Swvl secured a $13 million PIPE investment led by Coefficient LP, backed by the Sawiris family. Coefficient is investing $10 million and an existing shareholder $3 million. Coefficient founder Abdalla Ali will join Swvl's board, and the fund becomes the largest institutional shareholder upon closing.

Swvl will issue 8.99 million Class A shares at $1.446 per share, with the transaction expected to close August 27, 2026. The company's first-quarter revenue grew 68 percent year-on-year to $8.2 million, with GCC revenue up 111 percent. Recurring revenue hit 88 percent, and net dollar retention stood at 114 percent.

Proceeds will fund US expansion, a lending offering for transport operators, and balance sheet strengthening. Swvl operates in Egypt, Saudi Arabia, the UAE, Kuwait, Qatar, the UK and the US. The deal is a rare bright spot in a tough MENA funding market, and it shows that mobility platforms with real revenue can still raise capital. The AI Race
​
Nvidia's quarter resets the AI buildout clock
Nvidia reported 96.2 billion in revenue for the second quarter. That is a 106 percent jump from a year earlier. Data center revenue hit 75.2 billion. Three customers alone made up 21 percent, 17 percent and 16 percent of revenue, and 64 percent of accounts receivable. The company expects 108 billion next quarter. It also announced financing platforms with investment firms to mobilize more than 500 billion for AI infrastructure.

The numbers matter less than the forecast. Nvidia is not selling chips. It is financing the buyers of its chips. If the next quarter lands, the Gulf's data center and power bets look early. If it misses, the whole AI capex story pauses for a quarter. The year-old AI startup Instinct raised 350 million at a 2.5 billion valuation, showing venture money still chases consumer AI. Nvidia is the bellwether for every one of these bets. Gulf funds exposed to AI infrastructure now have a cleaner signal: Nvidia's forecast, not its history, is the number that moves them.

The rent burden is starting to ease
Keyper has financed more than 44 million in rent and caps rent at 45 percent of a tenant's salary. The old annual cheque model is loosening. That means a working family can keep more of its monthly cash flow. For Gulf renters, the shift to monthly payments is a real, if quiet, pay rise.

Dubai's gem maxxing moment
A trend called gem maxxing has taken over Dubai, possibly from Kylie Jenner's birthday. It means decking out in crystals and gems. Six Dubai venues now court the look. The trend will fade like all the others, but for now the city is sparkling harder than usual. Dubai's trend cycle turns on a dime, and the venues that ride it best are the ones that never buy permanent decor.
