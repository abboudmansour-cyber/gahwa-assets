The Lead
​
A two-way highway 11 kilometers wide could reopen the world's most dangerous chokepoint.
Dubai International Airport handled 31.5 million passengers in the first half of 2026, down 31.3 percent from a year earlier. Aircraft movements fell 32.1 percent to just over 150,000. Air freight dropped 28.7 percent to roughly 751,000 tons.

The recovery is real but uneven. Passenger numbers climbed from 3.5 million in April to 5 million in June as airlines restored routes. By the end of June, nearly 50 international airlines served DXB, connecting to 217 destinations in 99 countries. Emirates flew at 90 percent capacity in July with premium cabins full, and ranked first globally for international capacity in August despite a 5.4 percent year-on-year capacity drop. UAE entry permits fell from 1.94 million in February to 647,944 in April, then recovered to 1 million by July.

The World Travel and Tourism Council expects the UAE to lose 46,000 tourism roles this year, part of 137,000 across the Gulf. Middle East travel and tourism activity is forecast to contract 14.5 percent in 2026, the only region expected to shrink. For Gulf operators, the signal is that demand is coming back in waves, not in a straight line. Hotels, airlines, and retailers should staff for the June level, not the February peak. The Gulf's travel economy is climbing back, but it will be a staircase, not an elevator.

02Gem maxxing trend
Dubai spots take gem maxxing to next level
Six Dubai venues are now selling themselves as gem maxxing destinations, the latest aesthetic trend to wash over the city. The term, which likely took off after Kylie Jenner's birthday bash, means dressing up, going out, and being photographed like a polished surface.

It is a natural fit for a city where the dinner reservation and the photo opportunity have merged into one event. The list includes venues betting that sparkle is a service, not a side effect. For Gulf operators, the trend is a reminder that Gen Z will pay for a backdrop as much as a meal. The camera eats first, and now it pays too. Restaurants that ignore the photo factor are leaving revenue on the table.

The AI Race
​
Nvidia's chips are selling faster than power can be built
Nvidia reported 96.2 billion dollars in quarterly revenue, up 106 percent from a year earlier and above the 92.2 billion dollars analysts expected. Data center revenue hit 75.2 billion dollars, up 92 percent. The company now expects 108 billion dollars for the current quarter, above the 104.2 billion dollars the market had forecast, and says AI chip sales will jump 70 percent next year.

The three biggest customers account for more than half of revenue and 64 percent of accounts receivable. That concentration is the new chokepoint in the AI race: if those customers pause, the queue behind them is long but the cash flow is thin. Nvidia has moved to build financing platforms with investment firms that could mobilize more than 500 billion dollars for AI infrastructure, which is a way of saying it will lend money to its own customers to keep buying its own chips. Every Gulf sovereign fund with an AI strategy is watching whether Nvidia's customers keep buying at this rate, because the data center buildout from Riyadh to Abu Dhabi runs on those chips.
