The Lead
​
Saudi Arabia's main oil artery is shut after projectiles hit its pumping stations, officials say, and the Houthis just seized the island that guards the Red Sea's southern gate.
A US assessment found the pump stations were hit by drones that may have originated from Iraq, according to officials, though Saudi authorities have not confirmed the pipeline itself sustained physical damage, and its closure removes a major artery from the global market. Satellite imagery showed extensive fire damage at one station. The attack, which Gulf News reported, reportedly marks the first time the Kingdom's oil transport infrastructure has been directly hit in the current conflict. That gives the group control of both sides of the narrow waterway through which much of the Gulf's oil exports pass. Ship traffic through the strait plunged after the Houthis earlier seized the port city of Mokha, about 150km south of Hodeidah, and now the encirclement of the route is reportedly complete.

Brent crude jumped more than 6 percent to over $107 a barrel, and WTI gained 1.48 percent to $104. The IEA cut its 2026 global oil demand forecast to a 2.5 million barrel per day decline, from 1.6 million before, yet prices still rose because the market is pricing the loss of physical supply, not the demand side. S&P Global now says Middle East crude flows will stay below pre-war levels through 2027. For Gulf operators, this is not a short-term shock but a standing condition: supply disruption has become the baseline. Plan around it.

02Hormuz escalation
Four projectiles hit vessels near Khasab
Four projectiles struck two commercial vessels about 4 nautical miles west of Khasab, Oman, and one of the ships caught fire, according to maritime reports. Iran said its talks with Oman on the Hormuz Strait have reached a final stage, and the IRGC Navy issued a warning to tanker crews near Kuwait and Bahrain, according to officials. Tehran also plans to declare a prohibited zone near the strait, a move that would formalise the current danger. US forces have already redirected 99 commercial vessels, per Centcom, and the naval blockade of Iran has pushed Tehran toward more forceful responses.

The strikes on the vessels near Khasab, which officials say sit just outside the strait, occurred where the shipping lane squeezes between Oman's Musandam peninsula and the Iranian coast. Vessels transiting the area now face a threat that is no longer theoretical. Brent crude topped $107.6 per barrel, and the Nikkei fell 3 percent as Asian markets priced in the risk to energy supply. For Gulf operators, the operational question is no longer whether to reroute but how long the rerouting will last.

03Mecca metro deadline
Mecca metro design deadline set for October
Saudi Arabia has set an October deadline for the design of a new Mecca metro, a four-line network with 89 stations and three depots. The project will be built in three phases between 2032 and 2045.

The metro is part of the Kingdom's broader effort to upgrade transport in the holy city, which faces intense pressure during the Hajj and Umrah seasons. The network will connect the main pilgrimage sites and residential districts, easing the reliance on buses and private cars.

The design contract is the first step in what will be a multibillion-dollar construction programme. International and local engineering firms are expected to compete for the work. For Gulf operators, the deadline is a signal that the Kingdom is pushing ahead with its infrastructure plans even as it absorbs the cost of the current conflict.

04VASP rules
UAE issues long-awaited VASP rules
The UAE's Capital Market Authority issued Decision No. 04/RM/2026, setting out rules for virtual asset service providers and alternative trading system operators. The decision covers licensing, operational, risk management, corporate governance and transparency requirements.

The rules bring long-expected clarity to the UAE's crypto sector, which has been waiting for a comprehensive regulatory framework. The country has positioned itself as a hub for digital assets, but the absence of a unified rulebook has been a barrier for larger institutional players.

For Gulf founders and investors in the crypto space, the regulations close the gap between the UAE's promotional ambition and legal reality. A clear rulebook means the next wave of crypto businesses can build with confidence, and the wave after that will arrive because of them.

Robot data rush
Mecka AI nears $500m in Sequoia round
Mecka AI is nearing a $500 million valuation in a Sequoia-led round, just months after the two-year-old company announced its Series A. The startup focuses on robot training data, the fuel that lets machines learn physical tasks from real examples.

The deal is the clearest sign yet that investors see the bottleneck in robotics shifting from hardware to data. A robot that can fold a shirt or stack a shelf needs thousands of hours of examples, and Mecka AI builds the pipelines that deliver them. Sequoia's move follows a wave of investment in the physical layer of AI, from data centers to the sensors and cameras that generate the training material.

For Gulf operators, the robotics story is no longer a Silicon Valley sideshow. The region's logistics, construction and healthcare sectors are among the most likely to adopt physical AI early, and the cost of that adoption depends on the data pipeline behind it. The AI Race
​
DeepSeek makes frontier AI cheap
DeepSeek released V4.1-Flash, a 552-billion-parameter open-weight model that beats its own V4-Pro on speed, cost and performance. The company then retired V4-Pro entirely. The model activates only 8 billion parameters when reading input and 16 billion when writing output. That efficiency cuts memory and storage needs dramatically. The result is API pricing of 30 cents per million input tokens. Off-peak pricing is half that.

Artificial Analysis found the model beats DeepSeek's full-size Pro model on the intelligence index at a quarter of the cost. It scores within a few points of GPT-6 Astra at 1.4 percent of the cost. That collapses the price of frontier-level capability. A startup can now rent a top model for less than a coffee per million tokens. The pressure falls on Western labs charging far more for marginal gains. It also pushes other labs to retire their own older models rather than defend them. For Gulf AI operators, the question is no longer whether they can afford a top model, but what they will build on infrastructure that is now almost free.

The Last Sip
​
Which Gulf waterway carries roughly 5 million barrels per day and just shut down?
The answer
The East-West Pipeline, also known as Petrolina. It moves crude from the eastern oil fields to the Red Sea. The pipeline is a major artery because it lets Saudi exports bypass Hormuz entirely. When projectiles hit its pumping stations, the Kingdom closed it as a precaution. The closure cut a route the global market had long treated as a constant. The market's only response was to add six percent to the price of a barrel, which is its way of clapping.
What did the Houthis just seize that guards the Red Sea's southern gate?
The answer
Perim Island. It sits in the Bab al-Mandab strait, the narrow waterway linking the Red Sea to the Indian Ocean. The island's capture means the group now holds both sides of the strait. That gives the Houthis a chokehold on one of the world's busiest shipping lanes. A sliver of volcanic rock just became the most expensive island in the Red Sea.
