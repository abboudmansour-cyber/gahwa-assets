The Lead
​
​
MBS asked Trump to stand down, and the oil market cratered anyway.
The Crown Prince's intervention came during a phone call on Saturday, as the White House was actively planning what Trump later reportedly described as 'the biggest attack since World War II'. Trump told reporters he called off the strikes, stating that an agreement to fully reopen the Strait of Hormuz and dismantle Iran's nuclear program was within reach, and that negotiations were happening 'right now,' according to his account. Iran's foreign ministry immediately denied any direct talks, though it confirmed negotiations over the Strait continue through Oman as an intermediary.

The diplomatic pivot landed in a market already under severe strain. BMI revised its Saudi economic forecast to a 1.3% contraction, a sharp reversal from an earlier 1.1% growth call, as months of Hormuz disruption have throttled crude exports. Saudi Arabia's real GDP contracted 4.8% year-on-year in the second quarter, the steepest decline since the pandemic. The TASI closed July down 1.95% with trading volumes nearly halved, and foreign outflows from UAE markets hit $828.8 million in the second quarter alone.

While a deal would relieve the single biggest pressure point on Gulf economies, the data already show a region absorbing a body blow. Even if Hormuz reopens fully, the second-half recovery will have to claw back ground from a deep first-half hole.
Operators should stress-test Q3 revenue models for a partial Hormuz reopening scenario rather than a full normalization; the economic damage is now locked into Q2 GDP figures, and even a swift deal leaves a 1, 2 quarter recovery lag.

FINTECH & CRYPTO
Emirates NBD buys HSBC's Egypt retail book
HSBC signed a definitive agreement to sell its entire Egyptian retail business to Emirates NBD Egypt, ending a 44-year consumer banking presence. The deal will generate a pre-tax gain of roughly $300 million for HSBC Group, and ENBD expects it will become the sixth or seventh-largest retail franchise in Egypt once the transaction closes, which is not expected before the second half of 2027 pending central bank approval.

The numbers reveal why HSBC is walking away. The Egyptian retail book held EGP 142.8 billion in deposits against just EGP 18.9 billion in loans in the first quarter, a 13% loan-to-deposit ratio that makes profitability an exercise in cost control. Retail contributed under 16% of HSBC Egypt's EGP 6.0 billion net income, while corporate banking carried nearly 70% of the bottom line. The customer base is roughly 330,000 active clients.

The deal extends ENBD's bold summer: the Dubai-controlled lender closed a $2.8 billion acquisition of 60% of India's RBL Bank in June. HSBC, meanwhile, is completing a global retreat from retail banking that has already seen it exit Canada, France, South Africa, Bahrain, and Sri Lanka, with Indonesia and Australia in mid-transition via a $25.3 billion mortgage and loan book sale to Blackstone. The pivot is pure arithmetic: 87.2% of HSBC's net new money came from Asia in the. Egyptian retail banking is consolidating toward scale players; any lender with a sub-20% loan-to-deposit ratio and a sub-500,000 customer base should evaluate its strategic options before the window narrows.

AI & TECH
Saudi startups retake MENA funding lead
Saudi Arabia reclaimed the top spot in MENA startup funding in July, with startups raising $106.6 million across 16 transactions, nearly 62% of the month's regional total of $172.6 million. That figure is up 16% from June but still 78% below the bonanza recorded in July 2025. The UAE pulled in $46.6 million across 16 deals, while Syria emerged as a surprise third-place market with three startups raising $10.16 million.

The structural shift is in the capital stack. Debt accounted for 56% of July's funding, compared to just 11.5% in June and 2% a year ago. E-commerce attracted 55% of all investment, with B2B startups capturing nearly 79% of deployed capital. Fintech recorded nine deals worth a combined $10.9 million, and proptech logged eight deals totalling $11.9 million.

Only 1% of July's capital went to startups founded solely by women, $1.7 million across four transactions. Male-founded teams collected 97% of the funding, a ratio that places the MENA ecosystem among the least gender-balanced in global venture. Founders should target B2B and e-commerce verticals with debt-ready models; venture debt providers are now the marginal price-setter for growth capital in the region, and the terms are better than equity dilution.

MACRO & GEO
Borouge reportedly restores Ruwais after Iranian attacks
Borouge has restored operations at its Ruwais complex after Iranian attacks reportedly damaged the facility, reporting a net income of $191 million in the second quarter backed by sales volumes of 900,000 tonnes. The company did not detail the full cost of the disruption, but the performance beat analyst expectations and sent the shares higher on the Abu Dhabi exchange.

According to analysts, Ruwais is the anchor of Abu Dhabi's downstream strategy, and its vulnerability to military strikes has been one of the unspoken risks since the Iran conflict escalated in February. The rapid restoration signals that redundancy and hardening investments made over the past decade are paying operational dividends, even if insurance premiums are almost certainly climbing.

The earnings beat is a narrow piece of good news in a Gulf industrials sector that has been priced for worst-case scenarios. For operators managing petrochemical supply chains, the takeaway, according to officials, is that regional production can absorb a direct hit and resume output faster than contingency planners penciled in. Supply chain managers should use Borouge's recovery timeline as a benchmark for their own contingencies: according to Borouge, a facility-wide restoration from a direct strike appears feasible within a single quarter, shifting the resilience math.

GCC & SAUDI
Spinneys lifts Saudi stake to 70%
Spinneys bought a further 20% of its Saudi subsidiary from Al-Hokair, increasing its ownership to 70% and deepening its bet on the Kingdom's grocery market. The transaction price was not disclosed. Spinneys has been expanding its Saudi footprint as part of a broader regional push, and the increased stake signals that the early returns justify more capital rather than a pullback.

The move arrives as Saudi retail consolidation accelerates under Vision 2030's push for modern trade to displace traditional corner stores. For Al-Hokair, the partial exit frees capital; for Spinneys, the controlling stake gives it full operational authority over a market where grocery spending is growing faster than in its mature UAE home base.

In a quarter where most regional business headlines involve falling volumes and contracting GDP, a grocery chain quietly buying control of its Saudi unit is a reminder that consumer staples do not track the Hormuz news cycle. Regional retailers should accelerate Saudi market entry plans now; the macroeconomic headwinds are suppressing asset valuations, creating a rare window to acquire controlling stakes at prices that may not be available once growth resumes.

GCC & SAUDI
UAE August fuel prices jump 6%
The UAE Fuel Price Committee set August rates with increases across the board: Super 98 at AED 3.60, up 5.9%; Special 95 at AED 3.49, up 6.1%; E-Plus 91 at AED 3.41, up 6.2%; and diesel at AED 3.80, up 5.6%. The hikes follow July cuts of up to 16.9% and partly reverse the relief that followed April's wartime spike, when diesel surged 72.4%.

The increases reflect the underlying tension in global refined product markets even as Brent crude slid 4.5% on hopes of a diplomatic deal. Logistics and transport operators should lock in Q4 fuel contracts now; a ceasefire would likely compress diesel prices further, and the August hike provides a negotiating anchor for volume discounts.

A bakery worker, ten friends, and Dh20 million
Reynaldo Herboso, a 61-year-old Filipino baker who has lived in the UAE since 1988, won the Dh20 million Big Ticket grand prize in Series 289. He is not keeping it alone. A 10-strong syndicate of friends from the Philippines, India, and Bangladesh, chasing the prize for 15 years, will split the jackpot evenly, roughly Dh2 million each. The winning ticket was picked by the group's Bangladeshi member, making the return on a decade and a half of friendship contributions a figure a VC partner would frame.
The office lottery pool just minted ten millionaires in Jebel Ali, and the math works out to a 15-year holding period with an exit multiple no fund can match.
