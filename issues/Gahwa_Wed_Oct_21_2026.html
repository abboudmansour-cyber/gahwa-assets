The Lead
​
Reportedly, Riyadh airport strike kills 18
Reportedly, Houthi drones and missiles struck King Khalid International Airport in Riyadh this week, killing 18 people and damaging the capital's main air hub. Reportedly, the attack came five days after the Mecca Joint Defense Agreement between Saudi Arabia, Pakistan and Turkey was activated on October 5. Houthi spokesperson Yahya Saree said Saudi forces carried out 93 airstrikes in response across eight Yemeni governorates in a single 24-hour period.

The Saudi government postponed the Future Investment Initiative, its flagship investment conference, to 2027. The event had been scheduled for October 26 and brought $50 billion in deals last year. Oxford Economics lead economist Akanksha Samdani said the initial impact on investment will be delayed decisions rather than cancellations. Tourism, hospitality and aviation are the most exposed sectors.

Energy Minister Prince Abdulaziz bin Salman told the WPC Energy Congress in Qatar that investment in the Kingdom must continue. Saudi Arabia's net foreign direct investment inflow fell 17.3% quarter-on-quarter to SAR 19.1 billion in the second quarter. Travel and hospitality operators are the first to feel it. Private jet charter prices from Saudi Arabia are up more than 50% since the airport closure. The Kingdom is racing to pipe up to 10 million barrels a day outside the Gulf within three years. This would reduce the leverage Iran holds over the Strait of Hormuz. A capital that was spoken of as a war-free island is now being assessed through a security lens. The premium on that assessment is climbing with every stranded flight.

02Land bank pop
Arada plants $13bn Riyadh bet
A UAE developer has assembled a SAR 50 billion Saudi land bank and a SAR 14 billion first project in north-eastern Riyadh. Arada entered through a partnership with Xtopia, with Xtopia chairman Mohammed AlDhabaan chairing the new Arada KSA. The debut project in Al Rimal spans 2.5 million square metres and will deliver roughly 3,500 villas and townhouses with construction starting in 2027.

Arada is betting it can build 100,000 homes across Saudi Arabia within three to five years. The company is co-owned by Prince Khaled bin Alwaleed's KBW Investments and Sharjah's Basma Group. It already runs a AED 185 billion development pipeline across five markets. The first project will be financed mostly through a CMA-regulated fund managed by Al Rajhi Capital.

This is a Gulf developer crossing the border at scale, not just opening a sales office. Saudi homeownership has passed 66% on its way to a 70% target, and designated zones in Riyadh and Jeddah are now open to foreign buyers. Arada's move signals that UAE developers see Saudi demand as the next big housing wave, even as the capital absorbs security shocks. Arada has acquired a modest 10 million square metres and a small ambition of 100,000 homes.

03Nine-figure AI
MGX returns for $30bn OpenAI round
MGX is among existing backers in talks to join OpenAI's new funding round of roughly $30 billion at a valuation above $1.4 trillion. SoftBank is also in talks. OpenAI wants to close the round before Anthropic's expected mid-November listing, pitching $50 billion in annualized revenue.

The valuation marks a 64% jump from the $852 billion OpenAI was worth in March. MGX co-led OpenAI's $122 billion round that closed in March, and co-led Anthropic's $30 billion Series G and $65 billion Series H. Anthropic's quarterly revenue rose to $16.8 billion in the third quarter from $11.6 billion in the second.

Abu Dhabi is now the most visible Middle East capital in the global AI race. MGX has backed the two largest labs on both sides of the OpenAI-Anthropic competition. The question for Gulf investors is whether the premium valuation on both deals still leaves room for returns. Or whether the region is paying whatever it takes to stay in the room. When the same Abu Dhabi fund is on both sides of the biggest AI rivalry, the Gulf has stopped betting on the race. It has started insuring it.

04Pipe dreams
Hormuz could lose its choke point
HSBC Group CEO Georges Elhedery said the Strait of Hormuz could become redundant within five years as Gulf nations build alternative pipeline routes. The waterway previously carried a fifth of global oil and LNG trade.

Roughly 40% of Gulf crude already bypasses Hormuz, against 17% before the war, according to Kpler. Saudi Arabia wants to pipe up to 10 million barrels a day outside the Gulf within three years. That is above the East-West Pipeline's current 7 million barrel capacity.

The broader Middle East and Turkey hold $872 billion in individual project packages within a $1.4 trillion pipeline of planned infrastructure, according to HSBC. The region is not just planning around Hormuz, it is using the threat to justify a new energy map. The Gulf is not waiting for peace to build its way around the strait. Every pipeline now carries as much strategic weight as it does crude.

05Local glow-up
Blue Rhine lands Sphere Abu Dhabi LEDs
UAE-based Blue Rhine Industries won the contract to install and commission the external LED display for the $1.7 billion Sphere Abu Dhabi project on Yas Island. The appointment was made by Alec, the main contractor.

The Sphere is one of Abu Dhabi's highest-profile entertainment builds, and the LED skin is the project's defining feature. Blue Rhine's win keeps the technical work in local hands rather than importing a specialist team.

The contract is the kind of work that builds a regional capability in large-format LED engineering. Once one Gulf venue has the skill installed, the next venue is cheaper and faster to build. Blue Rhine won the skin, which means the next venue can borrow more than just the scaffolding.
Source: MEED UAE

The AI Race
​
Anthropic wants a kill switch with a judge's permission
Anthropic is pitching a US AI safety bill that would allow the federal government to block a model's release, but only with a judge's permission. The threshold is specific: a model could be blocked if 50 or more people could be reportedly killed. The bill is not a blanket moratorium. It is a targeted kill switch.

CEO Dario Amodei wrote that AI capabilities are advancing faster than the ability to understand, align and control them. Deliberately slowing improvement is now on the table. The proposal is the lab's answer to a year in which AI safety went from academic concern to regulatory deadline. It pressures every other frontier lab to match the offer. Gulf regulators hosting frontier models can import this template without starting from scratch.

Taqa raises $1.75bn in first bond since delisting
Taqa sold a $1.1 billion five-year green note at 5.875% and a $650 million ten-year bond at 6.125%. Final orders topped $5.7 billion, over three times the deal size. The proceeds help fund a AED 75 billion capital plan through 2030.
Shell exits Aphrodite to MOL for up to $720m
Shell agreed to sell its 35% stake in the Aphrodite gas field offshore Cyprus to Hungary's MOL. The deal values the project at more than $2 billion. First gas is now targeted for 2031.
Diriyah awards $728m in luxury hotel contracts
Diriyah Company signed construction contracts worth SAR 2.73 billion for five luxury hotels and three residential assets. The contracts cover 413 hotel rooms and 34 branded residences in Diriyah Square. The masterplan targets nearly 34 luxury hotel brands.
Abu Dhabi launches TAMM Global to export its government app
TAMM Global is a private company that will license Abu Dhabi's AI-powered government services app to other countries. The platform carries more than 1,200 services. The move turns a public service into a commercial export.
MNT-Halan shares jump 6.9% on EGX debut
Egyptian fintech MNT-Halan closed at EGP 26.20 on its first day of trading on the EGX. The listing entity is the Egyptian subsidiary, with the parent holding company retaining Turkish, Emirati and Pakistani units. The retail tranche was 43.6 times oversubscribed.
