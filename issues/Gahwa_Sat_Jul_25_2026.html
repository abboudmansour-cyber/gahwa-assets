UAE's $1.4tn US Pledge Speeds Ahead
The UAE's $1.4 trillion US investment framework is ahead of schedule and could be completed sooner than originally planned, Foreign Trade Minister Thani Al Zeyoudi said, as the Gulf state prepares to start importing advanced US chips 'very soon' for a massive US-UAE data center complex.

The upgraded US export status, granted earlier this year, clears the way for purchasing American satellites, defense equipment, and semiconductors that will power a 5 GW data center in Abu Dhabi. Al Zeyoudi also said the UAE expects to sign a trade agreement with Canada this week, building on a $50 billion investment framework.

The progress on the investment front signals deepening economic integration even as the Gulf teeters on the edge of a wider military conflict. For US allies in the region, the dual track, security cooperation and commercial interdependence, is becoming the only workable strategy. FINTECH & CRYPTO
SILQ Lands $20M for Saudi SME Finance
Saudi fintech SILQ secured a $20 million Shariah-compliant financing facility from Gemcorp Capital to boom embedded lending for the kingdom's small businesses, the first direct lending and first Shariah-compliant structure the London firm has ever done in Saudi Arabia.

The facility will flow through SILQ's Fina platform, expanding invoice financing, trade finance, and other working-capital products for the more than 50,000 businesses the startup already serves. SILQ says it enabled over SAR 10 billion in B2B commerce in 2026, a number that makes clear how starved SMEs have been for working capital.

Gemcorp's move into Saudi mezzanine lending is the real signal: a London asset manager that cut its teeth on emerging-market sovereign debt now wants a piece of the kingdom's SME receivables, wagering that the yield on unpaid invoices in Riyadh beats anything it can find in Johannesburg or Cairo. If the pilot works, expect a queue of foreign credit funds at the Saudi border, each with a marginally different Shariah structure in their back pocket. Gemcorp Capital closed its first direct lending and first Shariah-compliant facility in Saudi Arabia via SILQ's embedded finance platform.

AI & TECH
e& Hits 4.58 Gbps on Live 5.5G
UAE telecom giant e& activated the region's first 1024QAM modulation on a live 5.5G network, hitting multi-gigabit speeds of 4.58 Gbps in a demo with Huawei's newly launched Pura 90s smartphone.

The modulation, a leap over the 256QAM used in most 5G rollouts, allows operators to pack more data into the same spectrum without adding towers, a handy trick when the Gulf's appetite for streaming and AI training is devouring bandwidth faster than an Abu Dhabi summer.

What makes the milestone stick is that it was on a commercial, not lab, network, meaning the gear is already in the field. For enterprise customers waiting on the edge computing and private 5G promised by 5.5G, it is a sign that the plumbing is real, not a slide deck. e& showed the region's first commercial 5.5G with 1024QAM modulation, hitting 4.58 Gbps on a live network.

GCC & SAUDI
Dubai Inflation May Have Peaked at 5.7%
Dubai's headline inflation hit 5.7% in June, up from 5.5% in May, but the monthly pace slowed to 0.4%, the weakest since February, leading Emirates NBD to call the peak and forecast a drop to 2.9% by year-end.

Transport costs were the biggest villain, up 18.1% year-on-year, with fuel and lubricant prices surging 48.3% and airfares climbing 16.5%. But tourism businesses cut selling prices for a second straight month, accommodation prices fell 8.5% from May, and packaged holiday prices dropped 4.3%, suggesting the worst of the post-war price squeeze is easing.

The risk is Hormuz. If shipping disruption keeps fuel costs elevated, the 2.9% year-end forecast starts to look like a brochure number from a quieter era. For now, the narrative in Dubai is that the inflation dragon is wounded, not slain. Dubai's CPI reached 5.7% in June but monthly momentum slowed sharply, and Emirates NBD predicts inflation will fall to 2.9% by December.
