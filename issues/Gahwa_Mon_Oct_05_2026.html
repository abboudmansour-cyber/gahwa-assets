The Lead
​
Saudi-backed Yemen offensive launches
Yemen's government launched military operations against the Houthis with Saudi support, President Rashad Al Alimi announced Sunday. Al Alimi said the campaign continues until the government retakes Houthi-held territory, according to Al Alimi. Riyadh is also making a new budget support disbursement of more than SAR 224 million to the Yemeni government via the Saudi Development and Reconstruction Program for Yemen.

A committee under the Makkah Agreement for Joint Defence between Saudi Arabia, Turkey, and Pakistan convenes in Riyadh this week. The committee will address regional developments and Alliance institutionalization. The offensive escalates a conflict that has drawn in regional powers and repeatedly threatened shipping lanes.

Opec+ held November output targets steady, pausing monthly increases, while seven countries produced 25 million barrels per day in August, roughly 5 million barrels per day below February levels. Brent is holding above $100 per barrel. The next Opec+ meeting is set for 1 November.

The fighting adds fresh uncertainty to Red Sea shipping and energy infrastructure at a moment when Gulf governments are pressing ahead with tourism and investment plans. Saudi Arabia still has 14 World Cup stadiums under construction or renovation ahead of 2034. A widening war in Yemen could force a reallocation of resources and security priorities. The Saudi treasury and military logistics are now committed to a campaign with no stated end date.

02The quiet judgment
GCC remittances hit record $161 billion
GCC outward workers' remittances reached a record $161 billion in 2025, up 13.6% year-on-year. The rise of about $19 billion from the previous year marks a second consecutive annual increase after a decline in 2023. The UAE was the largest source at $62.1 billion, followed by Saudi Arabia at $58 billion.

Saudi Arabia accounted for about $12.3 billion of the $19 billion rise, posting the fastest annual growth at 26.9%. The two countries together made up roughly 74.8% of the bloc's total. Egypt received $41.5 billion in transfers from Egyptians working abroad in 2025, the largest recipient in the region.

Combined GCC outflows exceeded the US at about $107 billion, Switzerland at $43 billion, Germany at $27 billion, and France at $21 billion. The money flowing out of the Gulf equals about 6.6% of the bloc's combined GDP, up from 6% in 2024.

Each remittance transfer is a quiet judgment on the Gulf labour model: the region keeps importing workers and the money they earn pours outward, creating whole economies dependent on Gulf salaries rather than their own wages. A fast growing remittance bill is the price Gulf states pay for a labour market built on millions of foreign workers sending wages home.

03One Sector at a Time
Saudi floats foreign ownership increase
Saudi Arabia is opening its market sector by sector, says CMA Chairman Mazen Al Sudairi, as foreign ownership limits on Saudi listed companies are being set sector by sector, negotiated with each industry's regulators. Morgan Stanley projected the foreign ownership cap could rise to 75%, drawing roughly $4.3 billion in additional inflows, or $7.4 billion if restrictions are lifted entirely.

The CMA has also drawn up a 90-day action plan covering IPO quality, declining stocks, and retail participation. It wants retail allocation in IPOs raised to 30% and is working on new short-selling rules. Listed companies will be required to hold two earnings discussion meetings a year.

Raising the ownership ceiling would let foreign money move into the Saudi market at a scale that has not been possible before. The sector-by-sector approach means some industries open first and others wait. Retail investors are being given a bigger slice of IPOs at the same time the door opens wider for international institutions. The IPO market is about to get more crowded, with retail allocations rising and foreign caps loosening at the same time.

04Five years, no complaints
UAE retail sukuk orders hit 5.7x
The UAE's second retail T-sukuk drew AED 285 million in orders, 5.7 times the AED 50 million target, even as the tenor stretched from two to five years. Retail investors seeking AED 10,000 or less accounted for 75% of subscriptions. UAE nationals generated 69% of demand and investors from 110 nationalities participated.

The sukuk trades on Nasdaq Dubai at a 5.06% annual coupon. The first tranche drew AED 445 million in orders, nearly nine times its original target, before the government doubled issuance to AED 100 million.

The order book shows retail investors in the Gulf are hungry for government paper that pays a clear yield, and they will line up for it even when the terms get longer. Small savers used to have few options beyond bank deposits. A sovereign sukuk at five years with a coupon above 5% becomes the benchmark every other savings product gets measured against. Small savers now have a sovereign alternative to bank deposits, and they are taking it.

Oman Economy
Oman GDP grows 5.1% on oil
Oman's GDP at constant prices reached approximately $25.8 billion in the second quarter, up from $24.5 billion year-on-year, a 5.1% growth rate. Petroleum activities grew 14.7% year-on-year to around $8.9 billion. Non-petroleum activities grew just 0.7% to approximately $18.2 billion.

Agriculture and fishing grew 2.3% to $846.6 million, while services expanded 1.5% to approximately $11.9 billion. Industrial activities contracted 1.5% year-on-year to around $4.9 billion. S&P Global Ratings affirmed Oman's long-term sovereign credit rating at BBB- with a stable outlook.

S&P raised Oman's real GDP growth forecast for 2026 to 3.5% from 1.6% previously and expects a fiscal surplus of about 4.8% of GDP this year. Cargo volumes at the ports of Salalah and Sohar rose 15% and 52% respectively in the first half.

Oman's oil windfall is doing the heavy lifting while non-oil growth barely moves. That makes the headline number look healthier than the underlying economy. The question for Oman is whether oil keeps covering for a non-oil sector stuck at 0.7% growth. Oman is running a fiscal surplus but is still hostage to a non-oil economy that is barely growing.

The AI Race
​
UAE puts Nvidia chips in orbit
The UAE launched Altair-1, the first satellite in a planned 10-satellite commercial Earth-observation constellation, aboard SpaceX's Transporter-18 mission from California. Altair-1 carries integrated sensors and onboard Nvidia GPUs for in-space data processing within minutes. The system will provide four-hour revisit capabilities once the full constellation is operational in 2027.

Separately, the Technology Innovation Institute unveiled the UAE's first fully homebuilt deep-space probe, designed and built entirely in Abu Dhabi. The probe will fly on a 5 billion kilometre journey past six asteroids, making contact with asteroid 269 Justitia in early 2036. In-space data processing means the UAE is building a commercial space economy, not just launching satellites.
Business
​
Aramco cuts Arab Light price by $3
Aramco cut the Arab Light crude price for Asian buyers for November by $3 from October. The cut drops the grade $5 below the regional benchmark, the lowest since June 2020. It also lowered Northwest Europe prices roughly $0.80 per barrel and North America by $0.10 per barrel.
Marriott sees regional RevPAR jump
Marriott's regional RevPAR is expected to climb roughly 70% from second-quarter levels, with Hilton's up about 65%. Dubai first-half occupancy fell 30.3% year-on-year to 56.4%. STR data puts RevPAR across Dubai, Abu Dhabi, Jeddah, and Riyadh about 30% below fourth-quarter 2025 levels.
China locks in battery dominance
China and six other government bodies issued a 2026-2030 plan making all-solid-state batteries a national industrial priority. China produced more than 80% of the world's batteries in 2025. BYD aims to begin mass manufacturing solid-state batteries for EVs by 2030.
Neom stadium on hold indefinitely
Saudi Arabia indefinitely paused the 2034 World Cup stadium planned inside Neom's The Line. The 46,000-seat, $2.5 billion venue is now listed as on hold per construction tracker Meed. No restart date has been given. Saudi Arabia is still building or renovating 14 other World Cup venues for 2034.
Raya Holding taps Zakaria Nabil as Raya Trade CEO
Egypt's Raya Holding appointed Zakaria Nabil as CEO of Raya Trade. Nabil takes over the trade division of the diversified Egyptian group.

Off Paper
​
UAE space team built a probe in 18 months
The UAE's first fully homebuilt deep-space probe was designed and built entirely in Abu Dhabi. A team that grew from 40 to 55 people pulled it together in about 18 months, with roughly 60% of the workforce Emirati. They drew on more than 25 UAE-based suppliers. A small team proving a country can build spacecraft, not just buy them.
Prepared Gulf firms kept hiring
Companies with contingency plans kept hiring through the shipping-cost shock, while unprepared firms froze headcount. Zeina Karrit, founder of Unicorn Talent GCC, says the gap will widen through the fourth quarter and into early 2027. Resilience is now a hiring advantage, not just a supply-chain one.
​
Saudi Arabia skips Joy Awards
Saudi Arabia will skip next year's Joy Awards. The Red Sea Film Festival has been moved to 2027 and the Soundstorm festival from MDLBEAST is off for December. The events calendar is being rebalanced just as the country competes for regional attention.
The Last Sip
​
Which Gulf country's economy grew faster than almost everywhere else in the region this quarter?
The answer
Oman's GDP grew 5.1% in the second quarter of 2026. The oil sector surged 14.7%, but non-oil growth slowed to just 0.7%. S&P expects a fiscal surplus of about 4.8% of GDP this year and cargo volumes at Sohar port jumped 52% in the first half. A healthy headline hiding an economy that still runs on one engine.
