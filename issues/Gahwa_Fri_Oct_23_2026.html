The Lead
​
UAE hiring recovers, Saudi Arabia cools
UAE hiring rose 4% quarter-over-quarter in Q3 2026, reversing a 4% contraction in Q2, according to Cooper Fitch's Gulf Employment Index. Saudi Arabia's hiring edged up just 1.5%, and several large employers have asked Cooper Fitch to pause searches as Houthi attacks escalate. CEO Trefor Murphy expects Saudi hiring to contract in Q4 'for sure,' while the UAE stays flat or slightly up. Energy hiring rose 7% on Saudi and UAE gas contracts.

The IMF now sees the GCC's non-oil economy shrinking this year, adding pressure on private businesses already facing war-related uncertainty. The UAE's rebound may not survive a fourth quarter shaped by a war that keeps Gulf budgets tight.

IMF Regional Economic Outlook
​
02Gulf war economy
Gulf economies to shrink 4.8% in 2026
The IMF now expects Gulf economies to contract 4.8% this year, a 6.8 percentage point cut from the 2% expansion it projected in April. It expects them to rebound to 9.1% growth in 2027. The region posted 4.5% growth last year.

Bahrain is projected to shrink by 11.3%, Kuwait by 16.7%, and Qatar by 15.6%. Saudi Arabia is expected to contract 3.3% and the UAE 3.9%, while Oman alone grows at 4.1%. Oil and gas production among Gulf exporters fell from 33 million barrels a day in February to about 19 million in May. Qatar's output fell as much as 86%.

The bloc's non-oil economy is forecast to contract 0.8% this year before returning to 4.5% growth in 2027. Oil prices are projected to average close to $96 per barrel in 2026 and $87 in 2027. IMF Middle East and Central Asia director Jihad Azour called the war a 'severe shock.'

The forecast assumes an end to hostilities and a gradual resumption of shipping through the Strait of Hormuz in early 2027. The combined fiscal deficit widens to 2.3% of GDP from 0.8%, while inflation is projected at 2.7%. Gulf budgets built on oil at $96 a barrel will feel very different from budgets built on $76.
Source: The National Business

03Houthi front
Reportedly, Houthi strikes hit Saudi oil sites, US staff ordered out
Reportedly, debris from a Houthi-fired drone intercepted by Saudi air defences on 21 October killed two residents, MEED reported. Another two were injured at the Shaybah oil field development in the Eastern Province, MEED reported. A separate attack on Dammam airport also reportedly killed two people, MEED reported.

The US State Department ordered non-emergency staff to leave Saudi Arabia over reported Houthi attacks, Bloomberg reported. Aramco evacuated its Dhahran headquarters after an alarm, Bloomberg reported. US media say a joint assault on Iran is being planned for after the 3 November midterms.
Source: MEED Saudi Arabia

04Arrows, eventually
Robinhood picks DIFC for 2027 Middle East debut
Robinhood received DFSA approval to offer trading and custody across all 10 approved asset classes from DIFC, including equities, cryptocurrencies, ETFs and investment tokens. The platform plans to start offering products in the region in 2027.

CEO Vlad Tenev said the DIFC expansion 'brings us closer to building a global platform that enables anyone, anywhere to own the highest quality financial assets.' DIFC registered 2,318 new companies in the 12 months to end-June, a 30% annual increase. The highest quality financial assets will now be reachable through a platform named after a bird that flies away when markets drop.
Source: The National Business

05Abu Dhabi property
Abu Dhabi property launches surge as Dubai's wane
Abu Dhabi will announce a new housing launch roughly every three days until the end of the year, with about 30 projects in the pipeline. Dubai's off-plan launches have nearly dried up during the Iran war.

Abu Dhabi also froze rent increases on lease renewals in June, and rents have since begun falling across several markets. UAE developer Arada entered Saudi Arabia via a joint venture with local developer Xtopia to build a master-planned community in Riyadh. The capital is using the war to build while Dubai waits for the peace to return.
Source: AGBI Construction Editor

The AI Race
​
Ziina secures CBUAE retail payments license
Ziina secured a Retail Payment Services Category III license and authorization for Exchange Business Category IV activities from CBUAE. It is the first homegrown UAE fintech to hold authorizations spanning all three activities. The company serves over 500,000 customers annually and processes $1.5 billion in annualized payment volume. It is used by one in 10 UAE SMEs. The CBUAE has granted Ziina the paperwork to move money for 500,000 customers, a crowd that would fill a small city.
Business
​
Adia unit co-invests in $12.1bn Qiagen takeover
Luxinva, a subsidiary of Abu Dhabi sovereign fund Adia, is co-investing with CPP Investments in EQT's EUR 10.8 billion takeover of German biotech firm Qiagen. The price is EUR 46.50 per share, a 40.3% premium. Closing is expected mid-2027 pending German regulatory approval. Adia is picking up a stake in a German company while the war economy constrains Gulf growth.
QatarEnergy and TotalEnergies to build Lebanon solar plant
QatarEnergy and TotalEnergies signed an agreement to develop a solar power plant in Lebanon. It is the first project under a broader Qatar-Lebanon energy cooperation framework. Qatar is using energy deals to build political ties with Lebanon. The solar plant gives Lebanon a source of electricity that does not depend on fuel imports.
Bahrain opens bids for Hawar Islands water projects
Bahrain opened bids for a seawater intake and outfall system. It will serve a planned desalination plant in the Hawar Islands archipelago. The project supports water security for the islands. Bahrain is building water infrastructure even while its economy is projected to shrink by 11.3% this year.
Saudi Vrtx raises $3.6m for embedded finance
Saudi fintech startup Vrtx raised $3.6 million to expand its embedded finance platform. The company helps businesses integrate financial services into their own products. Vrtx is betting that businesses will want payment and lending tools built directly into their software.
UAE's Ziina secures central bank approvals
UAE fintech Ziina secured a Retail Payment Services Category III license and authorization for Exchange Business Category IV activities from the central bank. The company serves over 500,000 customers and processes $1.5 billion in annualized payment volume.

Saudi safety institute wins international award
Saudi Arabia's Standard Arabia Training Institute won the IPAF Training Centre of the Year award at the IAPA 2026 ceremony in Istanbul. The institute organized what it described, according to the institute, as the Kingdom's largest-ever Mobile Elevating Work Platform safety awareness initiative. The award recognizes training centres that improve safety standards. Training people to work safely at height is the kind of unglamorous work that saves lives on Gulf construction sites every week.

UAE's wealthy rank third globally in life satisfaction
An HSBC study found 83% of more than 700 high-net-worth individuals surveyed in the UAE are satisfied with quality of life. The country places behind only Mexico and India. Half expect a significant financial inheritance, with an average expected inheritance of around $545,000. Half expect an inheritance, making satisfaction a family heirloom.
