The Lead · GCC & SAUDI
A Panjabi 40-year bet on Dubai still hinges, analysts say, on one shipping lane that Iran can close.
​
Jacky Panjabi arrived in Dubai in 1985 during the Iran-Iraq war and spotted an air conditioner price anomaly. That first trade became Jacky's Electronics, a multibillion-dollar electronics distribution and retail business with operations in the UAE, Hong Kong, and Africa. Business was doubling every year, he told AGBI.

But the current supply-chain crisis has the founder making a starker calculation. 'We can't ship anything' when it comes to bulkier electronics, Panjabi said. The Strait of Hormuz, which carries roughly 3.7 million barrels a day of oil, is also the only sea route for his containers. A single container from Europe that cost $3,000 before the war now runs $7,000, and it peaked at $17,000.

The company built its own 100,000-square-foot facility in Jebel Ali in 1996, moving the family from Hong Kong the same year. Now it is expanding into robotics, large-format digital printers, industrial digital presses, and commercial 3D printers. As Gulf competitors rush to build land routes, Panjabi is still waiting for the sea lane to reopen.
What to do with it
Reread your freight contracts today. If your goods move by sea through Hormuz, your costs are no longer the same as last quarter.

Jaywan Goes Global
The UAE's domestic card scheme Jaywan is expanding globally through an agreement between Al Etihad Payments and China's UnionPay International. The deal allows Jaywan cardholders to make payments and withdraw cash at more than 100 million points of sale across 183 countries.

The UAE also executed its first cross-border transaction using the digital AED with China. That is a clear signal that the central bank is pushing its payment rails beyond the region.

This move hands Gulf banks a card scheme that works abroad without relying on Visa or Mastercard. For merchants, that means lower acceptance fees when Gulf tourists arrive. For the Gulf, it is another step in building payment infrastructure the West cannot switch off. Ask your bank about Jaywan acceptance. It is no longer a domestic-only play.

GCC & SAUDI
Riyadh Opens Year Two of Tawazoun
The Royal Commission for Riyadh City is opening applications for year two of the Real Estate Balance Program, known as Tawazoun. The scheme offers 10,000 to 40,000 pre-planned residential plots at no more than SAR 1,500 per square meter.

Eligibility is limited to married citizens or singles over 25 who have no prior property ownership and at least three years of Riyadh residency. Year one's December lottery allocated just over 10,000 plots across eight Riyadh districts.

The Ladun-Al Ayuni consortium won a SAR 2.4 billion contract to build out the infrastructure. For Saudi families, this is a rare chance to own land in Riyadh at a price below market. For contractors, it is a steady pipeline from the government. If you are a Saudi citizen under 40, check your eligibility before applications close on September 15.

GCC & SAUDI
Saudi FDI Hits SAR 1.12 Trillion
Foreign direct investment in Saudi Arabia reached SAR 1.12 trillion at the end of the first quarter of 2026, up 12% year on year. Total foreign investment rose 18% year on year to SAR 3.53 trillion. FDI accounted for 32% of total foreign investment.

Portfolio investments accounted for SAR 1.47 trillion, and other investments stood at SAR 940.7 billion. The numbers show that foreign capital is coming into the kingdom even as the region is at war.

The mix is notable: more money is staying for the long term. FDI is a lagging indicator, so the real story is that investors are betting on Saudi Arabia after the war ends, not before. If your Saudi market entry is waiting for a better moment, this data says the moment is now.

Nvidia Turns Chips Into Collateral
Nvidia signed non-binding deals with Apollo, BlackRock, Blackstone, Brookfield, Goldman Sachs and KKR to raise over $500 billion for AI data center financing. The pitch is straightforward: GPUs are now productive assets, not just hardware, so they can back institutional debt. This turns the AI buildout into an asset class where capital markets fund data centers the way they fund toll roads.
What it means here
Gulf sovereign funds and family offices can now enter AI infrastructure on the same institutional terms as BlackRock, rather than as minority guests.
