The Lead
Gulf patience with Washington is wearing thin as the region debates the value of hosting US bases.
​
Five Gulf governments are now openly debating the value of continuing to host large US military installations, according to officials familiar with the discussions. Saudi Arabia, the UAE, Qatar, Kuwait, and Bahrain are united in frustration with the Trump administration's stop-start approach to the Iran war. The discontent, officials say, is at its highest point since US-Israeli strikes on Iran began in February.

The next real test comes in late October, when the Future Investment Initiative convenes in Riyadh. With the war unresolved, attendance by global CEOs will signal whether the region's business class still sees the Gulf as a safe bet. The frustration is not just diplomatic; it is structural. Gulf capitals want a security relationship that does not lurch with each White House policy shift.

For Gulf operators, the message is to plan for a more transactional US relationship. Base-hosting debates rarely become removal decisions, but the shift from unspoken acceptance to open questioning is a change in the region's political weather. Expect procurement and partnership decisions to be framed more explicitly around regional autonomy. Track FII attendance in October as a signal of global business confidence in the Gulf.

02​
Hormuz shipping slows after tanker attacks, according to shipping sources.
Only five commodity vessels transited the Strait of Hormuz on Saturday, with none registered for Sunday, compared to 31 the previous weekend, Kpler data shows. The slowdown follows attacks on two ADNOC tankers on Thursday and a third on Friday, according to reports. Iran and Oman are reportedly inching closer to a shipping map agreement for the strait.

The bottleneck is already reshaping Saudi oil logistics. Aramco is pushing loadings back into the Gulf as the Houthi campaign makes the Red Sea workaround untenable. Four Chinese buyers have not received September crude allocations at all, and Saudi shipments to Asia fell below 3 million barrels per day in July, down from a 2025 average of 4.9 million.

For Gulf operators, the Hormuz risk premium is now a hard cost, not a tail risk. Charterers are refusing both the Hormuz and Red Sea routes. The shipping map talks are the single most important diplomatic track to watch for any business dependent on Gulf energy exports. Monitor the Iran-Oman shipping map talks; a deal would be the fastest way to ease the transit risk premium.

03​
Saudi inflation holds at 1.8%
Saudi inflation held steady at 1.8% for a third consecutive month in July, matching June and May. Housing costs remained the main driver, rising 4.2% year on year, with actual rents up 4.3%. Jewellery and watch prices jumped 12%.

Wholesale prices rose 5.0% year on year in July, up from 4.8% in June. Producer prices climbed 8.5%, driven by a 66.7% surge in basic chemicals. The gap between consumer and producer inflation is widening, but the consumer side stays anchored by housing supply and price-monitoring measures.

For Gulf operators, the stable headline number masks cost pressure further up the chain. Businesses importing chemicals or refined products should lock in supplier contracts now before producer-level price rises filter through. Use the stable CPI window to renegotiate supplier terms before wholesale cost pressure lands.
​
AI valet parking nears Saudi launch
OMODA 4 will arrive in Saudi Arabia with a Super AI Cockpit powered by a large language model and SIVP, a smart valet parking system that handles space recognition, route planning, and obstacle response on its own. The car is pitched at young, connected Saudi drivers, with a design the company calls Cyber Mecha.

The SIVP system targets the Gulf's least glamorous driving ritual: circling a hot, crowded mall car park while the AC fights the outside heat. If the system works as claimed, it turns parking from a chore into a hands-free feature.

For Gulf operators, the launch signals that Chinese automakers are now selling AI convenience as their entry point into Saudi's young buyer market, not just lower prices. Dealerships and aftermarket services should prepare for more AI-forward models arriving with software subscriptions bundled into the sticker price. Watch the local launch timing and pricing; it will set the benchmark for AI features in mid-priced cars across the Gulf.
​
04​
Neom hydrogen project nears commissioning
Construction is complete on the $8.5 billion Neom hydrogen project at Oxagon, and the facility is entering the commissioning phase. Commercial operations are targeted for 2027.

The plant is meant to produce green hydrogen at scale, placing Saudi Arabia at the front of a global race that has produced far more announcements than operating plants. The 2027 target gives the project a real deadline against a backdrop of scepticism about green hydrogen economics.

For Gulf operators, the project is a test case for whether the region's hydrogen ambitions survive contact with real construction schedules. Suppliers and service firms with commissioning expertise should position now for the ramp-up. Track the 2027 commercial operations target; it will validate or puncture the region's green hydrogen narrative.

Oman teaches local etiquette to its own citizens
The National Museum in Muscat launched an Omani Etiquette summer programme at Bayt Al Greiza on Sunday. The state-backed programme aims to instil local social codes in participants. At a time when Gulf tourism is growing, Oman wants its own citizens as fluent in hospitality language as its hotel staff. For Gulf readers, it is a quiet signal that soft skills are becoming state policy, not just a private sector line item.
