Reportedly, Fertiliser Exports Down 70% on Hormuz Closure
Middle East fertiliser export volumes have fallen about 70% since the Iran war began on February 28, according to shipping data firm Kpler. September shipments totalled 994,000 tonnes, down from 3.41 million tonnes a year earlier. The collapse follows the effective closure of the Strait of Hormuz, which has reportedly throttled the region's ability to move product to global buyers.

The Gulf accounts for about 25% to 30% of global sulphur production and as much as half of all seaborne trade in the commodity. It also produces roughly 10% to 12% of world urea, but its share of seaborne trade is far higher at 30% to 40%. Qatar's LNG facilities have been severely damaged in the fighting, and oil prices have surged above $120 a barrel.

China has emerged as the swing supplier of fertilisers while Russia has absorbed displaced demand. The UN Food and Agriculture Organization's food price index averaged 136 points in September, up 1.5% month on month and 5.8% year on year. For food-importing Gulf states, a fertiliser crunch now becomes a food-price problem later. The region's farms and food importers will feel the delayed effect in 2027. Fewer fertilised fields globally will then mean higher grain and produce prices in Gulf supermarkets.

02IATA survey
Flyers Want Biometrics, Fewer Visa Hurdles
Faster checks and fewer visa hurdles topped the wish list in the IATA 2026 Global Passenger Survey, which drew more than 10,000 responses from over 200 countries. It found 58% of passengers used biometrics in the past 12 months, up from 50% the year before, with an 87% satisfaction rate. A full 78% said they would share biometric data to skip showing a passport and boarding pass.

The survey, released October 28, also found 49% of travellers used AI to plan a trip. Immigration requirements now put off 43% of passengers, up from 33% last year. Of those deterred by conflict, 72% cite safety as the main concern.

Middle East airspace closures since February 28 have hit the region hard. IATA projects Middle Eastern airlines will swing from a $7.2 billion profit in 2025 to a $4.3 billion loss in 2026. For Gulf carriers, the message from passengers is unambiguous: automate the journey, or lose the traveller to an easier hub. The region's airports can lift passenger conversion by investing in biometric corridors and common-sense visa reform. That is now a mathematical advantage in a market where traffic is still 7.5% below last year.

03One penalty, please
UAE Caps E-Invoicing Fines at Dh50,000
The UAE has capped e-invoicing implementation fines at Dh50,000 over any 12 consecutive months, replacing an uncapped Dh5,000 monthly charge. The government also removed the Dh1,000 daily penalty for failing to report an e-invoicing system failure.

A new safeguard means a business that is both invoice issuer and recipient receives only one administrative penalty per violation. Businesses with revenue of Dh50 million or more must appoint an Accredited Service Provider by October 30, 2026, with mandatory e-invoicing from January 1, 2027.

Smaller businesses, those under Dh50 million in revenue, must appoint a provider by March 31, 2027, and begin mandatory e-invoicing on July 1, 2027. The new structure gives companies a predictable cost ceiling and removes a penalty that could quietly add up to more than the fine itself. The daily penalty is gone, so a system failure no longer comes with a meter running.

Petro Rabigh Net Profit Hits $687m in Q3
Petro Rabigh, the Saudi Aramco-majority-owned refiner, posted a net profit of $687 million in the third quarter of 2026. The result was driven by improved refined-product margins and higher sales volumes from better plant utilisation. Plant utilisation and refined-product margins did not merely improve; they became the quarter.
