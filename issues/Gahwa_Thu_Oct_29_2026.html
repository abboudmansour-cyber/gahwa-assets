The Lead

Mubadala Shelves Porto Sale
Porto Sudeste’s $5 billion sale collapsed after Mubadala Capital and Trafigura pulled the port from the market. Bids of $3 to $3.5 billion came in 30% below their $5 billion target. The Brazilian iron-ore port shipped a record 27.8 million tonnes in 2025, up 27% from 2024. It handled 13.6 million tonnes in the first half of 2026. It is running at about 56% of its 50 million-ton capacity.

Iron ore freight costs have more than doubled to about $42 a tonne from $19 a year earlier, and Brazilian miners are cutting output. Itaminas is halting one of four processing plants, with about 300 workers on collective leave. CSN Mineração cut 2026 guidance to 39-41 million tonnes from 45-47 million. Porto Sudeste is investing BRL 177 million in liquid-bulk berthing due in the first half of 2027. That would let it handle up to 144 liquid-bulk operations a year versus 19 in 2025 and add 50 million tonnes of transfer capacity.

The pair also look at buying debt in Raizen, the Brazilian sugar and ethanol group restructuring about BRL 65 billion. Creditors would swap debt for more than 80% of capital. A bid by BlackRock's GIP, I Squared Capital and Stonepeak for Porto Sudeste failed at the price stage. A port that cannot sell at its number still earns from whatever iron ore does move, so the deal is on ice, not dead.

02Nothing through the strait
Adnoc's Hormuz Bypass Nears
Adnoc's expanded West-East pipeline could be operational as soon as early 2027, officials say. It would double the UAE's capacity to export crude while bypassing the Strait of Hormuz. Upper Zakum could be one of the first grades routed through the line. The UAE is also expanding the 360-kilometre Habshan-Fujairah pipeline. The Philippines is in talks with Abu Dhabi, Riyadh and Kuwait to store crude and refined fuel.

The race to build Hormuz bypasses now spans the whole Gulf. Saudi Energy Minister Prince Abdulaziz bin Salman has said the Kingdom could convert a domestic gas line to carry crude. This would add up to 1.2 million bpd within seven or eight weeks. Iraq and Syria are reviving a pipeline damaged in 2003. Abu Dhabi pipe maker Al Gharbia Pipe Company says every country in the region is considering a new route. US Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent told the World Petroleum Congress the strait will be a worthless piece of water within two years. Up to 70% of energy will move through pipelines.

The windfall is already visible in the UAE. Al Gharbia, a joint venture of Senaat, JFE Steel and Marubeni-Itochu Steel, expects its production to recover from 60,000 tonnes this year to 200,000 tonnes next year. War-related supply disruptions are easing. The first mover to complete a bypass gains pricing power with buyers who need guaranteed delivery. Adnoc's early 2027 opening matters more than the pipe's diameter.

Egypt Funds Hit EGP 500B
Egypt's fund industry crossed EGP 500 billion in the third quarter, with net asset value up 7.16% to EGP 504.71 billion. Assets under management rose 7.27% to EGP 512.84 billion, and eight new funds launched, taking the total to 232. Money market funds hold about 60% of assets.

The retail side is even hotter. Banque du Caire's retail IPO tranche closed 27 times oversubscribed, with EGP 49.2 billion chasing EGP 1.8 billion at EGP 5.30 per share. Pro-rata allocation lands near 3.7%, and trading is expected to start Monday. That is retail demand at a level Egypt has rarely printed.

Gulf fund managers should read it as a liquidity signal, not just a local story. Egypt is importing 15 million barrels of crude in 4Q at around $1.5 billion, funded by supplier credit. Its real-rate cushion above inflation is about 5.1 points. When Egyptian retail piles into a bank listing at 27 times, the Gulf's larger pools of dry powder are watching. The retail IPO tranche did not close; it collapsed under the weight of EGP 49.2 billion.

04World Bank warning
Energy Prices to Jump 32%
The World Bank forecasts global energy prices will rise 32% this year, with Brent crude averaging $91 a barrel in 2026, up from $69 in 2025, then easing to $79 in 2027. The bank sees global commodity prices climbing 23% this year and easing 7% in 2027. Global oil supply is projected to decline 5.2% in 2026, the largest drop since the 1980s oil embargo, reportedly. Food commodity prices are forecast to climb about 10% this year and 7% more in 2027. The IMF expects global headline inflation to reach 4.7% in 2026. The bank has provided $8.7 billion in support to 25 countries hit by the Iran war, and says it can scale that up to $80 to 100 billion versus a current estimate of $50 to 60 billion.

An adverse scenario puts Brent at $105 on average in 2027, with food prices 12% above baseline. Metals and minerals are projected to rise 25% and hit a new high in 2026, while fertiliser prices climb 14%. For Gulf producers, the windfall is cushioned by higher energy prices, but Gulf importers pay the food and metal side of this equation. Gulf energy exporters get the revenue, while Gulf food importers take the margins, so the winners and losers sit inside the same economy.

The AI Race
​
Azure's True Size Emerges
The figure lands in the same week the company reported 48% cloud growth and a $39 billion capital expenditure bill. For years investors only saw a percentage, so this is the first hard number. It shows Azure is closing in on AWS, turning a long-running cloud rivalry into a measurable race.

The AI stress test around it was less kind. Alphabet shares dropped more than 10% after hours on revenue of $111.2 billion ex-partner payouts against expectations of $111.3 billion. Earnings per share came in at $2.12 versus a $2.99 estimate. Google Cloud beat, but equity investments produced an $8.7 billion loss in Q3. With Microsoft and Alphabet about 11% of the S&P 500, one miss stalls the week's AI rally. Morgan Stanley still expects the biggest tech firms to lift combined capital expenditure by more than 50% in 2027. Gulf operators finally see the true dollar cost of a rival public cloud, which is the pricing reference they need before committing their own infrastructure budgets.

Moody's DIFC Unit Fined for Data Breaches
The DFSA fined Moody's Middle East unit $585,825 over seven incidents of inadvertent data disclosure between December 2023 and May 2026. Four involved confidential data sent to external recipients. The unit has been licensed in the DIFC since 2012. The fine is small against the firm's global revenue but lands on a regulator that rarely names data failures. Seven incidents of inadvertent disclosure now carry a $585,825 price tag.
Amanat Buys UK Care Homes
Reportedly, Amanat acquired UK care provider Cornerstone Healthcare for up to £76 million, giving it 283 beds outside the Gulf for the first time. The deal extends a Gulf healthcare investor into a market with ageing demographics and stable government payment. It is a small move in capital terms but a first footprint abroad.
Saudi Aramco offers European buyers spot crude after Yanbu outage
Aramco offered European buyers spot crude for October and November loading from Yanbu or Egypt's Sidi Kerir after the drone attack knocked the East-West pipeline offline. November term allocations were restored. The pipeline carries around 5 million bpd after Iran effectively closed the Strait of Hormuz in early March.
