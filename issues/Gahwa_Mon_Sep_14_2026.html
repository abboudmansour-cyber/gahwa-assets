The Lead
​
Riyadh has five days of oil left for export if a single buried pipe stays shut.
Saudi Arabia could run out of crude stocks for export within days after drone attacks reportedly forced the East-West pipeline to shut, removing up to 4% of global supply from the market. Yanbu, the kingdom's Red Sea export terminal, holds roughly 35 million barrels and enough stocks for only five to seven days of loadings. Repairs could take five to six weeks, though partial flows may resume sooner. The pipeline shutdown came after attacks from Iraq, while Houthi forces also struck a military base in Najran and expanded their control over Yemen's Red Sea coast, according to officials. The kingdom retaliated with 129 strikes over the weekend using F-15 and Typhoon jets based at Khamis Mushait and Taif, according to officials. More than 500 government fighters have been killed and 1,500 wounded on Yemen's west coast since early August, officials say. Around 85,000 people have fled the fighting in Yemen since July.

Egypt's Ain Sukhna and Sidi Kerir terminals add roughly 38 million barrels of alternative storage, but the math is unforgiving. A five-day stock buffer against a five-week repair timeline leaves a supply gap that no amount of diplomacy can paper over. European and Asian buyers meeting in Singapore this week will be recalculating their October loading plans around a Gulf that suddenly looks less reliable.

Why the Red Sea matters Every barrel that misses Yanbu adds a week of sailing around the Cape of Good Hope. Freight rates for clean tankers were already climbing before this outage, and insurers have begun quoting war-risk premiums that make every alternative route more expensive. The squeeze tightens exactly as Opec+ has chosen to hold production policy unchanged, leaving no spare barrels to backfill a prolonged Saudi shortfall. One buried pipe near the kingdom's eastern fields is now the single most expensive piece of steel in the oil market. A repair team in Saudi Arabia now holds more sway over global diesel prices than any Opec+ minister.

02Energy Deals
UAE's EUR 40 billion Germany pledge takes shape
Adnoc, XRG and Masdar signed agreements that could unlock more than EUR 5 billion of investment across gas, chemicals, offshore wind, battery storage and AI in Germany. The deals are part of the EUR 40 billion package the UAE unveiled for Germany, and President MBZ's visit produced 29 business agreements worth more than EUR 9.36 billion total.

Adnoc and RWE signed a letter of intent toward up to two long-term LNG sales agreements with deliveries starting in the early 2030s from Ruwais, Das Island, and other sources. Adnoc already supplies 1.6 million tonnes per year to Germany, enough for more than 3.5 million households. Ta'ziz and Covestro agreed on next steps toward a world-scale MDI plant in Abu Dhabi producing up to 600,000 tonnes a year, the UAE's first domestic MDI plant and part of a 14-chemical pipeline that could attract up to $10 billion.

Why Berlin is listening Germany wants energy security it cannot get from Russia, and the UAE wants industrial technology and a fortress of European demand for its hydrocarbons and chemicals. The investment council set up to identify projects will now have real money behind it, and the next wave will likely target German data centres and battery storage, sectors where the UAE can bring both capital and clean power. The quickest route to a German industrial renaissance now runs through Abu Dhabi's balance sheet, and the contracts are already being signed.

03Hormuz Transits
Hormuz vessel transits drop to near halt
Commodity vessel transits through the Strait of Hormuz fell to single digits per day over the weekend, with only four vessels exiting the Gulf and ten entering. That is well below the 10-day average of 14 exits per day. The drop follows an Iranian cargo vessel struck near Qeshm Island that killed one person and wounded four, an attack Tehran blamed on a terrorist enemy, according to officials. Threats to shipping have intensified around both Hormuz and the Bab el-Mandeb straits. Oman has delayed planned talks between Tehran and Gulf states on strait security.

For Gulf exporters and importers, every day of reduced Hormuz traffic means delayed deliveries, higher insurance premiums, and the creeping realization that the world's most important energy chokepoint is once again being priced as a war risk rather than a shipping lane. A strait that carries a fifth of the world's oil now moves less traffic on a weekend than a Dubai marina, and the insurance market has noticed.

04Egyptian Fintech
Egypt's Zeal raises $10 million for global push
Egypt-based fintech Zeal raised $10 million, bringing its total funding to $14 million, with investors undisclosed. Founded in 2019 by Omar Ebeid and Belal Mohamed, the company has signed contracts with payment acquirers globally to activate its technology on more than 4 million card machines over the next 24 months.

Zeal previously raised $4 million in 2024 led by Raed Ventures and Cur8 Capital, with earlier backing from Pinnacle Capital. The company was named Team of the Year at the UK FinTech Awards 2026, and co-founder Belal Mohamed was named Innovator of the Year. Zeal is part of the Scale Up by Endeavor programme.

Why the Gulf cares As payment volumes shift from cash to card across Egypt, Saudi Arabia and the UAE, the software that sits between the terminal and the bank is the new battleground. A company that can land four million machines in two years has distribution muscle worth watching, especially given Egypt's 100-million-plus population and the Gulf's push for cashless economies. Four million card machines now have a common software heart, and it beats in Cairo rather than London or San Francisco.

Saudi VC Fund
STV's AI fund secures Arcapita backing
Arcapita backs Saudi venture capital firm STV, though the size was not disclosed. The fund's institutional investor base already includes Google, regional semi-sovereign entities and endowment investors. STV has invested in four AI-native startups: Sawt for Arabic AI voice agents, Clarity for agentic AI customer service analytics, Signit for Saudi AI legal tech, and Stream for Saudi billing and payments.

The fund is betting on a market where application-layer AI captured more than $19 billion in enterprise spending in 2025, and AI-native ventures are outearning incumbents two-to-one. Arcapita, with over 30 years of operations and $32 billion in total transaction value, brings a global LP network.

What it signals Gulf sovereign-adjacent capital is no longer treating AI as a foreign tech story to import, but as a local asset class to own. The next Saudi AI startup with strong revenue will not want for term sheets, and the presence of Google as an LP means portfolio companies get a direct line to one of the world's largest AI distribution platforms. The Saudi AI startup scene just gained a backer with three decades of deal flow and a second-to-none contact list.

The AI Race
​
Saudi Arabia opens the AI hardware layer
LEAP 2026 day one landed more than $15 billion in technology launches, investments and partnerships. The investor room alone held 1,289 investors from 1,016 firms representing $14.5 trillion in assets under management. AMD, Cisco and HUMAIN announced production AI infrastructure powered by AMD Instinct GPUs is now live in Saudi Arabia. The partners plan up to 250 megawatts of additional capacity beginning in 2027. AWS confirmed its first Saudi cloud region opens in December as part of a planned investment exceeding $5.3 billion.

Microsoft's Saudi Arabia East data centre region goes live in November, and HUMAIN's AI PC running Windows reaches enterprise availability on September 20. HUMAIN and Microsoft plan to offer HUMAIN ONE alongside Microsoft 365 including Copilot, targeting one million users across the Middle East and Africa. Adobe became the first global software company to move AI workloads onto HUMAIN's infrastructure. The kingdom is no longer asking foreign clouds to serve its market from Bahrain or Frankfurt. It is rebuilding the physical layer of AI inside its own borders. The kingdom will soon be the cheapest place in the region to run AI workloads at scale, with data residency solved once AWS and Microsoft regions are live.

Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund is providing $49.6 million in debtor-in-possession financing to Liv Golf. The league filed for Chapter 11 protection five months after PIF withdrew multibillion-dollar backing. The new financing keeps the league operating through restructuring.
EGA completes acquisition of 80% stake in Italian recycler Eco Green
Emirates Global Aluminium completed its acquisition of an 80% stake in Eco Green, an Italian aluminium recycler. Eco Green operates two plants in northeast Italy handling more than 70,000 tonnes a year. The deal first announced in April strengthens EGA's European recycling footprint.

QatarEnergy acquired 30% stakes in Blocks 8 and 22 offshore Angola. Shell holds 50% and Angola's state energy company Sonangol E&P holds 20% in the respective blocks. The move adds to QatarEnergy's growing African upstream portfolio.
Heisco wins $359 million Kuwait oil storage tank contract
Kuwait's Heisco won a $359 million contract to develop storage tanks for Jurassic Light Oil export facilities. The work is part of Kuwait's push to expand its export capacity. The contract covers construction of new storage infrastructure for the lighter crude grade.

Travel firm Almatar is targeting a Tadawul listing next year, with timing still open, CEO Faisal Al Rajhi told Aleqtisadiah. The company had originally planned to float in the fourth quarter of 2025. The Last Sip
​
Which Gulf city has more registered cats than cars?
The answer
Kuwait City's stray cat population is enormous. Estimates run into the hundreds of thousands, and the city has tried feeding programs, trap-neuter-return campaigns, and public awareness drives. The cats have accepted the food and ignored the advice. The cats now sit on the car hoods, which is the closest thing to municipal planning they recognize.
Why does Saudi Arabia have a ministry for vegetation cover?
The answer
The National Center for Vegetation Cover was set up to reverse desertification and plant millions of trees as part of Vision 2030. It is now offering 103 projects worth SAR 500 million. The desert is being treated as infrastructure. A country whose whole economy once ran on oil is now bidding out contracts for shade.
What is the most expensive piece of steel in the oil market right now?
The answer
The East-West pipeline near Saudi Arabia's eastern fields. One buried pipe is holding up to 4% of global oil supply until repair teams can reach it. The rest of the market is repricing every barrel around its repair schedule. The whole global energy market is now a hostage negotiation with a welding crew.
