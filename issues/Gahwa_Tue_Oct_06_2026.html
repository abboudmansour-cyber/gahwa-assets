The Lead
​
PIF's Folk Maritime halts expansion
Folk Maritime, the PIF-owned container line, has paused its expansion as operating costs rose 30 to 40%, with some individual expenses more than doubling. CEO Poul Hestbaek says a round trip between India and the Red Sea that normally takes about 20 days now takes 30. The carrier now moves closer to 4,000 containers a week, below the roughly 5,000 needed to hit its 250,000 annual target.

Vessel prices have roughly doubled compared with pre-Covid averages. Folk operates six ships, five of them owned, and has shifted around 75% of its capacity into the Red Sea, up from about 50% before the conflict. It has not crossed Bab al-Mandab "for a while" because the Houthi target picture remains uncertain. Decisions are now week-by-week.

Gulf shipping costs feed directly into the price of goods on shelves. If the Red Sea reopens steadily after the Yemeni advance, that would ease the pressure. For now, the carrier is choosing caution over capacity. Gulf logistics providers waiting for expansion will have to wait longer.

02Concrete arrives before the concrete
Eagle Hills signs Syria framework deal
Eagle Hills is betting on Damascus and Latakia. UAE-based developer Eagle Hills has signed a framework agreement with the Syrian government for urban and tourism projects in Damascus and Latakia. The deal covers the Tadamun Towers residential development, branded Damascus Hills, and the Eco Life tourism project on the coast. It was signed Monday in the presence of President Ahmad al-Sharaa.

Syria's housing deficit could exceed 2 million units by 2030, according to its public works minister. Eagle Hills says the projects could generate more than 40,000 direct and indirect jobs, including over 10,000 permanent positions. Syria recorded 3.5 million visitors in the first half of 2026, up 111% from the same period last year, with international arrivals up 448%.

The deal is part of an $18 billion planned investment pipeline and the first major UAE real estate move into Syria since the war. The numbers are attractive, but the risk is geopolitical, not construction. The developer is betting on a country whose tourism recovery is real but whose security future is not yet settled. The first-mover advantage is enormous, and so is the political exposure.

03Six billion barrels, none handy
Aramco warns oil stocks scarily thin
Saudi Aramco CEO Amin Nasser says fewer than 6 billion barrels of commercial oil inventories remain, down from almost 10 billion when the war began. Less than 10% of that is practically available. Rebuilding stocks could add 2 million barrels a day of demand and take up to two years. Brent is trading around $100 a barrel.

The G7 has agreed to release up to 100 million barrels of crude and diesel from emergency reserves over four months. Seven major Gulf producers shipped 12.8 million barrels a day in September, about 6 million below February levels. Saudi crude flows through the Sidi Kerir terminal more than doubled to 2.3 million barrels a day in August.

The East-West pipeline is operating normally and Aramco has ramped it past 80% of its 7 million barrel per day capacity. That buffer, according to Aramco, is what keeps exports flowing even if the strait stays shut. The reserve release buys time, but the CEO's message is that time is not the same as supply. A full reopening of the Strait of Hormuz would still leave the market short for up to two years.

04No bank in the middle
Zamanat tokenizes GCC private credit fund
A Dubai fund is putting private credit on a blockchain, a shared digital ledger that records ownership without a bank in the middle. Zamanat is targeting up to $100 million for lending to profitable trading and services companies in the GCC. Entry is set at $250,000, and investors receive ZM1 tokens on the ZIGChain. The fund launched on 10 September with an anchor commitment from Disrupt Group and is now evaluating a $40 to 50 million pipeline of prospective deals.

Truleum Venture Partners manages the fund and Apex Group handles administration. Target borrowers need $2 to 5 million, and no single borrower or platform can exceed 25% of the fund's net asset value. The strategy is senior secured lending in most cases, with co-investments in Saudi Arabia under consideration. Capital had not been deployed at the time of the interview.

The move follows a wave of Gulf interest in tokenized private markets. Mubadala Capital partnered with Kaio to tokenize a private-markets fund in July. The pitch is simple: smaller investors get access, and the yield flows through tokens. The risk is the same as any private credit book, just with a blockchain wrapper. The same lending risks apply, but the investor pool will be wider and the redemption story less tested.

The AI Race
​
Gulf buys bigger seat at OpenAI table
MGX and BlackRock are reportedly negotiating to join a $30 billion financing round for OpenAI, with UAE funders expected to form a syndicate investing up to $10 billion. The round would value OpenAI at $1.2 trillion. MGX AI Investment chief Ali Osman says MGX plans to spend up to $10 billion a year on AI infrastructure.

MGX is already a backer of OpenAI, xAI and Anthropic, and co-led Anthropic's $30 billion Series G. The money buys influence over where data centres, chips and talent are built, and Gulf states want that physical infrastructure on their soil. A $30 billion round at a $1.2 trillion valuation would be the largest private financing in AI history, while regulatory scrutiny of AI concentration grows in Washington and Brussels. A stake in OpenAI gives Gulf funds a seat at the table when the future of AI is decided, and the deal is about owning the physical layer, not just the software.
Business
​
Rabigh 2 reaches financial close at $2.58bn
The Rabigh 2 expansion closed financing of $2.58 billion, adding 2.3 GW of combined-cycle capacity in Saudi Arabia's Western Province. ACWA Power holds a 40 percent stake. Financial impact begins in Q2 2029, so the cheques are signed and the construction starts before the returns do. The deal is another step in Saudi Arabia's build-out of gas-fired power to back up renewables.
Aramco hands $45m pipe deal to two local firms
Arabian Pipes Company and Saudi Steel Pipe Company won contracts worth $45 million for supply to Aramco. The orders run 10 and 12 months respectively. Aramco keeps its supply chain spend inside the Kingdom, and two local manufacturers get a year of visible backlog.
Dubai CommerCity expands by $490m
Dubai CommerCity launched a second expansion phase worth $490 million, adding 91,000 square metres of office, retail and logistics space. Delivery runs from Q1 2027 to Q4 2028. Occupancy is nearly 96 percent, so the new space lands into a market that is almost full.
UAE and Jordan start $2.3bn railway
The 360-km Aqaba-Shidiya-Ma'an railway launched, with financial close expected in early 2027. Construction is estimated at five years. The line connects Aqaba port to Jordan's rail network and gives Gulf trade a faster corridor to the Levant.
Jeddah Tower completion pushed to late 2028
Jeddah Tower has reached 116 floors, about 466 metres, with 41 floors still to build. The developer now targets completion in late 2028.

The Last Sip
​
Why does a round trip between India and the Red Sea now take 10 extra days?
The answer
Folk Maritime's CEO says the route now takes about 30 days instead of the normal 20. The reason is the Houthi target picture near Bab al-Mandab, which has kept the carrier from crossing for a while. Extra days mean extra fuel, crew time and insurance. A ship that routes around the Cape of Good Hope adds distance and time, but per analysts it reportedly stays out of missile range. The long way round is now the safe way round, and the freight bill knows it.
Which Gulf fund manager is backing three of the biggest AI labs at once?
The answer
MGX, the Abu Dhabi fund, already backs OpenAI, xAI and Anthropic. It co-led Anthropic's $30 billion Series G, and now plans up to $10 billion a year on AI infrastructure. The fund's money is not spread thin; it is deliberately in every major lab, which means one Gulf entity sits at the table no matter who wins the race. Betting on every runner is expensive, but it does remove the suspense.
