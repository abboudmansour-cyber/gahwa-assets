The Lead
​
Anthropic and OpenAI launch cheaper models on same day
Anthropic and OpenAI both released new flagship models the same day, cutting prices hard to stay competitive. Anthropic's Claude Opus 5.5 costs 40% less than Opus 5 to run and 30% faster on output, at $4 per million input tokens and $20 per million output. OpenAI's GPT-6 Sol comes in at $2 per million input tokens, half the previous generation's price.

Opus 5.5 beat its predecessor and Fable 5.1 on every benchmark Anthropic published, including a jump on TerminalBench 4.0 from 55.8% to 66.4%. OpenAI's Sol scored 33.2% on real-world workflows, up from 28.77% for GPT-5.6 at about half the price. Epoch AI data shows AI costs have been falling roughly 47% per quarter since 2023.

Anthropic says Opus 5.5 is the first model it released since its public call for pacing frontier AI development, tested pre-release by external evaluators. Enterprise results included a 680,000-line codebase migrated in under a day. For Gulf operators, the message is that model cost is no longer the barrier to AI adoption it was six months ago. Teams building on frontier models can now expect to pay half as much for more capable output, shifting the bottleneck from cost to data discipline and deployment.

02Brics bypass
UAE local-currency trade with India hits 15%
About 15% of trade between India and the UAE now bypasses the US dollar and is invoiced in local currencies. The Reserve Bank of India has approved 156 special INR accounts for 123 correspondent banks from 30 countries to settle this trade.

The UAE's total non-oil trade with Brics members topped $312 billion in 2025, up 28.5% from $243 billion a year earlier. That is roughly 31% of all UAE non-oil foreign trade. Brics members took 28% of the UAE's re-exports last year.

In May, Al Etihad Payments, the operator of Aani, picked Montran to build an international gateway starting with a link to India's Unified Payments Interface. That follows the UAE's first cross-border digital AED payment to China via mBridge in January 2024 and the launch of the Jisr CBDC platform in November. A growing share of the UAE's trade now settles outside the dollar, and the infrastructure to expand that share is being built deliberately, not accidentally.

03One of three still off
Alibaba expands UAE data centers as AWS zone stays dark
Alibaba plans to expand its data center footprint in the UAE as part of a push into Europe and the Middle East. The company aims to build a 20 gigawatt global data center network by 2032, which Citi estimates could generate more than $160 billion in external cloud revenue.

This comes as one of AWS's three data-hosting zones in the UAE remains offline after it was damaged in March. AWS still cannot access the data stored there.

Microsoft is also investing $2 billion in cloud computing, AI, and data center operations in Gulf nations, mobilizing $10 billion through 2030. The UAE is now a three-way competition ground for hyperscalers, and the outage shows the downside of concentration. Cloud capacity is arriving faster than it failed, but the AWS outage serves as a reminder that data center risk is now a board level question in the region.

04SME rails
Pemo gets CBUAE approval to expand SME services
Pemo, with 7,000 SME clients, received in-principle approval from the Central Bank of the UAE to broaden its financial services offering to SMEs. The company says more than 7,000 companies use its platform, with about 80 employees serving that base.

Pemo has raised $19 million from investors including Cherry Ventures, Shorooq Partners, Augmentum Fintech and Speedinvest. The company says revenue per head has grown 3.15x from 2024 to 2026. Its month-end close automation claims to drop the process from seven days to three hours.

SME banking remains one of the Gulf's most stubborn gaps, with traditional banks still hesitant to lend to small businesses. Pemo's license path suggests the Central Bank wants more technology-led competition in that segment. Every new CBUAE approval for a fintech serving SMEs widens the hole traditional banks left open.

05Cushioned landing
Dubai state entities take bigger share of contracts
Dubai's state entities are taking a growing share of the emirate's contract awards this year. The public sector push is offsetting a slowdown in off-plan sales and residential construction spending since late February.

The shift mirrors the pattern seen in the 2009 downturn, when state spending cushioned a hard landing in private construction. Dubai's real estate market is still making headlines, but the numbers underneath are cooling.

The UBS Global Real Estate Bubble Index put Dubai at 1.16 for 2026, up from 1.09 last year, with inflation-adjusted home prices rising just 0.4% year-on-year in the second quarter and real rents falling 4%. When the state becomes the buyer of last resort for construction, it usually means private developers have stopped filling the pipeline on their own.

Airtel Money picks London over UAE for IPO
Airtel Money plans to float on the London Stock Exchange, a year after securing approval to list in the UAE. The offering could raise $800 million at an $8-9 billion market cap. That is below the $1.5-2 billion previously expected. The UAE was in the running and lost, which is rare for a fintech listing this size.

EQT, which manages $389 billion in assets, launched a Middle East platform from an ADGM office. Jimmy Mahtani was named GCC chairman. The private equity group now has a formal base in Abu Dhabi, which increasingly wants the world's largest allocators on the ground rather than on a plane.

The Last Sip
​
Why do tanker counts in the Strait of Hormuz matter to a coffee drinker in Riyadh?
The answer
Because three transited on Tuesday, down from a ten-day average of about fifteen. Every barrel that does not move through Hormuz has to go around, and the cost of going around lands in the price of the diesel that moves the coffee, the beans, and the barista. A quiet strait is a loud bill. Maritime fear, as it turns out, is locally sourced.
What do you call a day when two rival AI labs cut prices in the same hour?
The answer
A sale, but for intelligence. The old model is gone: the new unit of trade is a million tokens at $2 or $4, and the thing being discounted is the cost of thinking. Consumers get smarter tools for less; every business that promised AI savings just got new math. Even the robots are in a price war now.
