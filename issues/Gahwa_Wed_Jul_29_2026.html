Saudi Arabia Intercepts Drone Strikes
The U.S. Tehran rejected an Omani proposal for 50-50 joint management of the waterway, instead demanding one lane entirely under its control and a share of the second. Oman, backed by the GCC, had hoped a fee-based joint system would stabilize shipping after months of disruption from the U.S.-Israeli air war on Iran.

The oil market felt the strain immediately. Brent crude, already climbing, moved above $88 a barrel, and shipping insurance premiums for Gulf transits remained elevated.
Operators should review insurance and alternate shipping routes as Hormuz deadlock coincides with direct drone threats to Saudi oil infrastructure.

Brookfield’s PIF Fund Hits $2B
The first close landed at $2 billion, with Brookfield itself writing a $500 million check and institutional investors joining the Public Investment Fund in a vehicle that will park at least half its capital inside the Kingdom.

Brookfield Middle East Partners, first agreed with PIF in October 2024, targets buyouts and growth equity across financial services, business services, consumer, industrials, tech, and healthcare. The firm also received CMA approval to manage investments directly in Saudi, setting up a local academy to train investment talent.

The fund is the latest brick in PIF’s determined effort to pull outside capital into the local market, following $24.5 billion in memorandums with the World Bank and U.S. EXIM and a $2 billion co-investment with I Squared Capital. For regional dealmakers, a $2 billion first close with Brookfield’s discipline attached signals that international capital is finally anchoring, not just visiting. Private equity firms and founders should track BMEP’s sector focus and deployment pace as a live indicator of international appetite for Saudi mid-market deals.

CULTURE
Guggenheim Abu Dhabi Opens December 11
Frank Gehry’s long-awaited Guggenheim Abu Dhabi will finally unlock its doors on December 11, 2026, becoming the largest museum in the network and giving Saadiyat Island its last big cultural anchor.

The 30 galleries will span modern and contemporary art from the 1960s onward, mixing Pollock, Rothko, Warhol, and Basquiat with artists from Asia, Africa, and the Arab world. A Renoir masterpiece from the Abu Dhabi Masterpieces Collection will warm up the public at Louvre Abu Dhabi for a year beforehand, and the opening is bookended by Frieze Abu Dhabi in November and the Grand Prix the week before, effectively turning December into a month-long cultural strafing run for visitors. Hospitality and retail operators should plan for a December visitor surge that blends art, finance, and motorsport into one extended peak season.

GCC & SAUDI
Riyadh Plans 456 Sq Km Expansion
The Royal Commission for Riyadh City completed the initial master plan for a phased development of 456 square kilometers northeast of the capital, connecting to King Khalid International Airport and major highways.

The plan sits inside a pipeline that already includes King Salman International Airport, a $3 billion mixed-use district in King Salman Park, and an estimated 830,000 housing units needed by 2034. If Riyadh’s last decade was about building big, the next looks set to be about stitching big things together before the sprawl outruns the utilities. Infrastructure and real estate firms should position for the northeast corridor now as master planning triggers early contractor and consultant packages.

GCC & SAUDI
Saudi Energy Gets $4.2 Billion Loan
Saudi Energy secured a SAR 15.8 billion sharia-compliant loan from seven local banks, a hefty liquidity injection just as its Q1 net profit rose 89% year-on-year to SAR 1.8 billion and revenue climbed 9% to SAR 21.3 billion.

The borrower’s current liabilities already exceed current assets by SAR 45.8 billion, making the new facility less a growth lever and more a working-capital bridge built with local bank muscle. When a state energy champion borrows $4.2 billion entirely from the neighborhood, it quietly signals that the international debt markets are not the only pocket that matters. Banking and energy investors should track Saudi Energy’s working-capital gap as a leading indicator of capital market activity from the sector.

FAB Joins $40B SoftBank Loan
First Abu Dhabi Bank is taking a nearly $1 billion slice of SoftBank’s $40 billion bridge loan backing its OpenAI bet, while also preparing up to $1.5 billion in financing for Indian expatriates investing in India’s foreign-currency deposit program.

The twin moves place FAB at the intersection of a global AI infrastructure debt binge and a Gulf-India remittance corridor that could pull in $50 billion in deposits. Lending to a SoftBank call that size is one way to announce that a regional bank intends to play on the same pitch as the global bookrunners. Corporate treasurers should watch the deposit program launch as a new funding stream that could reshape Gulf-India flows and bank liquidity.

Abu Dhabi Courts Go AI
The Abu Dhabi Judicial Department will roll out an AI judicial platform across its courts starting September, one of the first such deployments in a Gulf legal system. The first phase targets case routing and document review, not replacing judges, but the move signals that a jurisdiction known for the ADGM digital court wants algorithms reading filings before magistrates do.
​Gulf angle
Abu Dhabi stamps itself as the Gulf’s sandbox for court AI, and legal-tech firms across the GCC will now race to replicate it.
