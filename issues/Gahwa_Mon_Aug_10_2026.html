Saudi MOD Performs First Remote Robotic Surgery
A surgeon in Riyadh operated on a patient in Dhahran without leaving the capital, using a secure communications network built by stc group.

The Ministry of Defense Health Services conducted its first remote robotic surgery between the two cities. stc group designed and ran the integrated digital communications system that connected the operating rooms, carrying data transfer, medical imaging, and real-time control of the robotic surgical tools across the distance. The operation was a demonstration of the high-reliability communications technology needed to make remote specialist surgery routine. The ministry framed the achievement as a contribution to the healthcare development targets of Saudi Vision 2030. The successful demonstration puts a technical foundation under the Kingdom's ambition to extend specialist surgical care to remote areas without flying a surgeon there.

CULTURE
UAE Tourism to Hit $84.9B by 2036
The UAE's travel and tourism sector will add more than $16.4 billion to the economy over the next decade, lifting its total economic contribution to $84.9 billion by 2036 from $68.5 billion in 2025, according to the World Travel and Tourism Council.

The sector already accounts for 11.9% of UAE GDP and supported 947,100 jobs last year, or 13.6% of total employment. By 2036, jobs tied to tourism are expected to reach nearly 1.17 million. International visitors spent $56.9 billion in 2025, and that figure is projected to climb to $72.8 billion. International arrivals are forecast to hit nearly 49 million. India was the largest source market with 14% of arrivals, followed by the UK at 9% and Russia at 8%. The compound annual growth rate for the sector between 2026 and 2036 is forecast at 4.8%. Capital investment in tourism reached $9.4 billion in 2025 and is projected to rise to $13.8 billion by 2036. The country is already constructing hotel rooms faster than it can fill them, which means a 49 million arrival target is not a forecast so much as a shopping list. The WTTC's ten-year projection is a line-by-line breakdown of where the UAE tourism machine is aiming, and operators sizing their investment pipelines against these figures will find the arrival, spending, and employment targets for 2036 credible benchmarks for capacity planning.

Moove Hits $2.1B Value on $250M Round
Mubadala Investment Company led the Series C round, with Toyota's growth fund Woven Capital and Ion Pacific co-leading.

The Nigerian-founded mobility fintech raised $250 million at a $2.1 billion valuation. Moove operates roughly 42,000 vehicles across 29 cities in 13 countries, employs 3,300 people, and generates $420 million in annual recurring revenue. The company manages autonomous vehicle fleets through a partnership with Waymo in Phoenix and Miami, with London to follow, and it plans to grow its autonomous mobility workforce from about 150 to 500 employees by the end of the year. Moove expanded through acquisitions including Kovi in Brazil and Tokyo Taxi in Japan. Existing investors BlackRock, MUFG, Franklin Templeton, and Uber also sit on the cap table. Handing the car keys to a robot fleet and raising $250 million for it lands better than most drivers do in a single year. Moove's headcount plan to more than double its autonomous fleet workforce this year signals that the Waymo partnership is scaling faster than the funding announcement alone suggests.

GCC & SAUDI
Bids In for Third Hassyan Pipeline Package
Dewa split the Hassyan desalination plant network expansion into three packages, and contractors have now submitted bids for the third one.

The project connects the massive Hassyan SWRO desalination plant to Dubai's water network. The pipeline expansion is the connective tissue that turns a coastal desalination plant into a city-wide water supply. The third package tender represents the final stretch of that network buildout, and the submitted bids will set the cost benchmark for the remaining pipe-laying scope. The bid submission triggers the evaluation window; the contract award will complete the pipeline network buildout and determine the final cost for the third and last package.

MACRO & GEO
Syria, Russia End Military Use of Key Bases
Damascus and Moscow reached a memorandum of understanding to end Russia's military use of the Hmeimim air base and part of the Tartous port, converting the remaining military facilities into joint training and qualification centers.

The deal follows roughly 18 months of negotiations. Syria will assume management of the civilian facilities, including Hmeimim Airport and the fourth commercial berth at Tartous port. The two bases were Russia's principal military footholds in the Mediterranean and were used to support Bashar al-Assad during the Syrian civil war. Their effective demotion from military outposts to joint training centers sidelines Moscow from the eastern Mediterranean at a moment when the US-Iran confrontation has made every naval asset and basing right in the region count twice. The conversion of the bases into training centers, rather than a full Russian eviction, preserves a residual Moscow presence and leaves open the question of whether Russia can rebuild the footprint under a different legal label.

Dubai Hands Building Permits to an Algorithm
Dubai Municipality launched a project to fully automate building permit approvals, removing every human from the review chain. The AI starts with private and investment villas, checking each application against the Dubai Building Code, and rolls out in three phases across 26 weeks. The move follows a 2023 pilot that audited a 37-floor tower and the UAE’s April 2026 pledge to shift half of government services to agentic AI within two years.
What it means here
Gulf governments are moving past chatbot experiments and handing signature authority to a black box, with permitting as the first live case that directly touches a developer’s bottom line.
