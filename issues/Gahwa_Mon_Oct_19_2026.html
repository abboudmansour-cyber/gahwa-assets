The Lead

Reportedly, missiles over Riyadh
Saudi Arabia cut interceptors fired per incoming missile from five-to-seven down to "a couple" to conserve stocks, according to a UK source saying "we have very little left in the locker to give." The coalition intercepted two ballistic missiles aimed at Khamis Mushait and drones over Riyadh and the Eastern region on Sunday. Reportedly, the Houthis claimed Saudi warplanes flew 110 airstrikes and missile attacks in 24 hours. The Houthis said the jets were F-15 and Typhoon aircraft from Khamis Mushait and Taif bases.

King Khalid International Airport has reopened after a period of suspension, but the flight disruption continues. Cathay Pacific extended its Riyadh suspension to January 31, and Delta pushed its Atlanta-Riyadh launch to March. Lufthansa suspended until November 18, Philippine Airlines until November 29, and KLM and SalamAir until October 31. Air France, Air India, Akasa and IndiGo remain grounded until around October 24.

Egypt's National Defense Council pledged full support to Saudi Arabia, while Turkey and Pakistan committed forces for deployment inside the Kingdom. Ankara ruled out offensive operations. The UAE mourned two Saudi officers killed in a reconnaissance helicopter crash in Najran region.

The IMF expects the Saudi economy to shrink 3.3% in 2026 before rebounding 7.9% in 2027, according to the IMF. The World Bank's chief economist for MENAAP Roberta Gatti urged Riyadh to reassess diversification investments and redirect resources to pressing needs. Reportedly, the Strait of Hormuz closure took out a route carrying around 20 million barrels per day before the war. Saudi businesses are now operating with thinner air defense cover, longer flight disruptions, and the prospect of a full-year economic contraction. The Kingdom's air defense stocks are depleting faster than Europe can resupply them, and the flight cancellations now stretch into 2026.
Source: Gulf News

02Still worth more, on paper
Dubai economy shrinks first time since pandemic
Dubai's real GDP fell 2.3% year-on-year in the first half of 2026 to AED 473 billion, the first contraction since the pandemic. GDP at current prices rose 0.2% to AED 505 billion. The Iran war began February 28, disrupting regional travel and trade, and the UAE economy expanded just 0.4% in the first half.

Financial and insurance activities grew 8.8% in real terms to AED 65.9 billion, with the sector's contribution to GDP rising to 14% from 12.5%. DIFC companies exceeded 10,000 in the first half. Healthcare and social work grew fastest at 14.3%, while construction grew 2.4% to AED 33.2 billion. Wholesale and retail trade declined 6% but still represents 20.5% of GDP, and transport and storage fell 7.4%. Real estate contracted 2.5%, and manufacturing was down 0.9%.

Dubai is pushing forward with AED 2.1 trillion in projects. Dubai CommerCity announced an AED 1.8 billion expansion, and a AED 1.5 billion economic relief package was announced in May. The emirate's free zones trade rose 17% to AED 246 billion, a sixth consecutive record first half. Re-exports were up 22% to about AED 109 billion. Financial and insurance activities grew 8.8% while construction managed 2.4%, so the skyline now depends on the desks.
Source: The National Business

03BNPL for ads
UAE adtech JustAd raises $5m
UAE-based adtech startup JustAd secured a $5 million investment from angel investor Imad Jomaa, with stage and valuation not disclosed. Founded in 2025 by Ahmed Elsayed, the platform runs an AI-powered marketing tool with 'Advertise Now, Pay Later' embedded financing. The financing lets media spend be paid in installments up to 12 months.

The platform operates across Meta, TikTok, Snapchat and Google Ads. JustAd plans to onboard customers in its first new markets in early 2027 with local sales and customer success teams.

SMEs now account for 94% of businesses in the UAE. A pay-later model for advertising spend targets exactly the segment that finds upfront media budgets the hardest to stomach. If the platform works, the real product is not the ad tool but the financing layer. That layer lets small brands compete with big ones on the same channels without the same cash flow.
Source: Wamda

04Locked and loaded
Saudi cyber sector hits SAR 20bn
Saudi Arabia's cybersecurity sector contributed SAR 20 billion to GDP in 2025, up from nearly SAR 18.5 billion in 2024, according to the National Cybersecurity Authority. The market was valued at SAR 16.3 billion in 2025, growing 7.2% year-on-year, with the private sector contributing SAR 11.2 billion and government SAR 5.1 billion. A 7.2% annual growth rate in cybersecurity spending is the clearest sign yet that the region's digital buildout is starting to price in the cost of defending it.

05Steel flyovers
Riyadh bridges package goes to JV
A joint venture secured a $246 million package to construct steel flyovers across key Riyadh junctions for the Royal Commission for Riyadh City. The project is part of the capital's broader transport infrastructure upgrades. The bridges package is a smaller piece of the Kingdom's transport spend, but it confirms that infrastructure contracts are still flowing even as the war pulls fiscal attention elsewhere.
Source: MEED Saudi Arabia

The AI Race
​
Arabic AI film tools get their close-up
Popcorn AI Studio, a UAE-based startup founded this year by Egyptian entrepreneurs Shady Essam and Mohamed Hedar, has raised a $1 million pre-seed round led by Emirati entrepreneur Mohammed Bin Bouta Alharsoosi. The platform offers AI scriptwriting, character development, scene generation, voiceovers, lip-syncing and post-production. It is built specifically for Arabic content and regional dialects.

The company is also running an AI filmmaking competition at El Gouna Film Festival with a $70,000 prize pool. The bet is that Arabic-language tools, built for regional dialects rather than translated from English, can unlock a content market that global AI video tools have not served properly. The funding will support product growth across the UAE, Saudi Arabia and Egypt. The same logic that produced a Saudi super app for tickets now applies to filmmaking tools, with the Gulf funding the best Arabic-language AI before global platforms catch up.
