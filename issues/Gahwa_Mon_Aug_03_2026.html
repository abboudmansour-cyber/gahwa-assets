DataVolt's $5 Billion Saudi AI Bet
The company, wholly owned by Vision Invest and chaired by former Acwa Power CEO Paddy Padmanathan, expects to secure non-recourse financing for all its Saudi facilities under construction within six months. Its flagship is a 1.5GW AI-focused data center at Neom's Oxagon, part of a $5 billion investment in the Kingdom, with the first phase expected operational by 2028.

DataVolt just proved the model works on a smaller scale, reaching financial close on up to $150 million of 12-year non-recourse financing for a data center in Tashkent, Uzbekistan. The Tashkent project's first six-megawatt phase is almost fully leased, with less than 10% of capacity remaining, an encouraging signal for a company that now needs to finance something roughly a hundred times larger in Saudi Arabia. Parent Vision Invest and its affiliates operate more than $95 billion of infrastructure assets, so the track record is not in doubt, but the scale of the Neom project puts it in a different league entirely.

The context is a global hyperscaler spending spree: Alphabet, Amazon, Meta, Microsoft and Oracle are expected to spend about $750 billion on capital expenditure in 2026, and they issued roughly $115 billion in debt in the first quarter alone. DataVolt is positioning itself as the landlord for that capital, and the fact that it can point to… DataVolt's financing timeline and Tashkent lease-up data give investors concrete milestones for one of the region's largest AI infrastructure plays.

GCC & SAUDI
Marafiq to Supply Water for Oman Green Iron
Marafiq will build and own a desalination plant in Duqm that supplies industrial water to Meranti Green Steel's 2.5-million-tonne-a-year green iron complex, locking in a utility model where the water infrastructure is someone else's capital expenditure and the steelmaker just pays for the flow. The deal is one of the clearest examples yet of Gulf industrial players separating the heavy infrastructure from the core production process, a structure that keeps Meranti's balance sheet lighter and lets Marafiq amortize the desalination plant across a dedicated, creditworthy offtaker.

Green iron needs a lot of water, hydrogen production is thirsty, and Duqm is a desert port, so the desalination plant is not a nice-to-have but the difference between a viable project and a stranded asset. Marafiq, which already operates major utility assets across the Gulf, is effectively betting that the green steel demand story is solid enough to justify sinking capital into a dedicated plant at the far end of the Arabian Peninsula. The water supply agreement removes a key infrastructure bottleneck for a project central to Oman's green industrial ambitions.

UAE Experts Warn on Power Bank Safety
The general airline limit for power banks is up to 100 watt-hours in cabin baggage, but experts are more concerned about counterfeit units containing rejected, recycled, or mismatched battery cells with poor insulation and fake capacity ratings, the kind of product that turns a fully charged seat pocket into a thermal runaway experiment at 35,000 feet. Warning signs include swelling, distorted casing, unusual heat, cracks, dents, leaking fluid, strange smells, discolouration, unusual noises, and inconsistent charging, a list long enough to make any traveler eye their ten-dollar airport purchase with suspicion.

UAE travellers are advised to look for Emirates Conformity Assessment Scheme compliance and safety certifications including UN 38.3, UL 2056, or IEC 62133-2, standards that a counterfeit manufacturer has precisely zero incentive to meet. The advice is timely given the summer travel peak, and it doubles as a reminder that the lithium-ion supply chain has a dark underside that the Gulf's e-commerce boom has made harder to police. If your power bank was suspiciously cheap and is now suspiciously warm, the experts suggest you should probably stop using it before it makes its feelings known. The summer travel season and rising counterfeit electronics in Gulf markets make this safety guidance immediately relevant for travellers.

The AI Race
The global race, landed on the Gulf.
Kimi K3 puts frontier AI on a download
Moonshot AI released the weights for Kimi K3, a 2.8 trillion-parameter open-weights model that ranks third globally on the Artificial Analysis index, trailing only Claude Fable 5, and beats GPT-5.6 on several benchmarks. The release, according to developer reports, makes the largest open-weights model ever published available to any developer who can handle a 594-gigabyte download, and the open-source community has already quantized it to 1-bit and 2-bit formats while preserving roughly 79% top-1 accuracy.
​Gulf angle
Gulf AI strategists can now run a frontier model without a licensing deal, but the lesson is uncomfortable: the state of the art is a download, not a data center, and that flips the economics of every Neom-scale build.

A new moon, a dark desert, and 210,000 km/h comet dust
On August 12, the Perseids meteor shower peaks under a new moon, giving the UAE its darkest skies of the year for one of the most reliable celestial shows. The UAE's astronomy community is quietly growing, and the event draws crowds to desert camps from Al Quaa to Jebel Jais, but the real sell is simpler: a rare natural spectacle that requires no ticket, no booking, and no app, just a clear view and the patience to watch ancient comet dust burn up at 210,000 kilometers per hour. With temperatures easing toward the end of summer, lying on a blanket in the desert after midnight stops being an endurance test and starts being a genuine pleasure.
Why it matters
A rare alignment of a new moon with the Perseids peak turns a good viewing night into an exceptional one, and the calendar reminder is just over a week away.
