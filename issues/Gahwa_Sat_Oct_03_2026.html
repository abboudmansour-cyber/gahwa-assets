UAE Warns UK Over Manchester City Sanctions Fallout, Report Says
The UAE has warned the UK government that sanctions on Manchester City could affect future major British investments, according to a Bloomberg report. The warning comes after an independent commission found the Abu Dhabi-owned club guilty of all charges on Premier League financial rules. The four-year investigation concluded the club artificially inflated financial results by more than £900 million and used sham contracts.

The club could owe over £20 million and potentially face a criminal investigation. The findings, published September 29, cover nine seasons starting in 2009. Manchester City earned nearly $1.5 billion in prize money during the period under investigation.

Etihad Airways, the club's main sponsor, is reportedly considering a lawsuit against the Premier League. The airline denies involvement in improper commercial arrangements. This dispute now sits inside a much larger question about how far sports governance can reach into sovereign wealth before it hits political reality. The line between a football club and a sovereign asset has now been drawn in the open, and both sides are treating it as a diplomatic matter.

02Holy City Strike
Drone Strike Hits Medina Power Transformer, Officials Say
A drone strike disabled a power transformer at the Taibah distribution station serving the Prophet's Mosque in Medina, according to officials. The attack knocked one transformer offline, officials say. This is reportedly the second attack on a Saudi holy city in two weeks. According to officials, Türkiye's Head of Communications, Burhanettin Duran, condemned the strike on civilian infrastructure serving the Prophet's Mosque and said Ankara would continue to stand by Saudi Arabia. Duran called attacks on the Two Holy Mosques an unacceptable affront to the Islamic world. The strike, according to officials, fits a pattern of Houthi attacks targeting Saudi infrastructure far from the Yemeni border. The group has previously hit oil facilities, airports, and now power stations serving religious sites. The targeting of a civilian facility serving one of Islam's holiest sites is a direct challenge to Saudi Arabia's role as custodian of the Two Holy Mosques. It puts pressure on the kingdom to respond while also drawing international condemnation toward the Houthis. The wider pattern suggests the conflict is escalating rather than cooling.

03The Strait Shortcut
UAE Banks Set to Lead Gulf Earnings Growth
All but one of the major banks in Saudi Arabia, Qatar, Kuwait and the UAE increased first-half net profit despite unprecedented regional disruptions. The performance positions UAE banks to lead Gulf earnings growth in the coming quarters. Andrew Cunningham, an analyst writing in AGBI, says lending capacity, not interest rates, is the real constraint as liquidity tightens across GCC banking systems.

UAE and Oman are the only countries among five major Gulf crude producers to have increased oil export revenues since the start of the US-Iran war. Both benefit from pipeline routes that bypass the Strait of Hormuz. That export revenue flows directly into bank deposits and government spending.

Saudi Arabia's Ministry of Finance expects real GDP to fall 3.6 percent this year, driven by declining oil activity. The budget deficit is expected to widen again in 2027. Against that backdrop, UAE banks with stronger liquidity and diversified economies have an edge over regional rivals still tied to oil price swings. The Gulf banking story is splitting into two tracks: lenders in the UAE and Oman with cash to deploy, and everyone else watching liquidity drain away.

04Out of the ashes
Dubai's Lamcy Plaza to Reopen as The Plaza in 2027
Dubai's former Lamcy Plaza will reopen as The Plaza in the first or second quarter of 2027. The property sold at auction for Dh188.7 million in September 2025 and now sits under the asset management of Azure Properties. The original mall closed after a fire in March 2017.

The mall first opened in 1997 with more than 150 stores across five levels, long before Dubai became the retail capital it is today. For a generation of Dubai residents, Lamcy Plaza was where school trips, first jobs and Friday afternoons happened. Its closure left a hole in the older Bur Dubai retail landscape.

The rebrand to The Plaza signals a bet that nostalgia plus repositioning can revive a site that newer malls eclipsed. The mall's location, near densely populated residential districts, gives it a built-in customer base that newer destinations on the city's edges still chase. For longtime residents, the name Lamcy will mean something The Plaza cannot quite replace. Old Dubai retail is getting a second act, and the shoppers who remember Lamcy Plaza will decide if the new name earns the loyalty the old one never lost.

05e& 6G First
UAE's e& Launches World's First Mobile Network on Upper 6GHz
A world-first 6G step: UAE telecom group e& launched what it describes as the world's first mobile network on Upper 6GHz, a step toward 6G technology. The launch positions the UAE as an early mover in the next generation of mobile connectivity.

The Upper 6GHz band offers more capacity and lower latency than existing 5G networks. For a country that has marketed itself as a testbed for future technologies, being first to the new spectrum is a branding asset as much as a technical one.

The launch also signals where the global telecom industry is heading. Countries that secure early access to new spectrum bands will have an advantage in attracting AI firms, autonomous vehicle testing and other bandwidth-hungry industries. The UAE is betting that connectivity infrastructure will be a competitive advantage in the AI economy. Being first to 6G spectrum is like being first to build a port in a new trade route: the ships come to whoever has the dock ready.

The AI Race
​
Sean Parker Rebuilds Stability AI Around Music
Sean Parker is rebuilding Stability AI around music, with backing and blessing from major music labels. The former Facebook president and Napster founder is betting that generative AI's most commercially viable path lies in licensed, label-approved music tools rather than open-ended image generation. Stability AI was once best known for Stable Diffusion, the image model that helped spark the generative AI boom.

The company has struggled to monetize open-source models. A pivot to music, done with label partnerships rather than against them, is a different thesis: pay for the catalog, build the tools, and share revenue. The move puts pressure on other AI firms to choose between scraped data and licensed content. It also gives record labels a seat at the table they have spent years fighting for. Gulf sovereign funds and media companies invested heavily in AI entertainment, and this shift toward licensed music means their content libraries just became more valuable.

Arabian Drilling wins $533m Neutral Zone rig contract
Arabian Drilling won a four-year, $533 million contract from Al-Khafji Joint Operations for four high-specification offshore jack-up rigs. The deal covers drilling in the Neutral Zone shared between Saudi Arabia and Kuwait. The zone is one of the few places where both countries' oil interests are literally indistinguishable. The contract gives Arabian Drilling a long, predictable revenue stream at a time when rig demand is tightening across the Gulf.
Oman appoints consultants for 2.8GW gas-fired IPPs
Nama Power and Water Procurement is planning three new combined-cycle gas turbine plants in Oman with 2.8 gigawatts of total capacity. Consultants have been appointed to move the independent power projects forward. The projects add significant generation capacity to a grid that has been stretched by industrial growth and summer demand. For contractors, this is the first visible step in a procurement pipeline that will run for years.
Lowest bidder emerges for Oman's Al-Ghubar oil field project
Petroleum Development Oman plans a gas-oil gravity drainage facility at Al-Ghubar field to boost output. The lowest bidder has now emerged for the project in the Block 6 concession area. The facility is aimed at extracting more oil from a field that has been producing for years. For bidders, the next step is contract award and mobilization.
Puma taps Maersk for North America distribution
Puma handed Maersk management of its North American distribution network. Capacity across three Puma US distribution centers will open to other Maersk customers under the agreement. It is a signal that logistics companies are now competing on third-party capacity sharing, not just freight rates. For Gulf exporters, it is a reminder that North American distribution can be bought as a service, not built as a project.
