The Lead
​
Riyadh airport hit
A second Houthi missile strike on Riyadh's King Khalid International Airport within 24 hours reportedly killed at least one more person Sunday after Saturday's barrage left 13 dead and over 300 wounded. The Saudi-led coalition intercepted two ballistic missiles fired toward Riyadh and Khamis Mushait, but according to officials the repeated hit on a civilian airport marks the first time the capital itself has come under direct attack in this war. Qatar Airways suspended flights to and from Dammam and Riyadh, the FAA told American carriers to exercise caution flying over the kingdom, and Delta's planned Atlanta-Riyadh route set for 23 October now has no confirmation it will launch.

The strikes reportedly hit just as Saudi Arabia opened its first publicly reported talks with Iran since the Houthi escalation began. Foreign Minister Faisal bin Farhan Al Saud spoke with his Iranian counterpart about easing regional tensions, and the Saudi Grand Mufti condemned what officials say were attacks on civilian facilities. The kingdom is investigating how its air defenses missed the Saturday barrage. According to an initial probe, a staged assault saw drones clear the way for cruise and ballistic missiles to hit the airport. The United Nations has pledged technical support for Palestinian elections, but the region's immediate focus has narrowed to a single question.

Siemens Energy said its order book runs through 2032 and it is trying to reserve scarce turbine capacity for Saudi Arabia, even as it invests around $150 million to expand its Dammam hub. The business world is not waiting for the outcome of the investigation. It is already pricing the risk that the capital is no longer safe. The war has breached the capital, and the Gulf's commercial center is counting the cost in airlines, airports, and the confidence of the investors who keep the kingdom's Vision 2030 projects moving.

02The 5% counter
UAE banks pay premium for deposits
A 18.1% year-on-year jump in UAE credit against 14% deposit growth has pushed the system's loan-to-deposit ratio above 80%, up from 77.7% in 2025. First Abu Dhabi Bank is now offering 5.6% on new savings deposits through December, up from 4%, while Emirates NBD is advertising up to 5.5% on larger new deposits. The war has drained the monetary base by roughly 13.5% since it began, per Jefferies.

The scramble for funding comes as banks face heavier demand for credit just as the federal government opens its wallet. The UAE cabinet approved AED 292.5 billion in federal spending for 2027-2029, up 28.3% from the previous cycle. Fitch expects the non-oil economy to contract 3.7% this year before rebounding in 2027.

Gulf money has already fled to Indian NRI dollar deposits, which pulled in roughly $130 billion, most of it from the Gulf. The banks left behind are competing to hold what remains. A 5.6% headline rate is the price of staying funded. The banks paying up for deposits now are protecting their loan books for the day the strait reopens and credit demand returns in force.

03IMF cuts Gulf outlook
IMF cuts Mideast growth outlook
The IMF has sharply cut its growth forecast for the Middle East and Central Asia, now projecting a 0.3% regional contraction in 2026 compared with 1.9% growth expected six months ago. The fund reportedly cited "a longer closure of the Strait of Hormuz" compared with its April baseline assumptions. It predicts a major rebound in 2027.

Saudi Arabia's economy is now forecast to shrink 3.3% this year, despite the kingdom's push to keep oil and non-oil output moving. Fitch separately sees UAE non-oil GDP contracting 3.7% in 2026 before growing 6.1% in 2027. The twin downgrades frame the war's cost in hard numbers.

The fund says global growth will still hit 3.1% this year, powered by AI investment that is offsetting the Middle East energy shock. Oil prices held above $100 a barrel, and the region's pain is being absorbed by a world economy that has learned to route around it. The fund's message is that the Gulf's war bill is being paid through a smaller regional economy, while the rest of the world grows through AI.

The AI Race
​
Optro opens Dubai AI governance hub
Optro, an AI governance firm, has chosen Dubai as its regional centre, announcing the move at the 2026 CAE Conference in Abu Dhabi. The firm already has a Singapore presence and recently acquired AI testing solution Midship. Governance, the unglamorous side of AI, is now being sold as a service.

The timing is deliberate. The UAE's Government 4.0 directive mandates moving half of government services to autonomous agentic AI within two years. A firm that can prove an AI system is safe, fair, and auditable is selling the licence to operate. Optro is betting that Gulf governments and firms will pay for that assurance. The UAE is moving its own government onto AI rails, and the firms that can inspect those rails are opening shop next door to the regulator.

Emirates Islamic tests sukuk appetite
Emirates Islamic is testing investor appetite for a possible five-year USD sukuk under its USD 4 billion certificate program. The bank raised USD 500 million via sustainability-linked sukuk in September 2025 at 4.54%, drawing USD 1.2 billion in orders. Gulf bond and sukuk issuance fell 17.5% in Q3 amid higher borrowing costs.
Viking Completion Technology opens Dhahran plant
Dubai-based Viking Completion Technology has opened a Saudi entity and manufacturing plant in Dhahran through a JV with Abdulrahman Algosaibi Holdings. The plant builds well-completion equipment such as safety valves and packers. It is one of only two Saudi plants holding a full set of API licenses.
Kuwaiti banks pull half their CBK deposits
Kuwaiti banks withdrew half their deposits from the central bank, falling to KD 2.4 billion ($8 billion) at end-September from KD 4.8 billion, following Iran war emergency lending measures. The outflow signals that lenders no longer need the emergency liquidity buffer and are redeploying funds. The systemwide shift points to a banking sector regaining confidence.
Ronaldo scores 981st career goal
Cristiano Ronaldo scored his 981st career goal as Al Nassr beat Neftchi Fergana 3-1 in the AFC Champions League Elite in Riyadh. The match had been delayed an hour by a security alert in the capital. After the final whistle, Ronaldo draped a scarf bearing the Saudi flag and said he 'belongs to the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia.'
UAE teenagers turn chicken feathers into biofertiliser
Three UAE teenagers developed BioFeathers, turning chicken feathers into biofertiliser now being tested at 16 farms and nurseries across the UAE, Oman, and India. The project is part of a broader UAE push on food security, including Abu Dhabi's Qitaf City agricultural community. The drive comes as retailers adapt to Iran war supply chain disruption.

The Last Sip
​
What is the one thing Riyadh's offices are running out of faster than parking?
The answer
Desks. The city's office occupancy is 96%, and Grade A space is at 98%. Over 780 multinational firms operate regional headquarters there. New entrants are landing in flexible workspaces in KAFD and Olaya, where occupancy runs near 90%. The capital is full of companies that came for the Vision and stayed for the address.
