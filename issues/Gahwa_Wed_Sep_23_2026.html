The Lead
​
Iran floats reopening
Weeks after FinCEN moved to cut Banque Misr UAE off, the Central Bank of the UAE gave National Bank of Egypt preliminary approval to absorb Banque Misr's five UAE branches, a deal that would combine two state-owned Egyptian lenders under one roof in the Emirates. The approval landed weeks after the US Treasury's FinCEN proposed cutting Banque Misr UAE off from correspondent banking access on August 28.

FinCEN alleges the branches moved roughly $1.8 billion between January 2024 and June 2026 for 103 companies linked to Iranian shadow-banking networks. The takeover would consolidate Egypt's UAE banking footprint at a moment when Washington is reportedly applying maximum pressure on Iranian financial connections, and the UAE is cooperating with that pressure after halting all trade with Iran following the missile attacks on August 18. An Egyptian banking analyst said the deal lets the two lenders pool their UAE balance sheet and customer base, while a legal advisor pointed to the delicate timing. Whether the US objects to the merged Egyptian bank will be the clearest signal of how far Washington is prepared to push its financial pressure campaign against Iranian-linked flows in the Gulf.

02AI data infrastructure
Snorkel AI hits $3.5bn valuation
Snorkel AI raised $350 million in Series E funding, tripling its valuation to $3.5 billion. The seven-year-old startup sells a data-as-a-service platform that labels and prepares the training data that large language models learn from.

The round shows that the AI boom's money is moving from the models themselves to the data pipelines that feed them. Snorkel's approach lets companies build their own training sets without hiring thousands of human labelers, a cost that has become one of the biggest bottlenecks in scaling enterprise AI.

The deal has a direct Gulf read: as Saudi Arabia, the UAE and Qatar pour billions into sovereign AI infrastructure, the shortage of high-quality Arabic-language training data is among the largest constraints they face. A startup that can cut the cost of building that data by an order of magnitude becomes a strategic supplier, not just a software vendor. Who supplies the training data for Gulf AI models will determine how quickly those models become useful in Arabic and for regional business tasks, and Snorkel's rise prices that constraint in public.

03The long way round
Saudi terminals set post-war loading record
Six supertankers took on nearly 12 million barrels as Saudi Arabia's main Gulf export terminals recorded their busiest crude-loading day since before the war on September 21, when six supertankers took on nearly 12 million barrels at Ras Tanura and Juaymah. MarineTraffic data showed the loading surge came even as the Strait of Hormuz, the single exit point for those barrels, ran at a fraction of its normal capacity.

The numbers reveal the oil is still moving, just through longer and more expensive workarounds. Ship-to-ship transfers in the Gulf of Oman are handling roughly 2.5 million barrels a day this month, a route that keeps Saudi crude reaching Asian and European buyers but adds days of transit and millions in insurance and freight costs.

Saudi terminals running at full speed while Hormuz stays choked means the Kingdom's export machine is testing the limits of the workaround system. If those limits hold, it strengthens the case for the East-West pipeline and other overland routes as permanent hedges. Supertankers now take the long way, and buyers pay for the detour.

04US energy rebuild fund
US seeks $10bn Gulf energy rebuild fund
The US is trying to mobilize up to $10 billion to rebuild energy infrastructure damaged during the Iran war. The Trump administration has floated a $5 billion US contribution and is seeking matching funds from Saudi Arabia, Qatar, Bahrain, Kuwait, Oman, Iraq and Jordan.

The proposal turns Gulf states into both donors and beneficiaries: their own infrastructure was hit during the conflict, but the rebuild also creates a pipeline of contracts for regional engineering and construction firms. The US ask names specific countries, not a general appeal, which means serious negotiations are already underway.

The fund's size, if realized, would make it one of the largest reconstruction vehicles in the region's history. The larger question is whether it is a war-recovery fund or the start of a longer-term US-Gulf energy security partnership, and the matching structure gives Gulf states leverage over how that question is answered. Gulf states are now both donors and beneficiaries, writing checks that come back as contracts.

05Meta Muse blocked
Amazon blocks Meta's Muse
Amazon blocked Meta's Muse AI agent from shopping on its retail site, citing unauthorized access and violations of its conditions of use. Meta denies the charges. The move came 12 days after Muse launched and climbed to the top of the US App Store.

Meanwhile, Shopify partnered with Meta to let Muse power checkouts across its store network, a direct counterweight to Amazon's walled garden. Shopify CEO Tobi Lütke announced the partnership as a bet on open agentic commerce.

The split between Amazon's closed approach and Shopify's open one defines the next battle in e-commerce: who gets to control the AI agent that shops for customers. For Gulf retailers, the fight is a preview of the choices they will soon face when deciding which AI shopping assistants to support. The Amazon-Shopify split is the first clear fault line in agentic commerce, and Gulf retailers now have to choose whether to open their catalogs to AI agents or wall them off.

The AI Race
​
AI's building blocks get cheaper
A different company, Anthropic, just made its top AI cheaper to use. Its Claude Opus 5.5 model costs about 40 percent less to run than the version before it, while keeping the same level of intelligence. This follows OpenAI's release of two new models, GPT-6 Sol and GPT-6 Luna, which came with a 50 percent price cut for top business customers.

The price cuts land in the same week as reports that Meta and xAI are also lowering their prices. When the biggest AI labs compete on cost, it usually means the technology is becoming a commodity. Falling prices push more companies to actually use AI instead of just piloting it. The cost of running an Arabic-language customer service bot, for example, just got closer to the point where a mid-sized Gulf company can afford it. Cheaper AI models lower the barrier for every Gulf bank, retailer and government platform building Arabic-language tools.

Sharjah Islamic Bank prints $500m sukuk
Sharjah Islamic Bank sold a $500 million five-year senior sukuk at 105 basis points over US Treasuries. That spread came in 30 basis points tighter than the bank's initial guidance. Orders reached 2.5 times the amount on offer. The deal is among the tightest prints from a GCC bank this year.
XRG eyes Shell's LNG Canada stake
Adnoc's international investment arm XRG is looking at a possible stake in Shell's LNG Canada export project. The first phase cost roughly CAD 40 billion and has capacity for 14 million tons of LNG a year. XRG wants to reach 20 to 25 million tons per year of LNG capacity by 2035. Approval for Phase 2 could come next month, which would make the stake decision urgent.
Aldar and Mubadala JV buys Masdar City Square
A joint venture of Aldar and Mubadala acquired Masdar City Square for $250 million. The property covers more than 47,000 square meters of office space across seven buildings. It is 99 percent occupied, with Tenants including TAQA and the Mohamed bin Zayed University of Artificial Intelligence. The JV's Masdar City portfolio now totals $1.28 billion.
Oman blesses pharma and biomedical industrial clusters
Sultan Haitham bin Tarik approved a proposal to establish specialised industrial clusters for pharmaceuticals and medical devices. The move supports Oman's plan to diversify its economy beyond oil. It positions the country as a possible manufacturing base for regional health supply chains. The project is not yet funded at scale, but the royal blessing is the first formal step.
UAE prices second retail T-sukuk at 5.06% coupon
The UAE priced its second sovereign retail T-sukuk at 5.06%, up from 4.30% on the debut. The issue targets AED 50 million, with subscriptions open until September 28 and trading on Nasdaq Dubai from October 1. The debut drew AED 445 million in orders.

Japan's centenarian count hits a record 107,677
Japan now counts 107,677 people aged 100 or older, a new record. The number has risen every year for more than five decades. The government marks each milestone with a silver sake cup. Longevity on this scale is a public health success that comes with a pension bill, and it names the demographic future every Gulf state is watching as life expectancy rises.
Qatar Charity provides urgent relief to Yemeni refugees in Djibouti
Qatar Charity is delivering urgent relief to Yemeni refugees who have crossed into Djibouti. The aid includes food, shelter and basic supplies. The Horn of Africa now carries a second wave of displacement from the wider region, and Gulf-funded relief is doing the quiet work no headline tracks.
A 13-year-old Chinese swimmer wins two golds at the Asian Games
A 13-year-old from China won two gold medals at the Asian Games, breaking into a field usually dominated by swimmers a decade older. Teen prodigies keep arriving earlier, which matters for a Gulf region investing tens of billions in elite sports development and talent scouting.

The Last Sip
​
Which country sends a silver sake cup to every new centenarian?
The answer
Japan. Citizens who reach 100 receive a silver sake cup from the government. The tradition began in 1963, when just 153 people qualified. Now there are 107,677 centenarians. The cups are no longer made of pure silver. The government swapped to silver-plated cups years ago to control the cost. The most predictable budget line in the world is a gift to people who have already waited a century.
What animal contest is currently running in Alaska with 16 competitors?
The answer
Fat Bear Week in Katmai National Park. The public votes for the fattest brown bear, who has spent the summer gorging on salmon. The winner is named champion one week later. The contest began as a lighthearted way to celebrate the bears' survival before winter. Sixteen bears without a single press secretary, and the voting is still cleaner than most contests we cover.
