The Lead
​
​
Riyadh just flipped Hormuz from a choke point into a bargaining chip.
The UAE is fast-tracking a parallel pipeline to the existing Abu Dhabi Crude Oil Pipeline, expected in service next year, while Kuwait and Bahrain, still with no overland route, have opened talks with Riyadh about plugging into the Saudi network. The map of Gulf oil exports is being redrawn on a wartime clock.

Saudi Arabia's East-West Pipeline reached 7 million barrels a day of capacity in March 2025, up from roughly 2 million before the conflict began. A further expansion of up to 2 million barrels a day is planned for completion by 2030 or 2031. The UAE's ADCOP moves 1.1 million barrels a day with 0.7 million in spare capacity, and Iraq is working with an international consortium overseen by the US State Department to restore a pipeline to Syria's Mediterranean coast with an initial 2-million-barrel capacity.

The combined reroute capacity is, according to regional energy analysts, a structural hedge that makes Hormuz's physical closure far less economically catastrophic than it would have been a decade ago. For Gulf operators, the pace of pipeline capex is the signal: the spending is accelerating, not decelerating, which suggests the region's producers are not betting on a return to the old normal.
Map out alternative export routes now: the Saudi and UAE pipeline expansions are building a post-Hormuz oil architecture that will reshape shipping, storage, and insurance costs.

Fertiglobe Hunts Acquisitions Across Four Continents
Exported volumes equaled 56 percent of Fertiglobe's UAE production during the second quarter even as Hormuz logistical constraints tightened, CEO Ahmed El Hoshy told AGBI. The company is now scouting downstream plants inside the UAE to convert its own ammonia into higher-value fertilizers, keeping more margin at home.

The acquisition map spans North Africa, sub-Saharan Africa, Western Australia, and the United States, a geographic spread that reads as a deliberate hedge against a single chokepoint while Q2 net profit hit 145 million dollars on revenue that nearly doubled year-on-year to 1.1 billion. Adjusted Ebitda jumped 111 percent to 371 million.

Fertiglobe is not just surviving a strait closure, officials say; it is using the disruption to bolt on assets in jurisdictions its competitors cannot reach, turning a wartime logistics headache into a deal pipeline that doubles as a supply-chain insurance policy. Track Fertiglobe's downstream UAE plant decisions: if approved, they signal a permanent shift in Gulf fertilizer supply chains toward in-region conversion and value capture.

AI & TECH
DESAISIV Seeks $8 Million for AI Insurance Agent
DESAISIV launched an AI Insurance Agent platform and an 8-million-dollar growth round, with an existing book managing insurance portfolios worth over 2 billion Saudi riyals across 18 insurers and brokers. The company already serves more than 600 enterprise clients and is expanding from corporate health insurance into motor, leasing, and general lines, targeting over 10 billion riyals in premiums processed through its platform.

The Saudi insurance sector has long been a paper-heavy exercise in patience; DESAISIV is essentially trying to turn a 10-billion-riyal stack of forms into an API call, which is either a brilliant market-timing play or a lesson in exactly how stubborn a paper industry can be. Watch the motor and general insurance expansion: if DESAISIV cracks lines beyond health, it becomes a benchmark for insurtech platform plays across the GCC.

FINTECH & CRYPTO
UAE July T-Sukuk Oversubscribed 4.4 Times
The UAE's July Treasury sukuk and bond auctions drew 4.83 billion dirhams in bids for a 1.1-billion-dirham issuance, a 4.4-times oversubscription that priced the October 2027 T-sukuk at a 4.49 percent yield, 24 basis points above comparable US Treasuries. A January 2031 T-bond cleared at 4.48 percent, just four basis points above US benchmarks.

The demand signals, according to market participants, that Gulf sovereign paper is still pricing as a near-risk-free asset even with drone strikes hitting ports across the region. For a country that just scrapped 15 Capital Market Authority fees to streamline its financial sector, an auction book this deep is a green light for more aggressive issuance across the curve. Monitor the UAE's next auction for yield movement: if the spread to US Treasuries tightens further while regional conflict persists, it confirms a structural bid for Gulf sovereign paper.

MACRO & GEO
Hamas Disarmament Deal Announced, Details Scarce
US President Donald Trump announced Thursday that Hamas agreed to a disarmament roadmap ceding control of Gaza in exchange for a gradual Israeli military withdrawal, with mediation by Egypt, Turkey, and Qatar. The announcement came after a cabinet meeting at Camp David, though neither Hamas nor Israeli officials have publicly confirmed the terms as stated.

The proposal follows a pattern of Trump-announced breakthroughs in the region where the operational details arrive later, if at all. A separate report from Doha News quoted Trump thanking Qatar for its role, and the plan reportedly ties disarmament implementation to the pace of Israeli withdrawal.

If the agreement solidifies, the Gaza theater would be the first active front in the broader regional conflict to move toward a negotiated outcome, with implications for ceasefire dynamics across Lebanon, the Red Sea, and the Hormuz standoff. For now, the announcement is a diplomatic event, not a verified operational reality; markets are treating it as a signal rather than a fact. Monitor Israeli and Hamas statements for confirmation: if both sides publicly commit to terms, expect a near-term sentiment boost for regional equities and a potential softening of Red Sea shipping risk premiums.

Houthis Deny Red Sea Fee Plans After Earlier Threat
The Houthi-run maritime coordination body denied plans to impose fees on commercial ships transiting the Bab al-Mandeb strait, days after Reuters reported the group was considering such charges and a week after the Houthis reportedly declared a maritime blockade on Saudi Arabia. Iran Rejects Omani Hormuz Proposals, Pushes Own Plan
Iran has rejected several Omani proposals for de-escalation in the Strait of Hormuz and placed its own nonnegotiable proposal on the table, according to the Javan Daily, which characterized the current posture as a combined war-and-negotiation strategy.
