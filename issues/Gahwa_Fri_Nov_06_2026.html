The Lead
​
Adipec's $67bn haul
Adipec wrapped with roughly $67 billion in agreements, a number that lands differently in a war year. The conference ran without the top executives of Exxon and Chevron. It was a quiet signal that American oil majors still read the Gulf as a risk zone rather than a home base.

Adnoc used the week to lock in a $1 billion green financing package from a Japanese-led lender group headed by JBIC. The package was drawn from a $3 billion credit line signed in 2024. The first loan of $905 million includes $543 million from JBIC, with Mizuho, MUFG and HSBC Tokyo covering the remainder.

Why the Japanese money matters Tokyo is not lending into a boom; it is lending into a vacuum left by Western banks that have grown cautious about Gulf war exposure. The remaining $1.9 billion of that credit line is still undrawn, which gives Adnoc a cushion for whatever the next quarter brings.

The Abu Dhabi Department of Energy also signed a framework with H2G Green, Muzn Energy and CloudEnergi to assess an LNG 'virtual pipeline' for industrial and commercial sites off the gas grid. That project alone could unlock up to AED 1 billion in investment. The Gulf's energy sector is not waiting for the war to end; it is building the financing structure for the next phase now.

02Al Reef leads
Abu Dhabi property outpaces Dubai
Abu Dhabi residential prices rose 16.8% year on year in the third quarter of 2026, per ValuStrat. Dubai's ready freehold capital values fell 4.5% over the same period. The two biggest property markets in the UAE are now moving in opposite directions.

Abu Dhabi's ValuStrat Price Index advanced 3.1% quarter on quarter to 155.8 points. Apartments rose 4.3% quarter on quarter and 24% year on year. Al Reef apartments led with a 36.6% annual jump.

Why the divergence matters Dubai's pipeline is roughly 350,000 homes by the early 2030s, about ten times Abu Dhabi's. Dubai's off-plan registrations fell for a fourth straight quarter, down 45.7% year on year. Reportedly, asking rents in Dubai fell 4.9% quarter on quarter and turned negative year on year for the first time since the pandemic. Investors reading the two maps should watch whether Abu Dhabi's tighter supply keeps prices firm while Dubai's delivery wave keeps rents soft.

03Fewer landlords to answer to
Emaar delists development unit at $3.3 billion
Emaar Properties' board approved a Dh12 billion ($3.3 billion) buyout to delist Emaar Development from the Dubai Financial Market. The offer of Dh15 per share is a 19.4% premium over the closing price of Dh12.56 on November 2.

Emaar Properties and its subsidiary EPHL already hold more than 3.2 billion of the 4 billion shares, roughly 81%. The remaining 793.5 million shares bring the total offer to more than Dh11.9 billion. Emaar Development reported third quarter profit of Dh3.5 billion, up nearly 8% year on year, on revenue of Dh8.8 billion, up 14%.

Why delist now The developer's revenue growth slowed to 2.7% at the parent level. Taking the unit private lets Emaar manage its cash and land bank without the quarterly glare of minority shareholders. Dubai's property market is still absorbing a 45.7% drop in off-plan registrations. At Dh15 per share, a 19.4% premium over the Dh12.56 close is what it costs to fold Dh3.5 billion in quarterly profit back into one balance sheet.

04Oil logistics
Oil shipping costs up 15x in nine months
Combined tanker freight and marine insurance for Middle East-to-East Asia oil climbed to $49.42 per barrel in October, per OPEC's November 2026 report. That was up from $3.24 in January. That is an additional $46.18 per barrel in nine months. It means freight and insurance now account for roughly 30% of total crude oil costs versus about 5% in January.

VLCC freight rates jumped from $3.18 per barrel in January to $37.13 in October, nearly 12 times higher. Marine insurance costs rose from $0.06 to $12.29 per barrel, more than 200 times the January level. OPEC says the current tanker fleet disruption explains only a small fraction of the increase.

Why the Gulf should care These costs are embedded in every barrel that leaves the region, and they are paid by the buyer. When the logistic cost of moving oil rises fifteenfold, the region's price advantage narrows against suppliers like the US and West Africa. That is a structural shift, not a wartime blip. Insurance costs rose from $0.06 to $12.29 per barrel, meaning the policy for moving oil now rivals the value of the cargo.

05AI consolidation
HUMAIN buys Saudi tech firm CNTXT
HUMAIN, a Saudi PIF portfolio company, acquired Riyadh-based AI and cloud company CNTXT, with financial terms undisclosed. CNTXT, founded in 2022, provides cloud transformation, data analytics, AI, cybersecurity and managed technology services.

The deal folds CNTXT's technology, workforce and enterprise customer relationships into HUMAIN's platform across AI infrastructure, cloud, models and enterprise applications. HUMAIN CEO Tareq Amin and CNTXT CEO Abdullah Jarwan will now run a combined operation with a deeper enterprise funnel in the Kingdom.

What it signals Saudi Arabia's AI strategy is consolidating around a few national champions instead of spreading across a dozen startups. Every enterprise customer CNTXT had is now a PIF portfolio tenant. The government's own cloud and AI demand is the anchor tenant behind the deal. Tareq Amin and Abdullah Jarwan now share a single enterprise funnel that was once two separate lists of customer relationships in Riyadh.

The AI Race
​
Data center politics hit home
The Nasdaq Composite fell 5.5% on Thursday, its worst single session since Liberation Day, after Democrats won control of Congress. The rout followed a wave of local votes against data centers: 40 of 41 ballot measures in the US rejected them, and six cities voted to ban them outright. Texas elected a governor who campaigned on a moratorium, and Texas holds more than a quarter of planned new US builds.

The numbers are the political part. When voters reject a data center, they are rejecting the water, power and jobs claims that came with it. OpenAI moved the same day with a National Community Compact, promising to pay for its own energy, publish job reports and avoid NDAs with officials. That is the shape of the next phase: operators will have to buy community consent before they pour concrete. Gulf builders who can offer in-country power and transparency will find themselves holding a template others need. Gulf sovereign funds and builders chasing AI infrastructure now face a US that is re-pricing data center risk, which makes the region's in-country power and capital an even stronger pitch.
Business
​
Emirates Group profit falls 66% as war costs bite
Emirates Group posted net income of AED 3.6 billion for the first half of its fiscal year, down 66% year on year. Revenue fell 12% to AED 66.2 billion. Seat capacity dropped 17%. The airline is carrying the cost of rerouting around a war that has made the region's airspace more expensive to cross.
Empower profit rises 3.1% on cheaper debt
Dubai's Empower reported net income of AED 292 million for the third quarter, up 3.1% year on year. Net finance costs fell 23.6% after the company refinanced AED 5.5 billion in revolving credit. The district cooling firm is still growing even as Dubai's property market softens. AED 292 million of net income now owes a modest nod to the refinancing desk.
Qatar reshuffles QatarEnergy board
Qatar's Amir Sheikh Tamim bin Hamad Al Thani reconstituted the QatarEnergy Board of Directors with a four-year term. Board membership is renewable for additional terms of the same duration. Board renewal now runs four years, matching the patience of North Field rather than a real estate cycle.
Journify raises $12 million at 4x valuation
US-headquartered adtech startup Journify raised $12 million in Series A funding led by Al Mada Ventures. The round completed at four times the previous valuation. Revenue grew more than fivefold since Silicon Badia's initial investment. Journify serves over 100 businesses, including Jarir Bookstore and Baytonia. Jarir Bookstore and Baytonia are now inside a startup priced at four times its prior round.
Mantle8 starts natural hydrogen hunt in RAK
France-based Mantle8 began a 24-month exploration programme in Block 7, Ras Al Khaimah, to survey for natural hydrogen. The project is among the first natural hydrogen exploration programmes in the GCC. Natural hydrogen, sometimes called white hydrogen, occurs in geological deposits rather than being made from gas or water. Mantle8 is looking for hydrogen that comes out of the ground already made.
