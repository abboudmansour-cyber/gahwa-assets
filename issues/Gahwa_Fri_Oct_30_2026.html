The Lead
​
Reportedly, Binance hack freezes $3 billion
Reportedly, a suspected security breach at Binance, the world's largest crypto exchange, has frozen roughly $3 billion in assets and halted all deposits and withdrawals. Binance estimates the total impact at approximately $6.3 billion, making it potentially the largest theft in crypto history, surpassing the $1.5 billion Bybit hack from February 2025. Reportedly, Tether and Circle froze more than $2.3 billion in stablecoins linked to the attack. TRM Labs linked the attack with high confidence to TraderTraitor, a North Korean hacking group. An initial investigation suggests a compromised DevOps engineer's laptop allowed the attacker to access a service container and submit unauthorized withdrawal transactions. Binance Co-CEO Yi He said the exchange would cover all losses and that user funds remain completely safe.

Bitcoin dropped 4.5% in 24 hours to $76,664 before recovering to around $77,984, down 2.2%. Ether fell 5.2% to $2,190, while BNB slid 7.1% to $698.65, and more than $1.5 billion of leveraged positions were liquidated. Rick Maeda of Laevitas noted that BNB's fall was 'quite modest for possibly the largest exchange theft ever,' citing Binance's $144 billion in assets under custody, a characterization of the theft attributed to Laevitas, reportedly. What to watch next Institutional investors are pulling funds from exchanges, with withdrawals suggesting the fallout is spreading beyond Binance itself. Standard Chartered expects crypto prices to remain under pressure before finding a base. For Gulf operators moving money in and out of crypto, the next few days will reveal whether trust in exchanges, not just Binance, has taken permanent damage.

02SME lending rebound
UAE SMEs are borrowing again
Beehive, a UAE SME lender, expects around AED 150 million in loan approvals in October, its best month on record and beating February's pre-conflict peak. CEO Peter Tavener expects monthly approvals to pass AED 200 million by the second quarter of 2027. The lender has lent close to AED 6 billion since launch and holds a loan book of about AED 1.2 billion.

The rebound follows a punishing year in which shipping a container from China rose from $1,200 before the conflict to about $10,000, and delivery times doubled from 60 to 120 days. Beehive's early-warning metric, loans 5 to 30 days overdue, spiked to about 6% in June and July before falling back to the pre-conflict level of 2.5%.

A Central Bank of the UAE resilience package in March eased capital rules and allowed payment deferrals, with about AED 13.5 billion in repayments deferred for more than 135,000 customers. Deferred bank loans started coming due in September and October, and Bloomberg Intelligence expects any stress to show up six to twelve months later. A container that cost $1,200 before the conflict now costs about $10,000, so an SME borrowing at Beehive is not being reckless, just restocking.

03Hormuz tension spike
Hormuz tankers burn, Riyadh targeted
Iran's IRGC Navy claimed three oil tankers exploded and caught fire after striking naval mines while leaving the Strait of Hormuz via an unauthorised southern route. The claim was not independently verified. The Saudi-led coalition downed two ballistic missiles launched by Houthis towards Riyadh, with early warnings issued for Riyadh, Al Kharj and Najran, according to the coalition. The WHO reported Yemen's casualty toll reached 9,072 by October 25, including 1,677 deaths and 7,395 injuries.

US envoy Tom Barrack credited Qatar and Pakistan with helping bring US and Iran closer to renewed diplomacy, saying sides are very close to a de-escalation understanding. Iran's parliament speaker said Tehran's seven conditions must be agreed before progress. Reportedly, for Gulf operators, the immediate risk sits in shipping insurance and freight costs, not in a full closure of the strait.

The UAE's first CEPA with a G7 country, with Canada, is expected to add $8.7 billion to GDP by 2033, lifting exports to Canada by about $11.8 billion. The response to the Adipec cancellations will show whether Gulf business travel is now priced for conflict risk.

05Wafer thin gap
Gulf states cement AI corridor with Europe
The UAE and Oman are deepening their AI infrastructure ties with Europe, with Abu Dhabi's MGX and France's Mistral AI planning to expand Campus AI to as much as 3 gigawatts across France. The UAE also announced a EUR 40 billion investment package in Germany, including about 1 GW of new data center capacity.

Oman signed an agreement for UK-founded Yamna to develop an AI data center in Salalah, with more than $1.3 billion planned for the first phase. GlobalFoundries, majority-owned by Abu Dhabi's Mubadala, announced a EUR 1.1 billion expansion in Dresden to produce over 1 million wafers annually by end of 2028.

ECB President Christine Lagarde estimated closing Europe's data center capacity gap could cost up to EUR 600 billion over the next decade. KAPSARC found total ownership costs for Saudi data center operators can be up to 30% below comparable international markets, giving Gulf players a structural advantage in AI infrastructure. The Gulf's AI bet is now as much about owning the buildings as the brains, and the returns will accrue to those who can build fast and cheap.

The AI Race
​
AWS to $1 trillion, Jassy says
Amazon CEO Andy Jassy says AWS will become a $1 trillion revenue business, but the path there requires continued capital expenditure. Amazon burned $9.1 billion in free cash flow in Q3 as AI spending rose to $51.3 billion. AWS grew 39 percent in Q3, its fastest since 2021, yet Amazon shares fell 6 percent after missing estimates.

The market is now punishing tech giants for spending ahead of revenue, even as the cloud units themselves post record growth. Alphabet saw search revenue growth slow to 12 percent, and shares fell 13 percent despite cloud doubling to $30.7 billion. Gulf AI investors watching hyperscalers must now ask whether the capex cycle will produce returns before the market loses patience. The Gulf's AI buildout is racing the same market impatience, and the next few months will show whether regional capital keeps pouring into compute while global tech reprices.
