Abu Dhabi's Murban crude now fetches more than $30 per barrel over benchmark Dubai crude for delivery to East Asia.

The premium means Murban is at least $10 per barrel more expensive than shipping US WTI crude to the region. Dubai futures have climbed to nearly $100 per barrel, their highest since May.

Indian and Chinese buying is driving the surge. The premium reflects how buyers are scrambling for Gulf crude that does not depend on the Strait of Hormuz. UAE halted all trade, commercial exchange, and financial transactions with Iran days before Washington's sanctions push went public. Dubai did about $28 billion in trade with Iran in 2024.

Jebel Ali container volumes plunged more than 90% at the worst of the war. Hormuz transits are averaging 17 vessels a day against roughly 135 before the conflict. For Gulf operators, the premium is a signal of how much buyers will pay for routes that bypass the strait entirely.

02Riyadh's robotaxi bet
10,000 robotaxis by 2030
WeRide plans to put between 7,000 and 10,000 autonomous taxis on Saudi roads by the end of the decade, with the first 100 driving in Riyadh before this year is out.

The Chinese company already runs robotaxis in the capital through a partnership with Uber and local firm Ai Driver. It will extend its operating area into the central Olaya district, then launch a separate project in Qiddiya next year. It also has autonomous buses operating in AlUla.

Saudi Arabia has set a target of making 15% of public transport vehicles autonomous by 2030. Since August 2024 the country has been installing smart communication devices along roadways that interact with autonomous vehicles.

The desert has already forced one adjustment. WeRide's Robosweeper street-cleaning vehicles had to be recalled for upgrades because dust and sand in the GCC interfered with their operation. Summer heat is also making walking to pick-up points a problem for customers. The race is on to prove that a technology built for temperate cities can survive Gulf heat and sand without constant hand-holding.

03Abu Dhabi property boom
Abu Dhabi property tops 2025 total
Abu Dhabi property transactions reached AED 155 billion year-to-date, surpassing the AED 142 billion recorded during all of 2025.

Foreign direct investment into Abu Dhabi real estate spiked 309% year-on-year to AED 13.8 billion in the first half. Sales values reached AED 107.3 billion, up from AED 99.4 billion a year earlier. Mortgage values dipped slightly to AED 42.3 billion from AED 42.7 billion.

The surge is a direct result of capital seeking a stable Gulf base while regional conflict disrupts other markets. Abu Dhabi is drawing both end-users and investors who want exposure to a market insulated from Hormuz risk. The numbers suggest Abu Dhabi is becoming the default safe haven for property capital in the Gulf.

04UAE PMI jumps
UAE growth accelerates, hiring falls
The UAE's non-oil private sector grew at its fastest pace since December 2024 in August, with the S&P Global PMI climbing to 55.3 from 52.7 in July.

New business grew at the joint-fastest pace in more than two years. Export orders posted a second consecutive monthly expansion. Dubai's PMI rose to 54.1 from 51.7.

But employment fell for the second time in three months, the first stretch of multiple monthly declines in a single calendar year since 2021. Firms are facing higher costs for energy, fuel, cement, steel, and chemicals. Many are switching to domestic suppliers to hedge supply chains amid regional conflict. The split between rising activity and falling employment shows Gulf firms are getting more output from fewer people.

05Oxagon data centre
100MW AI data centre at NEOM
HUMAIN, a PIF firm, and DataVolt will develop a 100MW AI-ready data centre at Oxagon in NEOM, the first phase of a planned 360MW facility.

Construction is now under way, with the first 100MW expected to be available in 2028. The site is part of DataVolt's wider 1.5GW Oxagon campus.

The facility will integrate renewable energy sources, innovative cooling technologies, and high-performance computing clusters. It is a direct bet on making Saudi Arabia a physical home for AI compute rather than just a buyer of it. The 100MW first phase is a test of whether NEOM can deliver power and cooling at a scale AI training actually requires.

Dubai Islamic closes debut $750m syndicated facility
Dubai Islamic, the UAE’s largest Islamic bank, closed its first syndicated Islamic financing at $750 million. The facility drew about $1.2 billion in commitments, led by HSBC, Mizuho and Standard Chartered. The oversubscription shows lenders still have appetite for strong Gulf bank credit.
Nayla lands $18m for Saudi micro-business lending
Nayla secured roughly $18 million in pre-Series A equity and debt financing. Idrisi Ventures led the equity portion, and BLOMINVEST led the debt facility. The Sanabil Studio startup uses AI to underwrite loans for Saudi micro-businesses. It is the first venture from the studio to close an institutional round.
Oman oil windfall cuts H1 deficit to $44m
Oman’s H1 2026 fiscal deficit fell to OMR17 million from OMR259 million a year earlier. Average Omani crude rose nearly 10% to $81 per barrel. Oil still accounts for about 70% of government revenue. The windfall narrowed the deficit, but subsidies and defence spending are climbing.
Arada and Syrian fund form $7bn New Damascus JV
Arada entered Syria through a joint venture with the state-owned Syrian Sovereign Fund. The first project is a 4 million square metre city west of Damascus with an estimated development value of $7 billion. It will deliver 11,000 homes, a hospital, schools and a large public park.
Sirdab raises $10m Series A to expand GCC warehouses
Sirdab raised $10 million in Series A funding co-led by Elm and BECO Capital. The logistics platform spans over 120 warehouses and 60 transportation providers, serving more than 850 businesses. It has grown revenue 20x since Y Combinator.

It gives Oman a venue capable of hosting the kind of events that have skipped the capital. Cultural infrastructure is becoming a soft power tool for Gulf states beyond the usual big cities.
A first BYD ship calls at Duqm

The call hints Duqm wants a bigger role in Chinese vehicle exports to the Gulf. A small port call that points to a bigger shift in regional auto logistics.

The Last Sip

Which Gulf city has more registered cats than cars?
The answer
Kuwait City is often cited as having a huge stray cat population, far larger than the number of registered vehicles. The city has tried sterilisation and feeding programmes, but the cats keep outnumbering the cars. It is a fact that sounds wrong and is not. In Kuwait, even the traffic jams have fur.
What is the world’s busiest international airport?
The answer
Dubai International has held the title for international passengers for years, beating London Heathrow by a wide margin. It keeps the crown because it is a hub between Europe, Asia and Africa. The airport is a city in itself, with its own hotels and a five-storey indoor waterfall. Some passengers land just to see the waterfall.
