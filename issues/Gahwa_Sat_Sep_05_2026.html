The Lead
​
The US just approved a $5 billion bomb sale to Saudi Arabia, according to filings, while warning it may strike Iran's deepest nuclear bunker.
The package is designed to improve the Kingdom's airborne defense capability and its ability to operate alongside American forces. It comes after months of Iranian drone and missile strikes on Saudi territory, according to reports, and the sale now goes to Congress for review, where lawmakers have a limited window to object. Saudi Arabia has been hit repeatedly by Houthi and Iranian attacks since the war began in February, according to officials. The new munitions would replenish stocks depleted by continuous air defense operations.

A $5b deal signals Washington's commitment to Saudi security even as it escalates direct military action against Iran. The sale reinforces Riyadh's role as a frontline US partner. It also puts pressure on Gulf states to maintain their own defense spending while Washington carries the heavier load of striking Iran directly.

This is the largest US weapons package to Saudi Arabia this year. It coincides with US Central Command strikes, which officials say targeted Iranian air defenses, radar, and maritime assets across the Gulf. The dual track arms America's Gulf allies while targeting their shared adversary. The deal confirms Washington's bet: armed Gulf partners are more effective than deploying more American troops.

02Live shopping
Rwaj raises $1.2m for live shopping
Saudi live shopping startup Rwaj raised $1.2 million in a pre-seed round. Founded in 2024 by Taher Alblowe, the platform operates a live social shopping marketplace with fixed prices and real-time auctions.

Payments are held in escrow until buyers confirm delivery. Shipping is handled through integrated carriers. Buyers can pay using Mada, Apple Pay, or cards.

The funding will accelerate product development and support regional expansion. Live shopping has exploded in China but remains nascent in the Gulf. Rwaj is betting the model translates to Saudi consumer habits. Rwaj's escrow payments and live auctions are an early test of whether China's livestream commerce model fits Gulf consumers.

03Saudi AI agents
Muzn launches AI fraud agents
Saudi AI startup Muzn unleashed AI agents on banking, insurance, and public sector fraud prevention and event anticipation. The platform supports models from Anthropic, Grok, and OpenAI.

Muzn's agents integrate with ERP and CRM systems, including MSEGAT and Authentica. They are built for Saudi enterprises that need AI automation with specific controls and permissions.

The launch comes as Saudi Arabia pushes AI adoption across government and financial services. Muzn is betting that localized, compliant AI agents will win against generic US and Chinese tools. Muzn's focus on fraud and compliance is a signal that Saudi AI adoption will be driven by risk management, not just efficiency.

04Saudi fintech M&A
Salla buys Saudi payments firm Paylink
Saudi e-commerce platform Salla acquired Saudi fintech Paylink. Financial terms were not disclosed. Paylink was founded in 2017 and is licensed by the Saudi Central Bank for e-commerce payment services.

The acquisition will integrate Paylink's payments infrastructure with Salla's commerce ecosystem. Salla plans to expand its point-of-sale payment offering, including SoftPOS technologies. Paylink's infrastructure supports international payments, multiple currencies, and different payment methods.

The deal turns Salla from an e-commerce platform into a payments player. It follows a wave of Saudi fintech consolidation as SAMA pushes toward a cashless economy. For SMEs, it means an integrated commerce and payments stack without patching together third-party providers. Salla buying Paylink is a signal that Saudi e-commerce platforms want to own the payments layer, not rent it.

05Saudi auto tech
Syarah raises $12m from Impact46
Saudi autotech startup Syarah raised $12 million in a new funding round from Impact46. Founded in 2015, Syarah operates an online marketplace for new and used cars. It serves customers across all regions of Saudi Arabia.

The investment will support nationwide expansion and continued development of digital platform and delivery operations. In 2024, Syarah closed a $60 million Series C led by Artal Capital with participation from Elm, Impact46, Tawuniya, and Derayah Ventures.

The new round adds to a growing stack of Saudi startups chasing the consumer transition to online car buying. Syarah has handled thousands of purchases. The fresh capital suggests investors still see room to grow before the market consolidates. Syarah's ability to raise another round signals that Saudi online car sales still have room to scale.

The AI Race
​
Nvidia buys Hugging Face for $12.9 billion
Nvidia agreed to acquire Hugging Face for $12.9 billion. The platform hosts 3 million models and serves over 18 million developers. Hugging Face was valued at $4.5 billion after a 2023 funding round. The deal is expected to close in the first half of 2027.

This puts Nvidia in control of the largest open source AI model repository on earth. AI companies, researchers and hobbyists use Hugging Face daily to download and share models. Nvidia sells the chips those models run on. Owning the platform gives Nvidia direct sight of which models are growing fastest, and which customers are building them. Gulf developers and universities download heavily from Hugging Face; the deal puts their open-source AI habit inside Nvidia's commercial orbit.

Off Paper

Layn prints buildings in Cairo, eyes the Gulf
Egypt's first 3D concrete printing company, Layn, runs five machines 24 hours a day, six days a week, consuming 15 tons of concrete daily. It grew from two people to 70 and counts Sodic, Mountain View and Emaar among clients. Founder Zeyad Zein plans to expand into Saudi Arabia and the UAE. A bootstrapped Egyptian team built the hardware, software and concrete mix itself, and wants to print buildings in the Gulf.
Sumo comes to Abu Dhabi
The UAE hosts its first international professional sumo competition on September 26 at Space42 Arena. The event is part of the Department of Culture and Tourism's push to bring global cultural events to the capital. Japan's heaviest sport debuts in the desert. A small, strange bet that Abu Dhabi's cultural ambitions are more nimble than its rivals.
Homegrown cafes export their names
Four Emirati cafe brands have gone international. They moved from local favourites to regional and international franchises. Dubai has always imported food concepts, so exporting its own cafes is a shift. The Gulf is beginning to sell its own culture rather than simply buying everyone else's.
The Last Sip
​
Which Gulf city has more registered cats than cars?
The answer
No Gulf city actually keeps a register of cats, but Kuwait City and Doha both have famously large stray cat populations, fed by residents daily. The number is impossible to know precisely, but the cats are everywhere. They sit in parking lots, outside cafes and on car bonnets. The cats of Kuwait City have never been to a vet, but they have never once been late for dinner.
What is the second busiest airport for international passengers after Dubai?
The answer
Heathrow in London holds the second spot for international passengers. Dubai has led the world for years, but Heathrow has been closing the gap. The race between them is watched closely by airlines and duty-free operators. The second busiest airport is still not in the Gulf, but Dubai is comfortable with that.
