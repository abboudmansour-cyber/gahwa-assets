The Lead
​
Saudi Arabia's East-West oil pipeline went dark after a drone strike from Iraq, according to reports, and the Houthis are now 50 km from the Bab Al Mandab chokepoint.
The twin threats are squeezing the kingdom's main export routes at a moment when its supply is already at a multi-decade low.

The IEA put August Saudi supply at 6 million barrels per day, down 2.3 million from July and the lowest in over 30 years. The agency also cut its 2026 forecast for Saudi crude by 885,000 barrels per day to 7.6 million. Crude loadings fell 1.1 million barrels per day in August to 3.5 million.

Houthi strikes on Abha, Khamis Mushait and Jazan injured 73 civilians, according to official reports. According to reports, Saudi-backed forces answered with 54 airstrikes across six Yemen provinces. Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman asked Washington for direct military support against the Houthis. The East-West pipeline had been the main route out for Middle East oil for the past six months, with the Strait of Hormuz largely shut by war, according to reports. Losing both corridors at once leaves Saudi exports with no easy path to global markets. A Gulf operator that depends on stable crude flows should map alternative logistics now, because the pressure on this chokepoint is only building. The kingdom's export options are now measured in days, not months, and the Houthi advance toward Bab Al Mandab means the southern route is no longer a guarantee.

02PIF gaming play
PIF weighs EA-Savvy gaming merger
PIF is weighing a merger of Electronic Arts and Savvy Games Group that would combine Madden NFL, The Sims, Pokémon Go and Monopoly Go under one roof, according to people familiar with the talks. The deal would create a single gaming entity controlled by the Saudi sovereign fund, consolidating its scattered bets in the sector.

Any merger must wait until Savvy closes its $6 billion acquisition of China's Moonton. Savvy's CEO Brian Ward departed this month, and the handover went to PIF deputy governor Turqi Alnowaiser, who fronted the EA negotiations. The leadership shuffle adds timing uncertainty to a deal that would already be the fund's biggest gaming move yet.

The consolidation answer the question of how PIF plans to justify years of gaming investments that have produced little public payoff. For Gulf operators and investors, the signal is that the fund is shifting from buying franchises to building an integrated publisher. If the merger closes, it would, according to people familiar with the matter, rank among the largest gaming deals ever, and the asset would land entirely outside the US-listed market. The fund is no longer just writing checks into gaming; it is assembling a publisher, and that changes how every future Saudi sports and entertainment investment should be read.

03Bitcoin resistance
Bitcoin faces $81,700 test
Bitcoin needs to clear resistance at $81,700 to confirm a new bull market, according to CryptoQuant. The resistance range stretches to $88,700, and the outlook remains bullish, but the level is acting as the line between a breakout and another rangebound stretch.

The call matters because the live market is still trading around $77,212, below the threshold that would trigger fresh momentum buying. CryptoQuant's framing suggests the rally has not yet proven itself. For Gulf crypto operators and investors, the technical level is a useful discipline against the noise.

The regional angle is liquidity: a Bitcoin move above $81,700 would likely pull fresh capital into Gulf exchanges and tokenized asset plays. Until then, the market is waiting on a number, and for once the number is doing the talking instead of the influencers. Every Gulf crypto desk should have $81,700 on a sticker, because that is the number that decides whether the current rally is real or just the same old range in a louder shirt.

04Revolut data exposure
Revolut data exposed via fake government request
Revolut says customer KYC and Bitcoin transaction data was exposed after a fake request from a government domain. The breach appears to have targeted specific user records rather than the entire customer base.

Onchain investigator ZachXBT speculated the incident may have targeted high-net-worth users, which would make it a precision social-engineering attack rather than a broad compromise. Revolut has not said how many accounts were affected or which jurisdiction the fake request came from.

For Gulf fintech operators and regulators, the lesson is structural: identity verification processes that trust a government domain without a second channel are a single point of failure. A bank or exchange built on that design is one convincing domain spoof away from a headline. The fix is cheaper than the breach. The fake government domain worked, which means the next breach is already being drafted somewhere, and the GCC's young fintechs should read this one as a free security audit of their own KYC handoffs.

Dorra gas field
Bids go in for Dorra gas field
Al-Khafji Joint Operations plans to appoint a consultant to oversee EPC works on the multibillion-dollar offshore Dorra gas field project. The bid submission closed on 8 September, moving the long-stalled project closer to execution.

The Dorra field straddles the maritime boundary between Kuwait and Saudi Arabia, and it has been held up by disputes with Iran over the field's eastern edge. Progress on the consultant contract signals that the two Gulf states are now moving ahead on their side.

For Gulf operators, the Dorra project is a test case for how quickly shared energy infrastructure can get built when both governments decide the timing is right. The consultant award will set the pace for a project that could add significant non-associated gas to two Gulf economies. A consultant on Dorra is the first real signal that the project is moving from map to calendar, and the contractors who missed the bid are already a month late.

The AI Race
​
Anthropic IPO hits a safety wall
Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei called on AI companies to slow the pace of developing powerful models, citing risks from misuse. The warning landed just as Nvidia is in discussions to become an anchor investor in Anthropic's proposed IPO, potentially committing as much as $10 billion. Anthropic published research on September 10 showing frontier models are increasingly capable in military and intelligence tasks, including intelligence targeting and conventional weapons development.

The company is seeking up to $100 billion in its offering, which could value it at about $2 trillion and become the largest IPO in history, reportedly expected before the US midterm elections in November. OpenAI has also filed confidentially for an IPO but will not go public this year, according to CEO Sam Altman. The two leading labs are now publicly agreeing that the safety conversation has moved from the lab to the boardroom. For Gulf investors who have been chasing AI exposure, the caution from inside the leading labs is a signal to price risk higher.

Sabic Agri-Nutrients awarded a $3.5 billion contract to Samsung E&A for a new urea complex. The award is part of Saudi Arabia's push to expand downstream chemicals capacity. The complex is a big piece of industrial self-sufficiency in a kingdom that wants to sell more than crude. Samsung E&A has become the default partner for Saudi mega-projects.
EGA completes 80% acquisition of Italy's Eco Green
Emirates Global Aluminium completed its acquisition of an 80% stake in Eco Green, an Italian aluminium recycler. Eco Green operates two plants in northeast Italy handling over 70,000 tonnes a year. EGA is buying into recycled metal as a premium product line, not a niche. The deal gives the UAE producer a foothold in European circular manufacturing.
Heisco wins $359m Kuwait oil storage contract
Heisco won a $359 million contract for developing storage tanks for Jurassic Light Oil export facilities in Kuwait. The award covers export infrastructure work. Kuwait is investing in storage capacity as a hedge against shipping disruption. The contract is a small piece of a bigger buildout in the northern Gulf.

The Last Sip

Which Gulf country runs a formal gig economy inside its government procurement?
The answer
Oman, and it just made it official. The Ministry of Finance issued a circular allowing government units to award contracts worth up to RO 5,000 directly to freelancers. The threshold is small, but no other Gulf state has put independent workers inside its public procurement system this way. The RO 5,000 is the door opening, not the room.
Which Gulf city is getting the iPhone Duo before most of the world?
The answer
Dubai, and it lands on October 23. Pre-orders open October 16, with prices starting at AED 8,499. The UAE is in the first wave of markets, which is no accident given how many iPhones this city buys per square metre. Apple priced it like a small apartment and set it down on Sheikh Zayed Road.
